@topic Operations on matrices
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and $A^2 + 7I = kA$, then the value of $k$ is
(A) $1$
(B) $2$
(C) $5$
(D) $7$
--- ans C
$$A^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 9 - 1 & 3 + 2 \\ -3 - 2 & -1 + 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 5 \\ -5 & 3 \end{bmatrix},\qquad A^2 + 7I = \begin{bmatrix} 15 & 5 \\ -5 & 10 \end{bmatrix} = 5\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = 5A$$
$\therefore k = 5$.

@topic Adjoint and inverse
Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & -3 \\ 3 & -2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \dfrac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix} -2 & 0 & 1 \\ 9 & 2 & -3 \\ 6 & 1 & \lambda \end{bmatrix}$. If $AB = I$, then the value of $\lambda$ is
(A) $-\dfrac{9}{4}$
(B) $-2$
(C) $-\dfrac{3}{2}$
(D) $0$
--- ans B
$AB = I$ means $B = A^{-1}$. $|A| = 1(8 - 6) + 1(0 + 9) + 2(0 - 6) = -1$ and
$$\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & -1 \\ -9 & -2 & 3 \\ -6 & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \Rightarrow A^{-1} = -\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 0 & 1 \\ 9 & 2 & -3 \\ 6 & 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$
Comparing, $\lambda = -2$. (Equivalently, the $(1, 3)$ entry of $AB$ must be $0$: $1 + 3 + 2\lambda = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda = -2$.) The factor $\frac{1}{3}$ printed before B is inconsistent with $AB = I$ (it would give $AB = \frac{1}{3}I$); without it, $\lambda = -2$.

=== 7 | cd | 1 | mcq
@topic Differentiability
The function $f(x) = |x| + |x - 2|$ is
(A) continuous, but not differentiable at $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.
(B) differentiable but not continuous at $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.
(C) continuous but not differentiable at $x = 0$ only.
(D) neither continuous nor differentiable at $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.
--- ans A
$|x|$ and $|x - 2|$ are continuous everywhere, so $f$ is continuous.
$f(x) = \begin{cases} 2 - 2x, & x < 0 \\ 2, & 0 \le x \le 2 \\ 2x - 2, & x > 2 \end{cases}$: at $x = 0$ LHD $= -2 \ne$ RHD $= 0$; at $x = 2$ LHD $= 0 \ne$ RHD $= 2$.
So $f$ is continuous but not differentiable at $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.

=== 23 | aod | 2 | sub
@topic Increasing and decreasing functions
Show that the function $f$ given by $f(x) = \sin x + \cos x$, is strictly decreasing in the interval $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}, \dfrac{5\pi}{4}\right)$.
--- ans
$$f'(x) = \cos x - \sin x = \sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cos x - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin x\right) = \sqrt{2}\cos\left(x + \frac{\pi}{4}\right)$$
For $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$: $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < x + \dfrac{\pi}{4} < \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, where cosine is negative. So $f'(x) < 0$ throughout the interval.
Hence $f$ is strictly decreasing in $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}, \dfrac{5\pi}{4}\right)$.

=== 25 | int | 2 | sub
@topic Partial fractions
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{2x}{(x^2 + 1)(x^2 - 4)}\,dx$.
--- ans
Put $x^2 = t$, $2x\,dx = dt$:
$$I = \int \frac{dt}{(t + 1)(t - 4)}$$
$\dfrac{1}{(t + 1)(t - 4)} = \dfrac{A}{t + 1} + \dfrac{B}{t - 4}$: $1 = A(t - 4) + B(t + 1)$; $t = 4$: $B = \dfrac{1}{5}$; $t = -1$: $A = -\dfrac{1}{5}$.
$$I = \frac{1}{5}\int\left(\frac{1}{t - 4} - \frac{1}{t + 1}\right)dt = \frac{1}{5}\log\left|\frac{t - 4}{t + 1}\right| + C = \frac{1}{5}\log\left|\frac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 + 1}\right| + C$$

=== 26 | cd | 3 | sub
@topic Logarithmic differentiation
Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, if $y = (\cos x)^x + \cos^{-1}\sqrt{x}$ is given.
--- ans
Let $u = (\cos x)^x$, $v = \cos^{-1}\sqrt{x}$; $y = u + v$.
$\log u = x\log\cos x \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{u}\dfrac{du}{dx} = \log\cos x + x\cdot\dfrac{-\sin x}{\cos x}$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{du}{dx} = (\cos x)^x\left(\log\cos x - x\tan x\right)$$
$$\frac{dv}{dx} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} = -\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x - x^2}}$$
$$\therefore \frac{dy}{dx} = (\cos x)^x\left(\log\cos x - x\tan x\right) - \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x - x^2}}$$

=== 28 | int | 3 | sub
@topic Integration by parts
Find: $\displaystyle\int \sec^3\theta\,d\theta$
--- ans
Let $I = \displaystyle\int \sec\theta\cdot\sec^2\theta\,d\theta$. By parts (first function $\sec\theta$):
$$I = \sec\theta\tan\theta - \int \sec\theta\tan\theta\cdot\tan\theta\,d\theta = \sec\theta\tan\theta - \int \sec\theta(\sec^2\theta - 1)\,d\theta$$
$$I = \sec\theta\tan\theta - I + \int \sec\theta\,d\theta = \sec\theta\tan\theta - I + \log|\sec\theta + \tan\theta|$$
$$2I = \sec\theta\tan\theta + \log|\sec\theta + \tan\theta| \Rightarrow I = \frac{1}{2}\sec\theta\tan\theta + \frac{1}{2}\log|\sec\theta + \tan\theta| + C$$

@topic Systems of linear equations
Find $A^{-1}$, if $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$. Hence, solve the following system of equations:
$x + 2y + z = 5$
$2x + 3y = 1$
$x - y + z = 8$
--- ans
$|A| = 1(3 - 0) - 2(2 + 1) + 1(0 - 3) = 3 - 6 - 3 = -6 \ne 0$.
Cofactors:
$C_{11} = 3,\ C_{12} = -3,\ C_{13} = -3$
$C_{21} = -2,\ C_{22} = 0,\ C_{23} = 2$
$C_{31} = -5,\ C_{32} = 3,\ C_{33} = -1$
$$\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -2 & -5 \\ -3 & 0 & 3 \\ -3 & 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix},\qquad A^{-1} = -\frac{1}{6}\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -2 & -5 \\ -3 & 0 & 3 \\ -3 & 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$$
The coefficient matrix of the given system is $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = A'$, so the system is $A'X = B$ with $B = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 1 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix}$ and
$$X = (A')^{-1}B = (A^{-1})'B = -\frac{1}{6}\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -3 & -3 \\ -2 & 0 & 2 \\ -5 & 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 1 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{6}\begin{bmatrix} 15 - 3 - 24 \\ -10 + 0 + 16 \\ -25 + 3 - 8 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{6}\begin{bmatrix} -12 \\ 6 \\ -30 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\therefore x = 2,\quad y = -1,\quad z = 5$$
(Check: $2 - 2 + 5 = 5$, $4 - 3 = 1$, $2 + 1 + 5 = 8$.)
