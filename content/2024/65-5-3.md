@topic Adjoint and inverse
For the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 1 \\ \lambda & 2 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$ to be invertible, the value of $\lambda$ is :
(A) $0$
(B) $10$
(C) $\mathbb{R} - \{10\}$
(D) $\mathbb{R} - \{-10\}$
--- ans D
$A$ is invertible iff $|A| \ne 0$.
$$|A| = 2(6 - 0) - (-1)(3\lambda - 0) + 1(-2\lambda - 2) = 12 + 3\lambda - 2\lambda - 2 = \lambda + 10$$
$|A| \ne 0 \Rightarrow \lambda \ne -10$, i.e. $\lambda \in \mathbb{R} - \{-10\}$.

=== 5 | mat | 1 | mcq
@topic Operations on matrices
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} x & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, then value of $x$ for which $A^2 = B$ is :
(A) $-2$
(B) $2$
(C) $2$ or $-2$
(D) $4$
--- ans A
$$A^2 = \begin{bmatrix} x & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x^2 & 0 \\ x + 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
$A^2 = B \Rightarrow x^2 = 4$ and $x + 1 = -1$. The second gives $x = -2$, which also satisfies $x^2 = 4$. So $x = -2$ only.

@topic Evaluation of determinants
Let $f(x) = \begin{vmatrix} x^2 & \sin x \\ p & -1 \end{vmatrix}$, where p is a constant. The value of p for which $f'(0) = 1$ is :
(A) $\mathbb{R}$
(B) $1$
(C) $0$
(D) $-1$
--- ans D
$f(x) = -x^2 - p\sin x \Rightarrow f'(x) = -2x - p\cos x$.
$f'(0) = -p = 1 \Rightarrow p = -1$.

@topic Properties of definite integrals
If $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{3} x^2\,dx = k\int_0^2 x^2\,dx + \int_2^3 x^2\,dx$, then the value of k is :
(A) $2$
(B) $1$
(C) $0$
(D) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
--- ans A
$$\int_{-2}^{3} x^2\,dx = \int_{-2}^{0} x^2\,dx + \int_0^2 x^2\,dx + \int_2^3 x^2\,dx$$
Since $x^2$ is even, $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{0} x^2\,dx = \int_0^2 x^2\,dx$. Hence the RHS is $2\displaystyle\int_0^2 x^2\,dx + \int_2^3 x^2\,dx$, so $k = 2$.

=== 12 | int | 1 | mcq
@topic Integration by parts
The value of $\displaystyle\int_1^e \log x\,dx$ is :
(A) $0$
(B) $1$
(C) $e$
(D) $e\log e$
--- ans B
By parts, $\displaystyle\int \log x\,dx = x\log x - x$.
$$\int_1^e \log x\,dx = \left[x\log x - x\right]_1^e = (e - e) - (0 - 1) = 1$$

@topic Area under a curve
The area bounded by the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$, Y-axis and between the lines $y = 0$ and $y = 3$ is :
(A) $2\sqrt{3}$
(B) $27$
(C) $9$
(D) $3$
--- ans C
$y = \sqrt{x} \Rightarrow x = y^2$ ($y \ge 0$). The area between the curve and the Y-axis is
$$\int_0^3 x\,dy = \int_0^3 y^2\,dy = \left[\frac{y^3}{3}\right]_0^3 = 9\ \text{sq. units}$$

=== 21 | itf | 2 | sub
@topic Simplification
Simplify : $\cos^{-1}x + \cos^{-1}\left[\dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3 - 3x^2}}{2}\right]$; $\dfrac{1}{2} \le x \le 1$
--- ans
Put $x = \cos\theta$. Since $\dfrac{1}{2} \le x \le 1$, $0 \le \theta \le \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so $\sin\theta \ge 0$ and $\sqrt{3 - 3x^2} = \sqrt{3}\sin\theta$.
$$\frac{x}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3 - 3x^2}}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\cos\theta + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin\theta = \cos\frac{\pi}{3}\cos\theta + \sin\frac{\pi}{3}\sin\theta = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \theta\right)$$
As $0 \le \dfrac{\pi}{3} - \theta \le \dfrac{\pi}{3} \subset [0, \pi]$, $\cos^{-1}\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \theta\right) = \dfrac{\pi}{3} - \theta$.
$$\therefore \cos^{-1}x + \cos^{-1}\left[\frac{x}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3 - 3x^2}}{2}\right] = \theta + \frac{\pi}{3} - \theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$$

=== 23 | aod | 2 | sub
@topic Rate of change
The surface area of a cube increases at the rate of $72\ \text{cm}^2/\text{sec}$. Find the rate of change of its volume, when the edge of the cube measures 3 cm.
--- ans
Let the edge be $x$ cm. Surface area $S = 6x^2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dS}{dt} = 12x\dfrac{dx}{dt}$.
At $x = 3$: $72 = 12(3)\dfrac{dx}{dt} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dx}{dt} = 2$ cm/sec.
Volume $V = x^3 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dV}{dt} = 3x^2\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 3(9)(2) = 54$.
The volume is increasing at the rate of **$54\ \text{cm}^3/\text{sec}$**.

=== 27 | cd | 3 | sub
@topic Second order derivatives
If $y = (\tan^{-1}x)^2$, show that $(x^2 + 1)^2\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 2x(x^2 + 1)\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2$.
--- ans
$$y = (\tan^{-1}x)^2 \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = 2\tan^{-1}x\cdot\frac{1}{1 + x^2}$$
$$\Rightarrow (1 + x^2)\frac{dy}{dx} = 2\tan^{-1}x$$
Differentiating again w.r.t. $x$:
$$(1 + x^2)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 2x\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2}{1 + x^2}$$
Multiplying by $(1 + x^2)$:
$$(x^2 + 1)^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 2x(x^2 + 1)\frac{dy}{dx} = 2$$
Hence proved.

@topic Scalar product and projection
Find the projection of vector $(\vec{b} + \vec{c})$ on vector $\vec{a}$, where $\vec{a} = 2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}$, $\vec{b} = \hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $\vec{c} = \hat{i} + \hat{k}$.
--- ans
$\vec{b} + \vec{c} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$; $\ |\vec{a}| = \sqrt{4 + 4 + 1} = 3$.
$$\text{Projection of } (\vec{b} + \vec{c}) \text{ on } \vec{a} = \frac{(\vec{b} + \vec{c})\cdot\vec{a}}{|\vec{a}|} = \frac{4 + 6 + 2}{3} = \frac{12}{3} = 4$$

=== 31 | prob | 3 | sub
@topic Mean of a random variable
An urn contains 3 red and 2 white marbles. Two marbles are drawn one by one with replacement from the urn. Find the probability distribution of the number of white balls. Also, find the mean of the number of white balls drawn.
--- ans
Let $X$ = number of white marbles drawn; $X = 0, 1, 2$. In each draw (with replacement) $P(W) = \dfrac{2}{5}$, $P(R) = \dfrac{3}{5}$.
$P(X = 0) = P(RR) = \dfrac{3}{5}\cdot\dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{9}{25}$
$P(X = 1) = P(RW) + P(WR) = 2\cdot\dfrac{3}{5}\cdot\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{12}{25}$
$P(X = 2) = P(WW) = \dfrac{2}{5}\cdot\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{4}{25}$

| X | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| P(X) | $\frac{9}{25}$ | $\frac{12}{25}$ | $\frac{4}{25}$ |

$$\text{Mean} = \sum X\,P(X) = 0\cdot\frac{9}{25} + 1\cdot\frac{12}{25} + 2\cdot\frac{4}{25} = \frac{20}{25} = \frac{4}{5}$$

@topic Graphical method – bounded region
Solve the following L.P.P. graphically :
Minimise $Z = 6x + 3y$
Subject to constraints
$4x + y \ge 80$;
$x + 5y \ge 115$;
$3x + 2y \le 150$
$x, y \ge 0$
--- ans
Draw $4x + y = 80$ (through $(20, 0), (0, 80)$), $x + 5y = 115$ (through $(115, 0), (0, 23)$) and $3x + 2y = 150$ (through $(50, 0), (0, 75)$).
Intersections:
$4x + y = 80$ and $3x + 2y = 150$: $y = 80 - 4x \Rightarrow 3x + 160 - 8x = 150 \Rightarrow x = 2,\ y = 72$ → $A(2, 72)$.
$4x + y = 80$ and $x + 5y = 115$: $x = 115 - 5y \Rightarrow 460 - 19y = 80 \Rightarrow y = 20,\ x = 15$ → $B(15, 20)$.
$x + 5y = 115$ and $3x + 2y = 150$: $345 - 13y = 150 \Rightarrow y = 15,\ x = 40$ → $C(40, 15)$.
The feasible region is the bounded triangle ABC.
