@topic Minors and cofactors
If $A = [a_{ij}] = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 5 \\ 1 & 3 & 2 \\ 5 & 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $C_{ij}$ is the cofactor of element $a_{ij}$ then the value of $a_{21}\cdot C_{11} + a_{22}\cdot C_{12} + a_{23}\cdot C_{13}$ is
(A) $-57$
(B) $0$
(C) $9$
(D) $57$
--- ans B
The sum of the products of the elements of one row with the cofactors of the corresponding elements of a **different** row is always $0$.
Check: $C_{11} = 12 - 0 = 12$, $C_{12} = -(4 - 10) = 6$, $C_{13} = 0 - 15 = -15$, so $1(12) + 3(6) + 2(-15) = 0$.

@topic Operations on matrices
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $A^2 - kA - 5I = 0$, then the value of $k$ is
(A) $3$
(B) $5$
(C) $7$
(D) $9$
--- ans B
$A^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 + 9 & 3 + 12 \\ 3 + 12 & 9 + 16 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 15 \\ 15 & 25 \end{bmatrix}$.
$$A^2 - kA - 5I = \begin{bmatrix} 5 - k & 15 - 3k \\ 15 - 3k & 20 - 4k \end{bmatrix} = O \Rightarrow k = 5$$

@topic Chain rule and implicit differentiation
If $e^{x^2y} = c$, then $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ is
(A) $\dfrac{xe^{x^2y}}{2y}$
(B) $\dfrac{-2y}{x}$
(C) $\dfrac{2y}{x}$
(D) $\dfrac{x}{2y}$
--- ans B
Differentiating: $e^{x^2y}\left(2xy + x^2\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0$. As $e^{x^2y} \ne 0$:
$$x^2\frac{dy}{dx} = -2xy \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2y}{x}$$

@topic Continuity
The value of the constant c that makes the function f defined by
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 - c^2, & \text{if } x < 4 \\ cx + 20, & \text{if } x \ge 4 \end{cases}$$
continuous for all real numbers is
(A) $-2$
(B) $-1$
(C) $0$
(D) $2$
--- ans A
Both pieces are polynomials; continuity at $x = 4$ needs $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 4^-} f(x) = f(4)$:
$$16 - c^2 = 4c + 20 \Rightarrow c^2 + 4c + 4 = 0 \Rightarrow (c + 2)^2 = 0 \Rightarrow c = -2$$

A vector perpendicular to the line $\vec{r} = \hat{i} + \hat{j} - \hat{k} + \lambda(3\hat{i} - \hat{j})$ is

=== 23 | aod | 2 | sub
@topic Increasing and decreasing functions
Show that $f(x) = \dfrac{4\sin x}{2 + \cos x} - x$ is an increasing function of $x$ in $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$.
--- ans
$$f'(x) = \frac{4\cos x(2 + \cos x) - 4\sin x(-\sin x)}{(2 + \cos x)^2} - 1 = \frac{8\cos x + 4\cos^2 x + 4\sin^2 x}{(2 + \cos x)^2} - 1 = \frac{8\cos x + 4}{(2 + \cos x)^2} - 1$$
$$= \frac{8\cos x + 4 - (4 + 4\cos x + \cos^2 x)}{(2 + \cos x)^2} = \frac{\cos x\,(4 - \cos x)}{(2 + \cos x)^2}$$
For $x \in \left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$: $\cos x \ge 0$, $4 - \cos x > 0$ and $(2 + \cos x)^2 > 0$, so $f'(x) \ge 0$ (equality only at $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$).
Hence $f$ is increasing in $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$.

=== 25 | int | 2 | sub
@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \cos x\cdot\log\left(\frac{1 + x}{1 - x}\right)dx$
--- ans
Let $f(x) = \cos x\log\left(\dfrac{1 + x}{1 - x}\right)$. Then
$$f(-x) = \cos(-x)\log\left(\frac{1 - x}{1 + x}\right) = -\cos x\log\left(\frac{1 + x}{1 - x}\right) = -f(x)$$
so $f$ is odd, and $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = 0$ for an odd function.
$$\therefore \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \cos x\log\left(\frac{1 + x}{1 - x}\right)dx = 0$$

=== 26 | cd | 3 | sub
@topic Logarithmic differentiation
Given that $x^y + y^x = a^b$, where $a$ and $b$ are positive constants, find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
--- ans
Let $u = x^y$, $v = y^x$; then $u + v = a^b$ (constant), so $\dfrac{du}{dx} + \dfrac{dv}{dx} = 0$.
$\log u = y\log x \Rightarrow \dfrac{du}{dx} = x^y\left(\dfrac{y}{x} + \log x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)$.
$\log v = x\log y \Rightarrow \dfrac{dv}{dx} = y^x\left(\log y + \dfrac{x}{y}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)$.
$$x^y\left(\frac{y}{x} + \log x\frac{dy}{dx}\right) + y^x\left(\log y + \frac{x}{y}\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx}\left(x^y\log x + xy^{x-1}\right) = -\left(yx^{y-1} + y^x\log y\right)$$
$$\therefore \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{yx^{y-1} + y^x\log y}{x^y\log x + xy^{x-1}}$$

=== 28 | int | 3 | sub
@topic Partial fractions
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{2x + 3}{x^2(x + 3)}\,dx$
--- ans
Let $\dfrac{2x + 3}{x^2(x + 3)} = \dfrac{A}{x} + \dfrac{B}{x^2} + \dfrac{C}{x + 3}$, so $2x + 3 = Ax(x + 3) + B(x + 3) + Cx^2$.
$x = 0$: $3 = 3B \Rightarrow B = 1$. $\quad x = -3$: $-3 = 9C \Rightarrow C = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.
Coefficient of $x^2$: $0 = A + C \Rightarrow A = \dfrac{1}{3}$.
$$\int \frac{2x + 3}{x^2(x + 3)}\,dx = \frac{1}{3}\int \frac{dx}{x} + \int \frac{dx}{x^2} - \frac{1}{3}\int \frac{dx}{x + 3} = \frac{1}{3}\log|x| - \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{3}\log|x + 3| + C$$
$$= \frac{1}{3}\log\left|\frac{x}{x + 3}\right| - \frac{1}{x} + C$$

@topic Systems of linear equations
Use the product of matrices $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & -2 \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ -7 & 7 & -7 \\ -7 & 5 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$ to solve the following system of equations:
$x + 2y - 3z = 6$
$3x + 2y - 2z = 3$
$2x - y + z = 2$
--- ans
Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & -2 \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, $B = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ -7 & 7 & -7 \\ -7 & 5 & -4 \end{bmatrix}$.
$$AB = \begin{bmatrix} 0 - 14 + 21 & 1 + 14 - 15 & 2 - 14 + 12 \\ 0 - 14 + 14 & 3 + 14 - 10 & 6 - 14 + 8 \\ 0 + 7 - 7 & 2 - 7 + 5 & 4 + 7 - 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 7 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 7 \end{bmatrix} = 7I$$
$\therefore A^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{7}B$.
The system is $AX = C$ with $C = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$:
$$X = A^{-1}C = \frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ -7 & 7 & -7 \\ -7 & 5 & -4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix} 0 + 3 + 4 \\ -42 + 21 - 14 \\ -42 + 15 - 8 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ -35 \\ -35 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -5 \\ -5 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\therefore x = 1,\quad y = -5,\quad z = -5$$
