If A is a square metric of order 2 and $|A| = -2$, then value of $|5A'|$ is
(A) $-50$
(B) $-10$
(C) $10$
(D) $50$
--- ans A
("metric" is a misprint for "matrix".) For a matrix of order $n$, $|kA| = k^n|A|$, and $|A'| = |A|$.
$$|5A'| = 5^2|A'| = 25 \times (-2) = -50$$

=== 5 | mat | 1 | mcq
@topic Operations on matrices
The product of matrix P and Q is equal to a diagonal matrix. If the order of matrix Q is $3 \times 2$, then order of matrix P is
(A) $2 \times 2$
(B) $3 \times 3$
(C) $2 \times 3$
(D) $3 \times 2$
--- ans C
For $PQ$ to be defined, P must have 3 columns: P is $m \times 3$, and then $PQ$ is $m \times 2$.
A diagonal matrix is square, so $m = 2$. Hence P is of order $2 \times 3$.

=== 7 | cd | 1 | mcq
@topic Chain rule and implicit differentiation
If $\sin(xy) = 1$, then $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ is equal to
(A) $\dfrac{x}{y}$
(B) $-\dfrac{x}{y}$
(C) $\dfrac{y}{x}$
(D) $-\dfrac{y}{x}$
--- ans D
$\sin(xy) = 1 \Rightarrow xy = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ (a constant, taking the principal value; any value $\frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$ works the same way).
Differentiating $xy = \text{constant}$: $x\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y = 0 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{y}{x}$.

=== 11 | int | 1 | mcq
@topic Definite integrals
The value of $\displaystyle\int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2} \cot\theta\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta\,d\theta$ is
(A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
(B) $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
(C) $0$
(D) $-\dfrac{\pi}{8}$
--- ans A
Put $\cot\theta = t$, $-\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta\,d\theta = dt$; $\theta = \frac{\pi}{4} \to t = 1$, $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} \to t = 0$.
$$\int_1^0 t(-dt) = \int_0^1 t\,dt = \left[\frac{t^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{2}$$

@topic Variable separable
The general solution of the differential equation $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = e^{x + y}$ is:
(A) $e^x + e^{-y} = c$
(B) $e^{-x} + e^{-y} = c$
(C) $e^{x + y} = c$
(D) $2e^{x + y} = c$
--- ans A
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = e^x e^y \Rightarrow e^{-y}\,dy = e^x\,dx$.
Integrating: $-e^{-y} = e^x + k \Rightarrow e^x + e^{-y} = c$ (where $c = -k$).

=== 21 | itf | 2 | sub
@topic Principal values
If $a = \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) + \cos^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ and $b = \tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{3}\right) - \cot^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$ then find the value of $a + b$.
--- ans
Using the principal value branches:
$\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$; $\ \cos^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.
$$a = \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{11\pi}{12}$$
$\tan^{-1}\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$; $\ \cot^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = \pi - \cot^{-1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.
$$b = \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{2\pi}{3} = -\frac{\pi}{3}$$
$$\therefore a + b = \frac{11\pi}{12} - \frac{4\pi}{12} = \frac{7\pi}{12}$$

=== 23 | aod | 2 | sub
@topic Rate of change
Sand is pouring from a pipe at the rate of $15\ \text{cm}^3$/minute. The falling sand forms a cone on the ground such that the height of the cone is always one-third of the radius of the base. How fast is the height of the sand cone increasing at the instant when the height is 4 cm?
--- ans
$h = \dfrac{r}{3} \Rightarrow r = 3h$.
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2h = \frac{1}{3}\pi(3h)^2h = 3\pi h^3 \Rightarrow \frac{dV}{dt} = 9\pi h^2\frac{dh}{dt}$$
At $h = 4$: $15 = 9\pi(16)\dfrac{dh}{dt} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dh}{dt} = \dfrac{15}{144\pi} = \dfrac{5}{48\pi}$.
The height is increasing at **$\dfrac{5}{48\pi}$ cm/minute**.

=== 27 | cd | 3 | sub
@topic Differentiability
Find the values of a and b so that the following function is differentiable for all values of $x$:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} ax + b, & x > -1 \\ bx^2 - 3, & x < -1 \end{cases}$$
--- ans
(As printed, $f$ is not defined at $x = -1$; the intended function has $bx^2 - 3$ for $x \le -1$, which is used below.)
Both pieces are polynomials, so $f$ is differentiable for $x \ne -1$. For differentiability at $x = -1$, $f$ must first be continuous there:
$$\lim_{x \to -1^-}(bx^2 - 3) = \lim_{x \to -1^+}(ax + b) = f(-1) \Rightarrow b - 3 = -a + b \Rightarrow a = 3$$
Left hand derivative at $-1$: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(bx^2 - 3)\Big|_{x = -1} = 2b(-1) = -2b$.
Right hand derivative at $-1$: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(ax + b) = a$.
LHD $=$ RHD $\Rightarrow -2b = a = 3 \Rightarrow b = -\dfrac{3}{2}$.
$$\therefore a = 3,\quad b = -\frac{3}{2}$$

=== 30 | vec | 3 | sub
@topic Vector product and area
Given $\vec{a} = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + \hat{k}$, $\vec{b} = 3\hat{i} - \hat{k}$ and $\vec{c} = 2\hat{i} + \hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$. Find a vector $\vec{d}$ which is perpendicular to both $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ and $\vec{c}\cdot\vec{d} = 3$.
--- ans
A vector perpendicular to both $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is parallel to $\vec{a} \times \vec{b}$:
$$\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 3 & 0 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{i}(1 - 0) - \hat{j}(-2 - 3) + \hat{k}(0 + 3) = \hat{i} + 5\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$$
Let $\vec{d} = \lambda(\hat{i} + 5\hat{j} + 3\hat{k})$. Then
$$\vec{c}\cdot\vec{d} = \lambda(2 + 5 - 6) = \lambda = 3$$
$$\therefore \vec{d} = 3\hat{i} + 15\hat{j} + 9\hat{k}$$

=== 31 | prob | 3 | sub
@topic Total probability and Bayes' theorem
Bag I contains 3 red and 4 black balls, Bag II contains 5 red and 2 black balls. Two balls are transferred at random from Bag I to Bag II and then a ball is drawn at random from Bag II. Find the probability that the drawn ball is red in colour.
--- ans
Let $E_1$: both transferred balls are red; $E_2$: both are black; $E_3$: one red and one black; $A$: the ball drawn from Bag II is red.
$$P(E_1) = \frac{^3C_2}{^7C_2} = \frac{3}{21} = \frac{1}{7},\quad P(E_2) = \frac{^4C_2}{^7C_2} = \frac{6}{21} = \frac{2}{7},\quad P(E_3) = \frac{^3C_1\cdot{}^4C_1}{^7C_2} = \frac{12}{21} = \frac{4}{7}$$
After the transfer Bag II has 9 balls:
$P(A|E_1) = \dfrac{7}{9}$, $\ P(A|E_2) = \dfrac{5}{9}$, $\ P(A|E_3) = \dfrac{6}{9}$.
By the theorem of total probability,
$$P(A) = \frac{1}{7}\cdot\frac{7}{9} + \frac{2}{7}\cdot\frac{5}{9} + \frac{4}{7}\cdot\frac{6}{9} = \frac{7 + 10 + 24}{63} = \frac{41}{63}$$

Subject to $x + 2y \le 12$
$2x + y \le 12$
$x + \dfrac{5}{4}y \ge 5$

--- ans
Draw $x + 2y = 12$ (through $(12, 0), (0, 6)$), $2x + y = 12$ (through $(6, 0), (0, 12)$) and $x + \frac{5}{4}y = 5$ (through $(5, 0), (0, 4)$).
