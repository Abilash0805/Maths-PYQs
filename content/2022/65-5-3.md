@topic Direction cosines and ratios
The Cartesian equation of a line AB is: $\dfrac{2x - 1}{12} = \dfrac{y + 2}{2} = \dfrac{z - 3}{3}$
Find the direction cosines of a line parallel to line AB.
--- ans
Writing the equation in standard form:
$$\frac{x - \frac{1}{2}}{6} = \frac{y - (-2)}{2} = \frac{z - 3}{3}$$
So the direction ratios of AB (and of any line parallel to it) are $6, 2, 3$.
$\sqrt{6^2 + 2^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{49} = 7$
$\therefore$ Direction cosines of a line parallel to AB are $\dfrac{6}{7}, \dfrac{2}{7}, \dfrac{3}{7}$ (or $-\dfrac{6}{7}, -\dfrac{2}{7}, -\dfrac{3}{7}$).

@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_1^3 \frac{\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{4 - x}}\,dx$.
--- ans
$$I = \int_1^3 \frac{\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{4 - x}}\,dx \quad\cdots(1)$$
Using $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(a + b - x)\,dx$ with $a + b = 4$:
$$I = \int_1^3 \frac{\sqrt{4 - x}}{\sqrt{4 - x} + \sqrt{x}}\,dx \quad\cdots(2)$$
Adding (1) and (2):
$$2I = \int_1^3 \frac{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{4 - x}}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{4 - x}}\,dx = \int_1^3 1\,dx = \Big[x\Big]_1^3 = 2$$
$$\therefore\ I = 1$$

@topic Total probability and Bayes' theorem
There are two boxes, namely box-I and box-II. Box-I contains 3 red and 6 black balls. Box-II contains 5 red and 5 black balls. One of the two boxes, is selected at random and a ball is drawn at random. The ball drawn is found to be red. Find the probability that this red ball comes out from box-II.
--- ans
Let $E_1$: box-I is selected, $E_2$: box-II is selected, $A$: the ball drawn is red.
$$P(E_1) = P(E_2) = \frac{1}{2},\qquad P(A|E_1) = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3},\qquad P(A|E_2) = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}$$
By Bayes' theorem:
$$P(E_2|A) = \frac{P(E_2)P(A|E_2)}{P(E_1)P(A|E_1) + P(E_2)P(A|E_2)} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{4}} = \frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{5}{12}} = \frac{3}{5}$$
Required probability $= \dfrac{3}{5}$.
