@topic Special forms
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{x^2 - 6x + 13}$
--- ans
Completing the square: $x^2 - 6x + 13 = (x^2 - 6x + 9) + 4 = (x - 3)^2 + 2^2$.
$$I = \int \frac{dx}{(x - 3)^2 + 2^2}$$
Using $\displaystyle\int \frac{du}{u^2 + a^2} = \frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\frac{u}{a} + C$ with $u = x - 3$, $a = 2$:
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x - 3}{2}\right) + C$$

@topic Variable separable
Find the general solution of the differential equation: $e^{dy/dx} = x^2$.
--- ans
Taking logarithm on both sides ($x \ne 0$):
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \log x^2 = 2\log|x| \Rightarrow dy = 2\log|x|\,dx$$
Integrating both sides (integration by parts, taking $\log|x|$ as the first function and $1$ as the second):
$$y = 2\left[x\log|x| - \int \frac{1}{x}\cdot x\,dx\right] = 2\left[x\log|x| - x\right] + C$$
$$\therefore\ y = 2x\left(\log|x| - 1\right) + C$$
is the general solution.

=== 5 | prob | 2 | sub
@topic Random variables and probability distributions
Two cards are drawn successively with replacement from a well shuffled pack of 52 cards. Find the probability distribution of the number of spade cards.
--- ans
Let X = number of spade cards drawn. X can take the values $0, 1, 2$.
Since the cards are drawn with replacement, the two draws are independent, and in each draw
$$P(\text{spade}) = \frac{13}{52} = \frac{1}{4},\qquad P(\text{non-spade}) = \frac{3}{4}$$
$$P(X = 0) = \frac{3}{4}\times\frac{3}{4} = \frac{9}{16}$$
$$P(X = 1) = P(SN) + P(NS) = \frac{1}{4}\times\frac{3}{4} + \frac{3}{4}\times\frac{1}{4} = \frac{6}{16} = \frac{3}{8}$$
$$P(X = 2) = \frac{1}{4}\times\frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{16}$$
The probability distribution of X is

| $X$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $P(X)$ | $\dfrac{9}{16}$ | $\dfrac{6}{16}$ | $\dfrac{1}{16}$ |

(The publisher's solution uses combinations, i.e. drawing **without** replacement; that does not match the question.)

=== 6a | prob | 2 | sub
@topic Conditional probability
A pair of dice is thrown and the sum of the numbers appearing on the dice is observed to be 7. Find the probability that the number 5 has appeared on atleast one die.
--- ans
Sample space has $36$ equally likely outcomes.
Let E: 5 appears on at least one die; F: the sum of the numbers is 7.
$F = \{(1, 6), (2, 5), (3, 4), (4, 3), (5, 2), (6, 1)\} \Rightarrow P(F) = \dfrac{6}{36}$
$E \cap F = \{(2, 5), (5, 2)\} \Rightarrow P(E \cap F) = \dfrac{2}{36}$
$$P(E|F) = \frac{P(E \cap F)}{P(F)} = \frac{2/36}{6/36} = \frac{1}{3}$$

=== 6b | prob | 2 | sub
@topic Multiplication theorem and independent events
The probability that A hits the target is $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and the probability that B hits it, is $\dfrac{2}{5}$. If both try to hit the target independently, find the probability that the target is hit.
--- ans
$P(A) = \dfrac{1}{3}$, $P(B) = \dfrac{2}{5}$, so $P(\bar{A}) = \dfrac{2}{3}$, $P(\bar{B}) = \dfrac{3}{5}$.
The target is not hit only if both miss. Since A and B are independent,
$$P(\text{target not hit}) = P(\bar{A})P(\bar{B}) = \frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{5}$$
$$\therefore\ P(\text{target is hit}) = 1 - \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$$

=== 7 | int | 3 | sub
@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{dx}{1 + e^{\sin x}}$
--- ans
Let $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{\sin x}}$. Using $\displaystyle\int_0^{2a} f(x)\,dx = \int_0^{a}\left[f(x) + f(2a - x)\right]dx$ with $a = \pi$:
$$I = \int_0^{\pi}\left[\frac{1}{1 + e^{\sin x}} + \frac{1}{1 + e^{\sin(2\pi - x)}}\right]dx = \int_0^{\pi}\left[\frac{1}{1 + e^{\sin x}} + \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\sin x}}\right]dx$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-\sin x}} = \dfrac{e^{\sin x}}{e^{\sin x} + 1}$,
$$I = \int_0^{\pi} \frac{1 + e^{\sin x}}{1 + e^{\sin x}}\,dx = \int_0^{\pi} 1\,dx = \Big[x\Big]_0^{\pi} = \pi$$

=== 8a | de | 3 | sub
@topic Linear differential equations
Find the particular solution of the differential equation $x\dfrac{dy}{dx} - y = x^2 \cdot e^x$, given $y(1) = 0$.
--- ans
Dividing by $x$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} - \frac{1}{x}\,y = xe^x$$
This is linear of the form $\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Q$ with $P = -\dfrac{1}{x}$, $Q = xe^x$.
$$\text{I.F.} = e^{\int -\frac{1}{x}\,dx} = e^{-\log x} = \frac{1}{x}\quad (x > 0)$$
The solution is $y\cdot\text{I.F.} = \displaystyle\int Q\cdot\text{I.F.}\,dx + C$:
$$\frac{y}{x} = \int xe^x\cdot\frac{1}{x}\,dx + C = e^x + C$$
Given $y(1) = 0$: $0 = e + C \Rightarrow C = -e$.
$$\therefore\ \frac{y}{x} = e^x - e \quad\text{i.e.}\quad y = xe^x - ex$$
is the required particular solution.

=== 8b | de | 3 | sub
@topic Homogeneous equations
Find the general solution of the differential equation $x\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y(\log y - \log x + 1)$.
--- ans
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{x}\left(\log\frac{y}{x} + 1\right)$$
The right side is a function of $\dfrac{y}{x}$, so the equation is homogeneous. Put $y = vx \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = v + x\dfrac{dv}{dx}$:
$$v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = v(\log v + 1) \Rightarrow x\frac{dv}{dx} = v\log v \Rightarrow \frac{dv}{v\log v} = \frac{dx}{x}$$
Integrating (put $\log v = t$, $\dfrac{dv}{v} = dt$ on the left):
$$\log|\log v| = \log|x| + \log|C| \Rightarrow \log v = Cx$$
Putting $v = \dfrac{y}{x}$:
$$\log\frac{y}{x} = Cx \quad\left(\text{or } y = xe^{Cx}\right)$$
is the general solution.

@topic Vector product and area
The two adjacent sides of a parallelogram are represented by vectors $2\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$ and $\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$. Find the unit vector parallel to one of its diagonals, Also, find the area of the parallelogram.
--- ans
Let $\vec{a} = 2\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b} = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$. A diagonal is
$$\vec{d} = \vec{a} + \vec{b} = 3\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 2\hat{k},\qquad |\vec{d}| = \sqrt{9 + 36 + 4} = 7$$
Unit vector parallel to this diagonal:
$$\hat{d} = \frac{\vec{d}}{|\vec{d}|} = \frac{3}{7}\hat{i} - \frac{6}{7}\hat{j} + \frac{2}{7}\hat{k}$$
(For the other diagonal $\vec{a} - \vec{b} = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 8\hat{k}$, the unit vector is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{69}}(\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 8\hat{k})$.)
Area of the parallelogram $= |\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|$:
$$\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & -4 & 5 \\ 1 & -2 & -3 \end{vmatrix} = (12 + 10)\hat{i} - (-6 - 5)\hat{j} + (-4 + 4)\hat{k} = 22\hat{i} + 11\hat{j}$$
$$\text{Area} = \sqrt{22^2 + 11^2} = \sqrt{484 + 121} = \sqrt{605} = 11\sqrt{5}\ \text{sq. units}$$

@topic Scalar product and projection
If $\vec{a} = 2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$, $\vec{b} = -\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $\vec{c} = 3\hat{i} + \hat{j}$ are such that the vector $(\vec{a} + \lambda\vec{b})$ is perpendicular to vector $\vec{c}$, then find the value of $\lambda$.
--- ans
$$\vec{a} + \lambda\vec{b} = (2 - \lambda)\hat{i} + (2 + 2\lambda)\hat{j} + (3 + \lambda)\hat{k}$$
Since $(\vec{a} + \lambda\vec{b}) \perp \vec{c}$, $(\vec{a} + \lambda\vec{b}) \cdot \vec{c} = 0$:
$$3(2 - \lambda) + 1(2 + 2\lambda) + 0(3 + \lambda) = 0 \Rightarrow 6 - 3\lambda + 2 + 2\lambda = 0 \Rightarrow 8 - \lambda = 0$$
$$\therefore\ \lambda = 8$$

@topic Area bounded by a curve and a line
Find the area of the region bounded by curve $4x^2 = y$ and the line $y = 8x + 12$, using integration.
--- ans
Points of intersection: $4x^2 = 8x + 12 \Rightarrow x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 3)(x + 1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -1, 3$.
The points are $(-1, 4)$ and $(3, 36)$.

```plot

```

For $-1 \le x \le 3$ the line lies above the parabola, so
$$A = \int_{-1}^{3}\left[(8x + 12) - 4x^2\right]dx = 4\int_{-1}^{3}\left(2x + 3 - x^2\right)dx = 4\left[x^2 + 3x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{3}$$
$$= 4\left[(9 + 9 - 9) - \left(1 - 3 + \frac{1}{3}\right)\right] = 4\left[9 + \frac{5}{3}\right] = 4\times\frac{32}{3} = \frac{128}{3}$$
Required area $= \dfrac{128}{3}$ sq. units.

@topic Partial fractions
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{x^2}{(x^2 + 1)(3x^2 + 4)}\,dx$
--- ans
Put $x^2 = t$ (only for the partial fractions):
$$\frac{t}{(t + 1)(3t + 4)} = \frac{A}{t + 1} + \frac{B}{3t + 4} \Rightarrow t = A(3t + 4) + B(t + 1)$$
$t = -1$: $-1 = A$; $\quad t = -\dfrac{4}{3}$: $-\dfrac{4}{3} = -\dfrac{B}{3} \Rightarrow B = 4$.
$$\therefore\ \frac{x^2}{(x^2 + 1)(3x^2 + 4)} = \frac{-1}{x^2 + 1} + \frac{4}{3x^2 + 4}$$
$$I = -\int \frac{dx}{x^2 + 1} + \frac{4}{3}\int \frac{dx}{x^2 + \left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2} = -\tan^{-1}x + \frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\tan^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{3}x}{2} + C$$
$$\therefore\ I = -\tan^{-1}x + \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}x}{2}\right) + C$$

@topic Definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{1} \sqrt{5 - 4x - x^2}\,dx$
--- ans
$5 - 4x - x^2 = 5 - (x^2 + 4x + 4) + 4 = 9 - (x + 2)^2 = 3^2 - (x + 2)^2$.
Using $\displaystyle\int \sqrt{a^2 - u^2}\,du = \frac{u}{2}\sqrt{a^2 - u^2} + \frac{a^2}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{u}{a} + C$ with $u = x + 2$, $a = 3$:
$$I = \left[\frac{x + 2}{2}\sqrt{9 - (x + 2)^2} + \frac{9}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{x + 2}{3}\right]_{-2}^{1}$$
$$= \left[\frac{3}{2}\cdot 0 + \frac{9}{2}\sin^{-1}1\right] - \left[0 + \frac{9}{2}\sin^{-1}0\right] = \frac{9}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{9\pi}{4}$$

@topic Total probability and Bayes' theorem
@fig 2022-65-3-1-q14.webp
A shopkeeper sells three types of flower seeds A1, A2, A3. They are sold is the form of a mixture, where the proportions of these seeds are $4 : 4 : 2$ respectively. The germination rates of the three types of seeds are 45%, 60% and 35% respectively.

Based on the above information:
(a) Calculate the probability that a randomly chosen seed will germinate: **[2]**
(b) Calculate the probability that the seed is of type A2, given that a randomly chosen seed germinates. **[2]**
--- ans
Let $E_1, E_2, E_3$: the chosen seed is of type A1, A2, A3 respectively; $G$: the seed germinates.
$$P(E_1) = \frac{4}{10},\quad P(E_2) = \frac{4}{10},\quad P(E_3) = \frac{2}{10}$$
$$P(G|E_1) = 0.45,\quad P(G|E_2) = 0.60,\quad P(G|E_3) = 0.35$$

**(a)** By the theorem of total probability,
$$P(G) = P(E_1)P(G|E_1) + P(E_2)P(G|E_2) + P(E_3)P(G|E_3)$$
$$= 0.4\times 0.45 + 0.4\times 0.60 + 0.2\times 0.35 = 0.18 + 0.24 + 0.07 = 0.49$$

**(b)** By Bayes' theorem,
$$P(E_2|G) = \frac{P(E_2)P(G|E_2)}{P(G)} = \frac{0.24}{0.49} = \frac{24}{49}$$
