=== 2 | de | 2 | sub
@topic Variable separable
Find the general solution of the differential equation: $\log\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) = ax + by$.
--- ans
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{ax + by} = e^{ax}\cdot e^{by} \Rightarrow e^{-by}\,dy = e^{ax}\,dx$$
Integrating both sides:
$$\int e^{-by}\,dy = \int e^{ax}\,dx \Rightarrow -\frac{e^{-by}}{b} = \frac{e^{ax}}{a} + C$$
$$\therefore\ \frac{e^{ax}}{a} + \frac{e^{-by}}{b} + C = 0$$
is the general solution (here $a, b \ne 0$).

=== 7a | vec | 3 | sub
@topic Vector product and area
If $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}$ are three vectors such that $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{c}$ and $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{a} \times \vec{c}$, $\vec{a} \ne \vec{0}$, then show that $\vec{b} = \vec{c}$.
--- ans
$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{c} \Rightarrow \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = 0 \quad\cdots(1)$
$\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{a} \times \vec{c} \Rightarrow \vec{a} \times (\vec{b} - \vec{c}) = \vec{0} \quad\cdots(2)$
Suppose $\vec{b} - \vec{c} \ne \vec{0}$. Since $\vec{a} \ne \vec{0}$:
from (1), $\vec{a} \perp (\vec{b} - \vec{c})$; from (2), $\vec{a} \parallel (\vec{b} - \vec{c})$.
A non-zero vector cannot be both perpendicular and parallel to another non-zero vector — a contradiction.
(Equivalently, $|\vec{a}|^2|\vec{b} - \vec{c}|^2 = \left(\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{c})\right)^2 + |\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} - \vec{c})|^2 = 0$ forces $|\vec{b} - \vec{c}| = 0$.)
$$\therefore\ \vec{b} - \vec{c} = \vec{0} \Rightarrow \vec{b} = \vec{c}$$
Hence proved.

=== 7b | vec | 3 | sub
@topic Scalar product and projection
If $|\vec{a}| = 3$, $|\vec{b}| = 5$, $|\vec{c}| = 4$ and $\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = \vec{0}$, then find the value of $(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{b} \cdot \vec{c} + \vec{c} \cdot \vec{a})$.
--- ans
$$\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = \vec{0} \Rightarrow (\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}) \cdot (\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}) = 0$$
$$\Rightarrow |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 + |\vec{c}|^2 + 2(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{b} \cdot \vec{c} + \vec{c} \cdot \vec{a}) = 0$$
$$\Rightarrow 9 + 25 + 16 + 2(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{b} \cdot \vec{c} + \vec{c} \cdot \vec{a}) = 0$$
$$\therefore\ \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{b} \cdot \vec{c} + \vec{c} \cdot \vec{a} = -\frac{50}{2} = -25$$

=== 8 | int | 3 | sub
@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} |x^3 - x|\,dx$
--- ans
$x^3 - x = x(x - 1)(x + 1)$, which is $\ge 0$ on $[-1, 0]$, $\le 0$ on $[0, 1]$ and $\ge 0$ on $[1, 2]$.
$$I = \int_{-1}^{0}(x^3 - x)\,dx + \int_{0}^{1}(x - x^3)\,dx + \int_{1}^{2}(x^3 - x)\,dx$$
$$= \left[\frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{0} + \left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_{0}^{1} + \left[\frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{1}^{2}$$
$$= \left[0 - \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right] + \left[\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}\right] + \left[(4 - 2) - \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$$

=== 12 | aoi | 4 | sub
@topic Area of circles and ellipses
Using integration, find the area of the region bounded by the curves $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, $x = \sqrt{3}y$ and X-axis lying in the first quadrant.
--- ans
Intersection of $x = \sqrt{3}y$ with $x^2 + y^2 = 4$: $3y^2 + y^2 = 4 \Rightarrow y = 1$ (first quadrant), $x = \sqrt{3}$, i.e. $C(\sqrt{3}, 1)$. The circle meets the X-axis at $A(2, 0)$.

```plot
{"x":[-0.5,2.6],"y":[-0.5,2.4],"circles":[{"cx":0,"cy":0,"r":2,"label":"x² + y² = 4"}],"lines":[{"a":1,"b":-1.7320508,"c":0,"label":"x = √3 y"}],"areas":[{"upper":"x/sqrt(3)","lower":"0","from":0,"to":1.7320508},{"upper":"sqrt(4-x*x)","lower":"0","from":1.7320508,"to":2}],"points":[{"x":0,"y":0,"label":"O"},{"x":1.7320508,"y":1,"label":"C(√3, 1)"},{"x":2,"y":0,"label":"A(2, 0)"}]}
```

The region OCA is bounded above by the line $y = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{3}}$ for $0 \le x \le \sqrt{3}$ and by the circle $y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$ for $\sqrt{3} \le x \le 2$:
$$A = \int_0^{\sqrt{3}} \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}}\,dx + \int_{\sqrt{3}}^{2} \sqrt{4 - x^2}\,dx$$
$$\int_0^{\sqrt{3}} \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}}\,dx = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\cdot\frac{3}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$
$$\int_{\sqrt{3}}^{2} \sqrt{4 - x^2}\,dx = \left[\frac{x}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^2} + \frac{4}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{2}\right]_{\sqrt{3}}^{2} = \left[0 + 2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\right] - \left[\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot 1 + 2\cdot\frac{\pi}{3}\right] = \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$
$$\therefore\ A = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\pi}{3}\ \text{sq. units}$$
