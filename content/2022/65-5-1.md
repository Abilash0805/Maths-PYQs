@topic Integrals of the form ∫dx/√(a²−x²)
Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{4x - x^2}}$
--- ans
Completing the square: $4x - x^2 = -(x^2 - 4x + 4) + 4 = 2^2 - (x - 2)^2$.
$$I = \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{2^2 - (x - 2)^2}}$$
Using $\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2 - u^2}} = \sin^{-1}\frac{u}{a} + C$ with $u = x - 2$, $a = 2$:
$$I = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x - 2}{2}\right) + C$$

@topic Variable separable
Find the general solution of the following differential equation:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{x - y} + x^2e^{-y}$$
--- ans
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{-y}\left(e^x + x^2\right) \Rightarrow e^y\,dy = \left(e^x + x^2\right)dx$$
Integrating both sides:
$$\int e^y\,dy = \int \left(e^x + x^2\right)dx \Rightarrow e^y = e^x + \frac{x^3}{3} + C$$
is the general solution.

@topic Probability distribution of a random variable
Let X be a random variable which assumes values $x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4$ such that $2P(X = x_1) = 3P(X = x_2) = P(X = x_3) = 5P(X = x_4)$.
Find the probability distribution of X.
--- ans
Let $2P(X = x_1) = 3P(X = x_2) = P(X = x_3) = 5P(X = x_4) = k$. Then
$$P(X = x_1) = \frac{k}{2},\quad P(X = x_2) = \frac{k}{3},\quad P(X = x_3) = k,\quad P(X = x_4) = \frac{k}{5}$$
Sum of probabilities $= 1$:
$$\frac{k}{2} + \frac{k}{3} + k + \frac{k}{5} = 1 \Rightarrow \frac{15k + 10k + 30k + 6k}{30} = 1 \Rightarrow k = \frac{30}{61}$$
The probability distribution of X is

| $X$ | $x_1$ | $x_2$ | $x_3$ | $x_4$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $P(X)$ | $\dfrac{15}{61}$ | $\dfrac{10}{61}$ | $\dfrac{30}{61}$ | $\dfrac{6}{61}$ |

@topic Scalar and vector product
If $\vec{a} = \hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$, $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1$ and $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \hat{j} - \hat{k}$, then find $|\vec{b}|$.
--- ans
Let $\vec{b} = b_1\hat{i} + b_2\hat{j} + b_3\hat{k}$.
$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1 \Rightarrow b_1 + b_2 + b_3 = 1 \quad\cdots(1)$
$$\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{vmatrix} = (b_3 - b_2)\hat{i} - (b_3 - b_1)\hat{j} + (b_2 - b_1)\hat{k} = \hat{j} - \hat{k}$$
Comparing: $b_3 - b_2 = 0$, $b_1 - b_3 = 1$, $b_2 - b_1 = -1$, i.e. $b_3 = b_2$ and $b_1 = b_2 + 1$.
From (1): $(b_2 + 1) + b_2 + b_2 = 1 \Rightarrow b_2 = 0$, so $b_1 = 1$, $b_3 = 0$.
$\therefore \vec{b} = \hat{i}$ and $|\vec{b}| = 1$.

=== 5 | 3d | 2 | sub
@topic Direction cosines
If a line makes an angle $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ with the coordinate axes, then find the value of $\cos 2\alpha + \cos 2\beta + \cos 2\gamma$.
--- ans
Since $\cos\alpha, \cos\beta, \cos\gamma$ are the direction cosines of the line, $\cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta + \cos^2\gamma = 1$.
$$\cos 2\alpha + \cos 2\beta + \cos 2\gamma = (2\cos^2\alpha - 1) + (2\cos^2\beta - 1) + (2\cos^2\gamma - 1)$$
$$= 2\left(\cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta + \cos^2\gamma\right) - 3 = 2(1) - 3 = -1$$

=== 6a | prob | 2 | sub
@topic Independent events
Events A and B are such that $P(A) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $P(B) = \dfrac{7}{12}$ and $P(\bar{A} \cup \bar{B}) = \dfrac{1}{4}$. Find whether the events A and B are independent or not.
--- ans
$$P(\bar{A} \cup \bar{B}) = P\left(\overline{A \cap B}\right) = 1 - P(A \cap B) = \frac{1}{4} \Rightarrow P(A \cap B) = \frac{3}{4}$$
$$P(A)\cdot P(B) = \frac{1}{2}\times\frac{7}{12} = \frac{7}{24}$$
Since $P(A \cap B) = \dfrac{3}{4} \ne \dfrac{7}{24} = P(A)P(B)$, **A and B are not independent**.
(Note: the given data are actually inconsistent, since $P(A \cap B)$ cannot exceed $P(A) = \frac{1}{2}$; the intended answer is "not independent".)

=== 6b | prob | 2 | sub
@topic Probability of independent events
A box $B_1$ contains 1 white ball and 3 red balls. Another box $B_2$ contains 2 white balls and 3 red balls. If one ball is drawn at random from each of the boxes $B_1$ and $B_2$, then find the probability that the two balls drawn are of the same colour.
--- ans
The draws from the two boxes are independent.
$$P(\text{both white}) = \frac{1}{4}\times\frac{2}{5} = \frac{2}{20},\qquad P(\text{both red}) = \frac{3}{4}\times\frac{3}{5} = \frac{9}{20}$$
$$P(\text{same colour}) = \frac{2}{20} + \frac{9}{20} = \frac{11}{20}$$

=== 7 | int | 3 | sub
@topic Evaluation of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{dx}{1 + \tan x}$
--- ans
$$I = \int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{\cos x}{\cos x + \sin x}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{(\cos x + \sin x) + (\cos x - \sin x)}{\cos x + \sin x}\,dx$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi/4} 1\,dx + \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{\cos x - \sin x}{\cos x + \sin x}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\left(I_1 + I_2\right)$$
$I_1 = \Big[x\Big]_0^{\pi/4} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
For $I_2$ put $\cos x + \sin x = t \Rightarrow (\cos x - \sin x)\,dx = dt$; $x = 0 \Rightarrow t = 1$, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} \Rightarrow t = \sqrt{2}$.
$$I_2 = \int_1^{\sqrt{2}} \frac{dt}{t} = \Big[\log t\Big]_1^{\sqrt{2}} = \log\sqrt{2} = \frac{1}{2}\log 2$$
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\log 2\right) = \frac{\pi}{8} + \frac{1}{4}\log 2$$

=== 8a | vec | 3 | sub
@topic Scalar product – perpendicular vectors
If $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are two vectors such that $|\vec{a} + \vec{b}| = |\vec{b}|$, then prove that $(\vec{a} + 2\vec{b})$ is perpendicular to $\vec{a}$.
--- ans
$$|\vec{a} + \vec{b}| = |\vec{b}| \Rightarrow |\vec{a} + \vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{b}|^2$$
$$\Rightarrow (\vec{a} + \vec{b}) \cdot (\vec{a} + \vec{b}) = |\vec{b}|^2 \Rightarrow |\vec{a}|^2 + 2\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + |\vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{b}|^2$$
$$\Rightarrow \vec{a} \cdot \vec{a} + 2\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0 \Rightarrow \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}) = 0$$
Since the dot product of $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}$ is zero, $(\vec{a} + 2\vec{b})$ is perpendicular to $\vec{a}$. Hence proved.

=== 8b | vec | 3 | sub
@topic Scalar product – angle between unit vectors
If $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are unit vectors and $\theta$ is the angle between them, then prove that $\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}|\vec{a} - \vec{b}|$.
--- ans
Given $|\vec{a}| = |\vec{b}| = 1$, so $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta = \cos\theta$.
$$|\vec{a} - \vec{b}|^2 = (\vec{a} - \vec{b}) \cdot (\vec{a} - \vec{b}) = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 - 2\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 2 - 2\cos\theta$$
$$= 2(1 - \cos\theta) = 2\cdot 2\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2} = 4\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}$$
Since $0 \le \theta \le \pi$, $\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2} \ge 0$, so taking square roots:
$$|\vec{a} - \vec{b}| = 2\sin\frac{\theta}{2} \Rightarrow \sin\frac{\theta}{2} = \frac{1}{2}|\vec{a} - \vec{b}|$$
Hence proved.

@topic Integration by parts (e^x sin bx type)
Find: $\displaystyle\int e^x \sin 2x\,dx$
--- ans
Let $I = \displaystyle\int e^x\sin 2x\,dx$. Integrating by parts with $\sin 2x$ as the first function:
$$I = \sin 2x\cdot e^x - \int 2\cos 2x\cdot e^x\,dx$$
Again by parts with $\cos 2x$ as the first function:
$$I = e^x\sin 2x - 2\left[\cos 2x\cdot e^x - \int(-2\sin 2x)e^x\,dx\right] = e^x\sin 2x - 2e^x\cos 2x - 4I$$
$$\Rightarrow 5I = e^x(\sin 2x - 2\cos 2x)$$
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{e^x}{5}\left(\sin 2x - 2\cos 2x\right) + C$$

=== 10b | int | 3 | sub
@topic Substitution and partial fractions
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{2x}{(x^2 + 1)(x^2 + 2)}\,dx$
--- ans
Put $x^2 = t \Rightarrow 2x\,dx = dt$:
$$I = \int \frac{dt}{(t + 1)(t + 2)}$$
Let $\dfrac{1}{(t + 1)(t + 2)} = \dfrac{A}{t + 1} + \dfrac{B}{t + 2} \Rightarrow 1 = A(t + 2) + B(t + 1)$.
$t = -1$: $A = 1$; $\quad t = -2$: $B = -1$.
$$I = \int \left(\frac{1}{t + 1} - \frac{1}{t + 2}\right)dt = \log|t + 1| - \log|t + 2| + C$$
$$\therefore\ I = \log\left|\frac{x^2 + 1}{x^2 + 2}\right| + C$$

=== 11 | prob | 4 | sub
@topic Bayes' theorem
Three persons A, B and C apply for a job a manager in a private company. Chances of their selection are in the ratio $1 : 2 : 4$. The probability that A, B and C can introduce chances to increase the profits of a company are $0.8$, $0.5$ and $0.3$ respectively. If increase in the profit does not take place, find the probability that it is due to the appointment of A.
--- ans
Let $E_1, E_2, E_3$: A, B, C respectively gets the job; $E$: the profit does **not** increase.
$$P(E_1) = \frac{1}{7},\quad P(E_2) = \frac{2}{7},\quad P(E_3) = \frac{4}{7}$$
$$P(E|E_1) = 1 - 0.8 = 0.2,\quad P(E|E_2) = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5,\quad P(E|E_3) = 1 - 0.3 = 0.7$$
By Bayes' theorem:
$$P(E_1|E) = \frac{P(E_1)P(E|E_1)}{P(E_1)P(E|E_1) + P(E_2)P(E|E_2) + P(E_3)P(E|E_3)}$$
$$= \frac{\frac{1}{7}(0.2)}{\frac{1}{7}(0.2) + \frac{2}{7}(0.5) + \frac{4}{7}(0.7)} = \frac{0.2}{0.2 + 1.0 + 2.8} = \frac{0.2}{4} = \frac{1}{20}$$
Required probability $= \dfrac{1}{20}$.

=== 12 | aoi | 4 | sub
@topic Area bounded by lines (modulus function)
Find the area bounded by the curve $y = |x - 1|$ and $y = 1$, using integration.
--- ans
$y = |x - 1| = \begin{cases} 1 - x, & x < 1 \\ x - 1, & x \ge 1 \end{cases}$
Points of intersection with $y = 1$: $1 - x = 1 \Rightarrow x = 0$ and $x - 1 = 1 \Rightarrow x = 2$, i.e. $(0, 1)$ and $(2, 1)$. The vertex of the curve is $(1, 0)$.

```plot

```

The required region lies between $y = 1$ (above) and $y = |x - 1|$ (below) for $0 \le x \le 2$:
$$A = \int_0^2 \left(1 - |x - 1|\right)dx = \int_0^1 \big(1 - (1 - x)\big)dx + \int_1^2 \big(1 - (x - 1)\big)dx$$
$$= \int_0^1 x\,dx + \int_1^2 (2 - x)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1 + \left[2x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_1^2$$
$$= \frac{1}{2} + \left[(4 - 2) - \left(2 - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1$$
Required area $= 1$ sq. unit.

=== 13a | de | 4 | sub
@topic Linear differential equation
Solve the following differential equation:
$$(y - \sin^2 x)\,dx + \tan x\,dy = 0$$
--- ans
$$\tan x\frac{dy}{dx} = \sin^2 x - y \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} + (\cot x)\,y = \frac{\sin^2 x}{\tan x} = \sin x\cos x$$
This is linear of the form $\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Q$ with $P = \cot x$, $Q = \sin x\cos x$.
$$\text{I.F.} = e^{\int \cot x\,dx} = e^{\log|\sin x|} = \sin x$$
The solution is $y\cdot\text{I.F.} = \displaystyle\int Q\cdot\text{I.F.}\,dx + C$:
$$y\sin x = \int \sin^2 x\cos x\,dx + C$$
Put $\sin x = t$, $\cos x\,dx = dt$: $\displaystyle\int t^2\,dt = \frac{t^3}{3} = \frac{\sin^3 x}{3}$.
$$\therefore\ y\sin x = \frac{\sin^3 x}{3} + C$$
is the general solution.

=== 13b | de | 4 | sub
@topic Homogeneous differential equation
Find the general solution of the differential equation: $(x^3 + y^3)\,dy = x^2y\,dx$
--- ans
$$\frac{dx}{dy} = \frac{x^3 + y^3}{x^2y}$$
— a homogeneous equation. Put $x = vy \Rightarrow \dfrac{dx}{dy} = v + y\dfrac{dv}{dy}$:
$$v + y\frac{dv}{dy} = \frac{v^3y^3 + y^3}{v^2y^3} = \frac{v^3 + 1}{v^2} = v + \frac{1}{v^2}$$
$$\Rightarrow y\frac{dv}{dy} = \frac{1}{v^2} \Rightarrow v^2\,dv = \frac{dy}{y}$$
Integrating: $\dfrac{v^3}{3} = \log|y| + C$.
Putting $v = \dfrac{x}{y}$:
$$\frac{x^3}{3y^3} = \log|y| + C$$
is the general solution.

=== 14 | 3d | 4 | case
@topic Shortest distance and intersection of lines – case study
@fig 2022-65-5-1-q14.webp
Two motorcycles A and B are running at the speed more than the allowed speed on the roads represented by the lines $\vec{r} = \lambda(\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - \hat{k})$ and $\vec{r} = (3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}) + \mu(2\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k})$ respectively.

Based on the above information, answer the following questions:
(a) Find the shortest distance between the given lines. **[2]**
(b) Find the point at which the motorcycles may collide. **[2]**
--- ans
**(a)** Here $\vec{a}_1 = \vec{0}$, $\vec{b}_1 = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - \hat{k}$, $\vec{a}_2 = 3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}$, $\vec{b}_2 = 2\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$.
$$\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2 = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = (2 + 1)\hat{i} - (1 + 2)\hat{j} + (1 - 4)\hat{k} = 3\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$$
$|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2| = \sqrt{9 + 9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{3}$, $\quad \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1 = 3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}$
$$d = \frac{\left|(\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \cdot (\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2)\right|}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} = \frac{|9 - 9 + 0|}{3\sqrt{3}} = 0$$
The shortest distance between the lines is $0$ (the lines intersect).

**(b)** At the common point,
$$\lambda\hat{i} + 2\lambda\hat{j} - \lambda\hat{k} = (3 + 2\mu)\hat{i} + (3 + \mu)\hat{j} + \mu\hat{k}$$
$\Rightarrow \lambda = 3 + 2\mu$, $\ 2\lambda = 3 + \mu$, $\ -\lambda = \mu$.
From the first and third: $\lambda = 3 - 2\lambda \Rightarrow \lambda = 1$, $\mu = -1$; check in the second: $2 = 3 - 1$ ✓.
Putting $\lambda = 1$: the motorcycles may collide at the point $\boxed{(1, 2, -1)}$.
