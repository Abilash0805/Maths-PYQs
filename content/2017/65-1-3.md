@topic Derivatives of inverse trigonometric functions
If $y = \sin^{-1}\left(6x\sqrt{1 - 9x^2}\right)$, $-\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt{2}} < x < \dfrac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}$, then find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
--- ans
Put $3x = \sin\theta$, i.e. $\theta = \sin^{-1}(3x)$. Since $-\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt{2}} < x < \dfrac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}$, we have $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} < \sin\theta < \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, so $-\dfrac{\pi}{4} < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\cos\theta > 0$.
$$y = \sin^{-1}\left(2\sin\theta\sqrt{1 - \sin^2\theta}\right) = \sin^{-1}(2\sin\theta\cos\theta) = \sin^{-1}(\sin 2\theta)$$
As $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} < 2\theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$: $\ y = 2\theta = 2\sin^{-1}(3x)$.
$$\therefore\ \frac{dy}{dx} = 2\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 9x^2}}\cdot 3 = \frac{6}{\sqrt{1 - 9x^2}}$$

@topic Graphical method – bounded region
Solve the following L.P.P. graphically :
Maximise $Z = 20x + 10y$
Subject to the following constraints $x + 2y \le 28$,
$3x + y \le 24$,
$x \ge 2$,
$x, y \ge 0$
--- ans
Draw the lines $x + 2y = 28$ (through $(28, 0)$, $(0, 14)$), $3x + y = 24$ (through $(8, 0)$, $(0, 24)$) and $x = 2$.
* $x + 2y \le 28$ and $3x + y \le 24$: origin side of the lines;
* $x \ge 2$: right of the line $x = 2$; $y \ge 0$: above the $x$-axis.

Intersections: $x + 2y = 28$ and $3x + y = 24$ give $C(4, 12)$; $x = 2$ and $x + 2y = 28$ give $D(2, 13)$.

@topic Homogeneous equations
Show that the family of curves for which $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{2xy}$, is given by $x^2 - y^2 = cx$.
--- ans
The right side is a homogeneous function of degree 0, so put $y = vx$, $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = v + x\dfrac{dv}{dx}$:
$$v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{1 + v^2}{2v} \Rightarrow x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{1 + v^2 - 2v^2}{2v} = \frac{1 - v^2}{2v}$$
Separating the variables:
$$\int \frac{2v}{1 - v^2}\,dv = \int \frac{dx}{x} \Rightarrow -\log|1 - v^2| = \log|x| + \log k$$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{1}{1 - v^2} = kx \Rightarrow \frac{x^2}{x^2 - y^2} = kx \quad\left(v = \frac{y}{x}\right)$$
$$\Rightarrow x^2 - y^2 = \frac{1}{k}\,x = cx, \quad\text{where } c = \frac{1}{k}$$
Hence the family of curves is $x^2 - y^2 = cx$.

@topic Partial fractions
Find : $\displaystyle\int \frac{(3\sin x - 2)\cos x}{13 - \cos^2 x - 7\sin x}\,dx$
--- ans
Put $\sin x = t \Rightarrow \cos x\,dx = dt$. The denominator becomes $13 - (1 - t^2) - 7t = t^2 - 7t + 12 = (t - 3)(t - 4)$.
$$I = \int \frac{3t - 2}{(t - 3)(t - 4)}\,dt$$
Let $\dfrac{3t - 2}{(t - 3)(t - 4)} = \dfrac{A}{t - 3} + \dfrac{B}{t - 4}$, so $3t - 2 = A(t - 4) + B(t - 3)$.
$t = 3$: $7 = -A \Rightarrow A = -7$; $\quad t = 4$: $10 = B$.
$$I = -7\int \frac{dt}{t - 3} + 10\int \frac{dt}{t - 4} = -7\log|t - 3| + 10\log|t - 4| + C$$
$$\therefore\ I = 10\log|\sin x - 4| - 7\log|\sin x - 3| + C$$
(equivalently $10\log(4 - \sin x) - 7\log(3 - \sin x) + C$).

@topic Simplification
Solve the following equation for $x$ :
$\cos(\tan^{-1}x) = \sin\left(\cot^{-1}\dfrac{3}{4}\right)$
--- ans
Let $\tan^{-1}x = \theta$, so $\tan\theta = x$ with $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$; then $\cos\theta > 0$ and
$$\cos(\tan^{-1}x) = \cos\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \tan^2\theta}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2}}$$
Let $\cot^{-1}\dfrac{3}{4} = \phi$, so $\cot\phi = \dfrac{3}{4}$ with $0 < \phi < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$; then
$$\sin\left(\cot^{-1}\frac{3}{4}\right) = \sin\phi = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \cot^2\phi}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{9}{16}}} = \frac{4}{5}$$
The equation becomes
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2}} = \frac{4}{5} \Rightarrow 1 + x^2 = \frac{25}{16} \Rightarrow x^2 = \frac{9}{16}$$
$$\therefore\ x = \pm\frac{3}{4}$$

@topic Systems of linear equations
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \\ -3 & 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$, find $A^{-1}$ and hence solve the system of equations $2x + y - 3z = 13$, $3x + 2y + z = 4$, $x + 2y - z = 8$.
--- ans
$$|A| = 2(-2 - 2) - 3(-1 + 6) + 1(1 + 6) = -8 - 15 + 7 = -16 \ne 0$$
so $A^{-1}$ exists. Cofactors:
$A_{11} = -4,\ A_{12} = -5,\ A_{13} = 7$
$A_{21} = 4,\ A_{22} = 1,\ A_{23} = -11$
$A_{31} = 4,\ A_{32} = -3,\ A_{33} = 1$
$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A = -\frac{1}{16}\begin{bmatrix} -4 & 4 & 4 \\ -5 & 1 & -3 \\ 7 & -11 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
The given system in matrix form:
$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & -3 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 13 \\ 4 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix}, \quad\text{i.e. } A'X = B$$
$$\Rightarrow X = (A')^{-1}B = (A^{-1})'B$$
$$\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{16}\begin{bmatrix} -4 & -5 & 7 \\ 4 & 1 & -11 \\ 4 & -3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 13 \\ 4 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{16}\begin{bmatrix} -52 - 20 + 56 \\ 52 + 4 - 88 \\ 52 - 12 + 8 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{16}\begin{bmatrix} -16 \\ -32 \\ 48 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$$
$\therefore\ \boxed{x = 1,\ y = 2,\ z = -3}$

=== 29 | de | 6 | sub
@topic Linear differential equations
Find the particular solution of the differential equation
$\tan x\cdot\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x\tan x + x^2 - y$; $(\tan x \ne 0)$ given that $y = 0$ when $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
--- ans
Dividing by $\tan x$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} + y\cot x = 2x + x^2\cot x$$
This is linear with $P = \cot x$, $Q = 2x + x^2\cot x$.
I.F. $= e^{\int \cot x\,dx} = e^{\log\sin x} = \sin x$.
Solution:
$$y\sin x = \int (2x + x^2\cot x)\sin x\,dx = \int \left(2x\sin x + x^2\cos x\right)dx$$
Since $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^2\sin x\right) = 2x\sin x + x^2\cos x$:
$$y\sin x = x^2\sin x + C$$
When $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $y = 0$: $0 = \dfrac{\pi^2}{4} + C \Rightarrow C = -\dfrac{\pi^2}{4}$.
$\therefore$ The particular solution is
$$y\sin x = x^2\sin x - \frac{\pi^2}{4}, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad y = x^2 - \frac{\pi^2}{4}\operatorname{cosec}x$$
