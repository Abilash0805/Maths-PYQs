@topic Rate of change
The radius r of the base of a right circular cone is decreasing at the rate of 2 cm/min. and its height h is increasing at the rate of 3 cm/min. When $r = 3.5$ cm and $h = 6$ cm, find the rate of change of the volume of the cone. [Use $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$]
--- ans
Given $\dfrac{dr}{dt} = -2$ cm/min and $\dfrac{dh}{dt} = 3$ cm/min.
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \Rightarrow \frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{\pi}{3}\left(2rh\frac{dr}{dt} + r^2\frac{dh}{dt}\right)$$
At $r = 3.5$, $h = 6$:
$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{22}{21}\Big[2(3.5)(6)(-2) + (3.5)^2(3)\Big] = \frac{22}{21}(-84 + 36.75) = \frac{22}{21} \times (-47.25) = -49.5$$
$\therefore$ The volume of the cone is **decreasing** at the rate of $49.5\ \text{cm}^3/\text{min}$.

@topic Partial fractions
Find :
$$\int \frac{x\,dx}{(2 + x^2)(4 + x^4)}$$
--- ans
Put $x^2 = t \Rightarrow x\,dx = \dfrac{dt}{2}$:
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{dt}{(2 + t)(4 + t^2)}$$
Let $\dfrac{1}{(2 + t)(4 + t^2)} = \dfrac{A}{2 + t} + \dfrac{Bt + C}{4 + t^2}$, so $1 = A(4 + t^2) + (Bt + C)(2 + t)$.
$t = -2$: $8A = 1 \Rightarrow A = \dfrac{1}{8}$; $\quad$ coefficient of $t^2$: $A + B = 0 \Rightarrow B = -\dfrac{1}{8}$; $\quad$ constant term: $4A + 2C = 1 \Rightarrow C = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{8}\int \frac{dt}{2 + t} - \frac{1}{8}\int \frac{t\,dt}{4 + t^2} + \frac{1}{4}\int \frac{dt}{4 + t^2}\right]$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{8}\log|2 + t| - \frac{1}{16}\log\left(4 + t^2\right) + \frac{1}{8}\tan^{-1}\frac{t}{2}\right] + C$$
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{1}{16}\log\left(2 + x^2\right) - \frac{1}{32}\log\left(4 + x^4\right) + \frac{1}{16}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x^2}{2}\right) + C$$

Solve the following LPP graphically :
Minimise $Z = 3x + 9y$
subject to the constraints
$x + 3y \le 60$
$x + y \ge 10$
$x \le y$
$x \ge 0,\ y \ge 0.$
--- ans
Draw the lines $x + 3y = 60$ (through $(60, 0)$, $(0, 20)$), $x + y = 10$ (through $(10, 0)$, $(0, 10)$) and $x = y$ (through $(0, 0)$, $(15, 15)$).
* $x + 3y \le 60$: origin side of the line;
* $x + y \ge 10$: side away from the origin;
* $x \le y$: above the line $y = x$ (test point $(0, 1)$); $x \ge 0$: right of the $y$-axis.

Intersections: $x = y$ and $x + y = 10$ give $B(5, 5)$; $x = y$ and $x + 3y = 60$ give $C(15, 15)$; the $y$-axis meets $x + y = 10$ at $A(0, 10)$ and $x + 3y = 60$ at $D(0, 20)$.

=== 22 | vec | 4 | sub
@topic Vector product and area
Find the area of a parallelogram ABCD whose side AB and the diagonal DB are given by the vectors $5\hat{i} + 7\hat{k}$ and $2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$ respectively.
--- ans
By the triangle law, $\overrightarrow{AD} + \overrightarrow{DB} = \overrightarrow{AB}$, so
$$\overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{AB} - \overrightarrow{DB} = (5\hat{i} + 7\hat{k}) - (2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}) = 3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$$
Area of parallelogram $= \left|\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AD}\right|$.
$$\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AD} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 5 & 0 & 7 \\ 3 & -2 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = (0 + 14)\hat{i} - (20 - 21)\hat{j} + (-10 - 0)\hat{k} = 14\hat{i} + \hat{j} - 10\hat{k}$$
$$\text{Area} = \sqrt{14^2 + 1^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{297} = 3\sqrt{33}\ \text{sq. units}$$

=== 23 | itf | 4 | sub
@topic Simplification
Prove that :
$$\tan^{-1}2x + \tan^{-1}\frac{4x}{1 - 4x^2} = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{6x - 8x^3}{1 - 12x^2}\right);\quad |x| < \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}$$
--- ans
Put $2x = \tan\theta$, i.e. $\theta = \tan^{-1}2x$. Since $|x| < \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}$, $|\tan\theta| < \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, so $-\dfrac{\pi}{6} < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

$\dfrac{4x}{1 - 4x^2} = \dfrac{2\tan\theta}{1 - \tan^2\theta} = \tan 2\theta$, with $-\dfrac{\pi}{3} < 2\theta < \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so
$$\tan^{-1}\frac{4x}{1 - 4x^2} = \tan^{-1}(\tan 2\theta) = 2\theta$$
$\dfrac{6x - 8x^3}{1 - 12x^2} = \dfrac{3(2x) - (2x)^3}{1 - 3(2x)^2} = \dfrac{3\tan\theta - \tan^3\theta}{1 - 3\tan^2\theta} = \tan 3\theta$, with $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} < 3\theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so
$$\text{RHS} = \tan^{-1}(\tan 3\theta) = 3\theta$$
$$\text{LHS} = \theta + 2\theta = 3\theta = \text{RHS}$$
Hence proved.

@topic Systems of linear equations
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 2 & 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$, find $A^{-1}$ and hence solve the system of equations $x + 2y + 5z = 10$, $x - y - z = -2$ and $2x + 3y - z = -11$.
--- ans
$$|A| = 1(1 + 3) - 2(-1 + 2) + 5(3 + 2) = 4 - 2 + 25 = 27 \ne 0$$
so $A^{-1}$ exists. Cofactors:
$A_{11} = 4,\ A_{12} = -1,\ A_{13} = 5$
$A_{21} = 17,\ A_{22} = -11,\ A_{23} = 1$
$A_{31} = 3,\ A_{32} = 6,\ A_{33} = -3$
$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A = \frac{1}{27}\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 17 & 3 \\ -1 & -11 & 6 \\ 5 & 1 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$$
The given system is $AX = B$, where $X = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}$, $B = \begin{bmatrix} 10 \\ -2 \\ -11 \end{bmatrix}$.
$$X = A^{-1}B = \frac{1}{27}\begin{bmatrix} 40 - 34 - 33 \\ -10 + 22 - 66 \\ 50 - 2 + 33 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{27}\begin{bmatrix} -27 \\ -54 \\ 81 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ -2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$$
$\therefore\ \boxed{x = -1,\ y = -2,\ z = 3}$

=== 29 | de | 6 | sub
@topic Linear differential equations
Find the particular solution of the differential equation
$dy = \cos x\,(2 - y\operatorname{cosec}x)\,dx$, given that $y = 2$ when $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
--- ans
The equation can be written as
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2\cos x - y\cos x\operatorname{cosec}x \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} + y\cot x = 2\cos x$$
This is linear with $P = \cot x$, $Q = 2\cos x$.
I.F. $= e^{\int \cot x\,dx} = e^{\log\sin x} = \sin x$.
Solution:
$$y\sin x = \int 2\sin x\cos x\,dx = \int \sin 2x\,dx = -\frac{\cos 2x}{2} + C$$
When $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $y = 2$: $2 \cdot 1 = -\dfrac{\cos\pi}{2} + C = \dfrac{1}{2} + C \Rightarrow C = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
$$\therefore\ y\sin x = \frac{3 - \cos 2x}{2} = \frac{2 + 2\sin^2 x}{2} = 1 + \sin^2 x$$
$\therefore$ The particular solution is $\boxed{y = \sin x + \operatorname{cosec}x}$
