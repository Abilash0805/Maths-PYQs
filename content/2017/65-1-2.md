@topic Rate of change
For the curve $y = 5x - 2x^3$, if $x$ increases at the rate of 2 units/sec, then find the rate of change of the slope of the curve when $x = 3$.
--- ans
Slope of the curve: $m = \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 5 - 6x^2$.
Differentiating w.r.t. $t$:
$$\frac{dm}{dt} = -12x\,\frac{dx}{dt}$$
At $x = 3$, with $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 2$ units/sec:
$$\frac{dm}{dt} = -12 \times 3 \times 2 = -72$$
$\therefore$ The slope is decreasing at the rate of **72 units/sec** (rate of change $= -72$ units/sec).

@topic Random variables and probability distributions
The random variable X can take only the values 0, 1, 2, 3. Given that $P(2) = P(3) = p$ and $P(0) = 2P(1)$. If $\Sigma p_i x_i^2 = 2\Sigma p_i x_i$, find the value of p.
--- ans
Let $P(1) = q$, so $P(0) = 2q$. Since $\Sigma p_i = 1$: $2q + q + p + p = 1 \Rightarrow 3q + 2p = 1 \quad\cdots(1)$

| $x_i$ | $p_i$ | $p_ix_i$ | $p_ix_i^2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $2q$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $1$ | $q$ | $q$ | $q$ |
| $2$ | $p$ | $2p$ | $4p$ |
| $3$ | $p$ | $3p$ | $9p$ |
| **Total** | $1$ | $q + 5p$ | $q + 13p$ |

Given $\Sigma p_ix_i^2 = 2\Sigma p_ix_i$:
$$q + 13p = 2q + 10p \Rightarrow q = 3p$$
Substituting in (1): $9p + 2p = 1 \Rightarrow \boxed{p = \dfrac{1}{11}}$
(Then $P(0) = \frac{6}{11}$, $P(1) = \frac{3}{11}$, $P(2) = P(3) = \frac{1}{11}$, which add up to 1.)

@topic Vector product and area
Using vectors find the area of triangle ABC with vertices $A(1, 2, 3)$, $B(2, -1, 4)$ and $C(4, 5, -1)$.
--- ans
$$\overrightarrow{AB} = (2 - 1)\hat{i} + (-1 - 2)\hat{j} + (4 - 3)\hat{k} = \hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$$
$$\overrightarrow{AC} = (4 - 1)\hat{i} + (5 - 2)\hat{j} + (-1 - 3)\hat{k} = 3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$$
$$\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 1 & -3 & 1 \\ 3 & 3 & -4 \end{vmatrix} = (12 - 3)\hat{i} - (-4 - 3)\hat{j} + (3 + 9)\hat{k} = 9\hat{i} + 7\hat{j} + 12\hat{k}$$
$$\left|\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}\right| = \sqrt{81 + 49 + 144} = \sqrt{274}$$
$$\text{Area of } \triangle ABC = \frac{1}{2}\left|\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}\right| = \frac{\sqrt{274}}{2}\ \text{sq. units}$$

@topic Graphical method – bounded region
Solve the following L.P.P. graphically
Maximise $Z = 4x + y$
Subject to following constraints $x + y \le 50$,
$3x + y \le 90$,
$x \ge 10$
$x, y \ge 0$
--- ans
Draw the lines $x + y = 50$ (through $(50, 0)$, $(0, 50)$), $3x + y = 90$ (through $(30, 0)$, $(0, 90)$) and $x = 10$.
* $x + y \le 50$ and $3x + y \le 90$: origin side of the lines;
* $x \ge 10$: right of the line $x = 10$; $y \ge 0$: above the $x$-axis.

Intersections: $x + y = 50$ and $3x + y = 90$ give $C(20, 30)$; $x = 10$ and $x + y = 50$ give $D(10, 40)$.


The feasible region ABCD is bounded.

| Corner point | $Z = 4x + y$ |
|---|---|
| $A(10, 0)$ | $40$ |
| $B(30, 0)$ | $120$ ← maximum |

@topic Partial fractions
Find : $\displaystyle\int \frac{2x}{(x^2 + 1)(x^4 + 4)}\,dx$
--- ans
Put $x^2 = t \Rightarrow 2x\,dx = dt$:
$$I = \int \frac{dt}{(t + 1)(t^2 + 4)}$$
Let $\dfrac{1}{(t + 1)(t^2 + 4)} = \dfrac{A}{t + 1} + \dfrac{Bt + C}{t^2 + 4}$, so $1 = A(t^2 + 4) + (Bt + C)(t + 1)$.
$t = -1$: $5A = 1 \Rightarrow A = \dfrac{1}{5}$; $\quad$ coefficient of $t^2$: $A + B = 0 \Rightarrow B = -\dfrac{1}{5}$; $\quad$ constant term: $4A + C = 1 \Rightarrow C = \dfrac{1}{5}$.
$$I = \frac{1}{5}\int \frac{dt}{t + 1} - \frac{1}{5}\int \frac{t}{t^2 + 4}\,dt + \frac{1}{5}\int \frac{dt}{t^2 + 4}$$
$$= \frac{1}{5}\log|t + 1| - \frac{1}{10}\log\left(t^2 + 4\right) + \frac{1}{10}\tan^{-1}\frac{t}{2} + C$$
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{1}{5}\log\left(x^2 + 1\right) - \frac{1}{10}\log\left(x^4 + 4\right) + \frac{1}{10}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x^2}{2}\right) + C$$

@topic Optimisation problems
A metal box with a square base and vertical sides is to contain $1024\ \text{cm}^3$. The material for the top and bottom costs ₹ 5 per $\text{cm}^2$ and the material for the sides costs ₹ 2.50 per $\text{cm}^2$. Find the least cost of the box.
--- ans
Let the side of the square base be $x$ cm and the height be $h$ cm. Then
$$x^2h = 1024 \Rightarrow h = \frac{1024}{x^2}$$
Area of top and bottom $= 2x^2$; area of the four sides $= 4xh$.
Cost
$$C = 5(2x^2) + 2.5(4xh) = 10x^2 + 10x\cdot\frac{1024}{x^2} = 10x^2 + \frac{10240}{x}$$
$$\frac{dC}{dx} = 20x - \frac{10240}{x^2} = 0 \Rightarrow x^3 = 512 \Rightarrow x = 8$$
$$\frac{d^2C}{dx^2} = 20 + \frac{20480}{x^3} > 0 \quad\text{at } x = 8$$
So $C$ is minimum at $x = 8$ cm (then $h = \dfrac{1024}{64} = 16$ cm).
$$C_{\min} = 10(64) + \frac{10240}{8} = 640 + 1280 = 1920$$
$\therefore$ Least cost of the box $=$ **₹ 1920**.

@topic Systems of linear equations
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 10 \\ 4 & -6 & 5 \\ 6 & 9 & -20 \end{bmatrix}$, find $A^{-1}$. Using $A^{-1}$ solve the system of equations
$\dfrac{2}{x} + \dfrac{3}{y} + \dfrac{10}{z} = 2;\ \dfrac{4}{x} - \dfrac{6}{y} + \dfrac{5}{z} = 5;\ \dfrac{6}{x} + \dfrac{9}{y} - \dfrac{20}{z} = -4$
--- ans
$$|A| = 2(120 - 45) - 3(-80 - 30) + 10(36 + 36) = 150 + 330 + 720 = 1200 \ne 0$$
so $A^{-1}$ exists. Cofactors:
$A_{11} = 75,\ A_{12} = 110,\ A_{13} = 72$
$A_{21} = 150,\ A_{22} = -100,\ A_{23} = 0$
$A_{31} = 75,\ A_{32} = 30,\ A_{33} = -24$
$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A = \frac{1}{1200}\begin{bmatrix} 75 & 150 & 75 \\ 110 & -100 & 30 \\ 72 & 0 & -24 \end{bmatrix}$$
Put $\dfrac{1}{x} = u,\ \dfrac{1}{y} = v,\ \dfrac{1}{z} = w$. The system becomes $AX = B$ with $X = \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ w \end{bmatrix}$, $B = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \\ -4 \end{bmatrix}$.
$$X = A^{-1}B = \frac{1}{1200}\begin{bmatrix} 150 + 750 - 300 \\ 220 - 500 - 120 \\ 144 + 0 + 96 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{1200}\begin{bmatrix} 600 \\ -400 \\ 240 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/2 \\ -1/3 \\ 1/5 \end{bmatrix}$$
$\therefore\ \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{1}{2},\ \dfrac{1}{y} = -\dfrac{1}{3},\ \dfrac{1}{z} = \dfrac{1}{5}$, i.e. $\boxed{x = 2,\ y = -3,\ z = 5}$
