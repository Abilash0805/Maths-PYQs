@topic Evaluation of determinants
If $x \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\begin{vmatrix} x + 3 & -2 \\ -3x & 2x \end{vmatrix} = 8$, then find the value of $x$.
--- ans
$$\begin{vmatrix} x + 3 & -2 \\ -3x & 2x \end{vmatrix} = (x + 3)(2x) - (-2)(-3x) = 2x^2 + 6x - 6x = 2x^2$$
$2x^2 = 8 \Rightarrow x^2 = 4 \Rightarrow x = \pm 2$.
Since $x \in \mathbb{N}$, $\boxed{x = 2}$.

@topic Vector product and area
The two adjacent sides of a parallelogram are $2\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}$ and $2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$. Find the two unit vectors parallel to its diagonals. Using the diagonal vectors, find the area of the parallelogram.
--- ans
Let $\vec{a} = 2\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b} = 2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$. The diagonals are
$$\vec{d}_1 = \vec{a} + \vec{b} = 4\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 2\hat{k},\qquad \vec{d}_2 = \vec{b} - \vec{a} = 0\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} + 8\hat{k}$$
$|\vec{d}_1| = \sqrt{16 + 4 + 4} = 2\sqrt{6}$, $\quad |\vec{d}_2| = \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10$.
Unit vectors parallel to the diagonals:
$$\hat{d}_1 = \frac{4\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}}{2\sqrt{6}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{i} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{j} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{k},\qquad \hat{d}_2 = \frac{6\hat{j} + 8\hat{k}}{10} = \frac{3}{5}\hat{j} + \frac{4}{5}\hat{k}$$
(their negatives are also parallel to the diagonals).
$$\vec{d}_1 \times \vec{d}_2 = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 4 & -2 & -2 \\ 0 & 6 & 8 \end{vmatrix} = (-16 + 12)\hat{i} - (32 - 0)\hat{j} + (24 - 0)\hat{k} = -4\hat{i} - 32\hat{j} + 24\hat{k}$$
$|\vec{d}_1 \times \vec{d}_2| = \sqrt{16 + 1024 + 576} = \sqrt{1616} = 4\sqrt{101}$
Area of parallelogram $= \dfrac{1}{2}|\vec{d}_1 \times \vec{d}_2| = \boxed{2\sqrt{101}\ \text{sq. units}}$

=== 9a | prob | 4 | sub
@topic Mean of a random variable
In a game, a man wins ₹ 5 for getting a number greater then 4 and loses ₹ 1 otherwise, when a fair die is thrown. The man decided to throw a die thrice but to quit as and when he gets a number greater than 4. Find the expected value of the amount he wins/loses.
--- ans
In a throw, P(number greater than 4) $= P(\{5, 6\}) = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and P(otherwise) $= \dfrac{2}{3}$.
Let $X$ = amount (in ₹) he wins. The possible cases:
* success on the 1st throw: $X = 5$, probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$
* failure, then success: $X = -1 + 5 = 4$, probability $\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{9}$
* failure, failure, success: $X = -2 + 5 = 3$, probability $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{4}{27}$
* failure in all three throws: $X = -3$, probability $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^3 = \dfrac{8}{27}$

| $X$ | $5$ | $4$ | $3$ | $-3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $P(X)$ | $\frac{1}{3}$ | $\frac{2}{9}$ | $\frac{4}{27}$ | $\frac{8}{27}$ |

(Check: $\frac{9 + 6 + 4 + 8}{27} = 1$.)
$$E(X) = \sum XP(X) = \frac{5}{3} + \frac{8}{9} + \frac{12}{27} - \frac{24}{27} = \frac{45 + 24 + 12 - 24}{27} = \frac{57}{27} = \frac{19}{9}$$
$\therefore$ Expected value: he wins ₹ $\dfrac{19}{9}$, i.e. **₹ $2\frac{1}{9}$** (about ₹ 2.11).

=== 9b | prob | 4 | sub
@topic Total probability and Bayes' theorem
A bag contains 4 balls. Two balls are drawn at random (without replacement) and are found to be white. What is the probability that all balls in the bag are white?
--- ans
Since two white balls were drawn, the bag has at least 2 white balls. Assuming all compositions are equally likely, let
$E_1$: all 4 balls are white, $E_2$: 3 balls are white, $E_3$: 2 balls are white, and $A$: the two balls drawn are white.
$$P(E_1) = P(E_2) = P(E_3) = \frac{1}{3}$$
$$P(A|E_1) = 1,\quad P(A|E_2) = \frac{{}^3C_2}{{}^4C_2} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2},\quad P(A|E_3) = \frac{{}^2C_2}{{}^4C_2} = \frac{1}{6}$$
By Bayes' theorem:
$$P(E_1|A) = \frac{P(E_1)P(A|E_1)}{\sum_{i=1}^{3} P(E_i)P(A|E_i)} = \frac{\frac{1}{3}\cdot 1}{\frac{1}{3}\left(1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{6}\right)} = \frac{1}{\frac{5}{3}} = \frac{3}{5}$$
(Including the compositions with 0 or 1 white ball as further equally likely cases gives the same answer, since for them $P(A|E) = 0$.)

@topic Logarithmic differentiation
Differentiate $x^{\sin x} + (\sin x)^{\cos x}$ with respect to $x$.
--- ans
Let $y = u + v$, where $u = x^{\sin x}$ and $v = (\sin x)^{\cos x}$. Then $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{du}{dx} + \dfrac{dv}{dx}$.

$\log u = \sin x\log x$
$$\frac{1}{u}\frac{du}{dx} = \cos x\log x + \frac{\sin x}{x} \Rightarrow \frac{du}{dx} = x^{\sin x}\left(\cos x\log x + \frac{\sin x}{x}\right)$$
$\log v = \cos x\log(\sin x)$
$$\frac{1}{v}\frac{dv}{dx} = -\sin x\log(\sin x) + \cos x\cdot\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} \Rightarrow \frac{dv}{dx} = (\sin x)^{\cos x}\big(\cos x\cot x - \sin x\log(\sin x)\big)$$
$$\therefore\ \frac{dy}{dx} = x^{\sin x}\left(\cos x\log x + \frac{\sin x}{x}\right) + (\sin x)^{\cos x}\big(\cos x\cot x - \sin x\log(\sin x)\big)$$

@topic Second order derivatives
If $y = 2\cos(\log x) + 3\sin(\log x)$, prove that $x^2\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + x\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y = 0$.
--- ans
Differentiating w.r.t. $x$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2\sin(\log x) + 3\cos(\log x)}{x} \Rightarrow x\frac{dy}{dx} = -2\sin(\log x) + 3\cos(\log x)$$
Differentiating again w.r.t. $x$:
$$x\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2\cos(\log x) - 3\sin(\log x)}{x} = -\frac{y}{x}$$
Multiplying by $x$:
$$x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + x\frac{dy}{dx} + y = 0$$
Hence proved.

@topic Parametric differentiation
If $x = a\sin 2t\,(1 + \cos 2t)$ and $y = b\cos 2t\,(1 - \cos 2t)$, find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
--- ans
$x = a\sin 2t + a\sin 2t\cos 2t = a\sin 2t + \dfrac{a}{2}\sin 4t$
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = 2a\cos 2t + 2a\cos 4t$$
$y = b\cos 2t - b\cos^2 2t$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = -2b\sin 2t + 4b\cos 2t\sin 2t = -2b\sin 2t + 2b\sin 4t$$
At $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$: $\cos 2t = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$, $\cos 4t = \cos\pi = -1$, $\sin 2t = 1$, $\sin 4t = \sin\pi = 0$.
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = 0 - 2a = -2a,\qquad \frac{dy}{dt} = -2b + 0 = -2b$$
$$\therefore\ \left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{t = \frac{\pi}{4}} = \frac{-2b}{-2a} = \boxed{\frac{b}{a}}$$

@topic Partial fractions
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{x^2}{x^4 + x^2 - 2}\,dx$
--- ans
$x^4 + x^2 - 2 = (x^2 - 1)(x^2 + 2)$. Put $x^2 = t$ (only for finding partial fractions):
$$\frac{t}{(t - 1)(t + 2)} = \frac{A}{t - 1} + \frac{B}{t + 2} \Rightarrow t = A(t + 2) + B(t - 1)$$
$t = 1$: $A = \dfrac{1}{3}$; $\quad t = -2$: $B = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{1}{3}\int \frac{dx}{x^2 - 1} + \frac{2}{3}\int \frac{dx}{x^2 + (\sqrt{2})^2}$$
$$= \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\log\left|\frac{x - 1}{x + 1}\right| + \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}} + C$$
$$= \frac{1}{6}\log\left|\frac{x - 1}{x + 1}\right| + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}} + C$$

--- ans
$$I = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^2 x}{\sin x + \cos x}\,dx \quad\cdots(1)$$

@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_0^{3/2} |x\cos \pi x|\,dx$
--- ans
For $0 \le x \le \dfrac{1}{2}$: $\cos\pi x \ge 0$, so $x\cos\pi x \ge 0$; for $\dfrac{1}{2} \le x \le \dfrac{3}{2}$: $\cos\pi x \le 0$, so $x\cos\pi x \le 0$.
$$I = \int_0^{1/2} x\cos\pi x\,dx - \int_{1/2}^{3/2} x\cos\pi x\,dx$$
Integrating by parts: $\displaystyle\int x\cos\pi x\,dx = \frac{x\sin\pi x}{\pi} - \int \frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi}\,dx = \frac{x\sin\pi x}{\pi} + \frac{\cos\pi x}{\pi^2} = F(x)$ (say).
$F(0) = \dfrac{1}{\pi^2}$, $\quad F\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2\pi}$, $\quad F\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right) = \dfrac{3}{2}\cdot\dfrac{(-1)}{\pi} + 0 = -\dfrac{3}{2\pi}$
$$I = \left[\frac{1}{2\pi} - \frac{1}{\pi^2}\right] - \left[-\frac{3}{2\pi} - \frac{1}{2\pi}\right] = \frac{1}{2\pi} - \frac{1}{\pi^2} + \frac{2}{\pi}$$
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{5}{2\pi} - \frac{1}{\pi^2}$$

=== 16 | de | 4 | sub
@topic Homogeneous equations
Solve the differential equation:
$$y + x\frac{dy}{dx} = x - y\frac{dy}{dx}$$
--- ans
$(x + y)\dfrac{dy}{dx} = x - y \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{x - y}{x + y}$, a homogeneous equation.
Put $y = vx$, $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = v + x\dfrac{dv}{dx}$:
$$v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{1 - v}{1 + v} \Rightarrow x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{1 - 2v - v^2}{1 + v}$$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{1 + v}{1 - 2v - v^2}\,dv = \frac{dx}{x}$$
Since $\dfrac{d}{dv}(1 - 2v - v^2) = -2(1 + v)$, integrating gives
$$-\frac{1}{2}\log\left|1 - 2v - v^2\right| = \log|x| + \log C_1$$
$$\Rightarrow \log\left|1 - 2v - v^2\right| + \log x^2 = \log C \Rightarrow x^2\left(1 - \frac{2y}{x} - \frac{y^2}{x^2}\right) = \pm C$$
$\therefore$ The general solution is
$$x^2 - 2xy - y^2 = C$$
($C$ an arbitrary constant).

@topic Simplification
If $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{x}{a} + \cos^{-1}\dfrac{y}{b} = \alpha$, prove that
$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} - 2\frac{xy}{ab}\cos\alpha + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = \sin^2\alpha$$
--- ans
Let $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{x}{a} = A$ and $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{y}{b} = B$, so $\cos A = \dfrac{x}{a}$, $\cos B = \dfrac{y}{b}$ and $A + B = \alpha$.
Since $A, B \in [0, \pi]$, $\sin A = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{x^2}{a^2}} \ge 0$ and $\sin B = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2}} \ge 0$.
$$\cos\alpha = \cos(A + B) = \cos A\cos B - \sin A\sin B = \frac{xy}{ab} - \sqrt{1 - \frac{x^2}{a^2}}\sqrt{1 - \frac{y^2}{b^2}}$$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{xy}{ab} - \cos\alpha = \sqrt{\left(1 - \frac{x^2}{a^2}\right)\left(1 - \frac{y^2}{b^2}\right)}$$
Squaring both sides:
$$\frac{x^2y^2}{a^2b^2} - 2\frac{xy}{ab}\cos\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = 1 - \frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{x^2y^2}{a^2b^2}$$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{x^2}{a^2} - 2\frac{xy}{ab}\cos\alpha + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 - \cos^2\alpha = \sin^2\alpha$$
Hence proved.

A trust invested some money in two type of bonds. The first bond pays 10% interest and second bond pays 12% interest. The trust received ₹ 2,800 as interest. However, if trust had interchanged money in bonds, they would have got ₹ 100 less as interest. Using matrix method, find the amount invested by the trust. Interest received on this amount will be given to Helpage India as donation. Which value is reflected in the question?
--- ans
Let ₹ $x$ be invested in the first bond and ₹ $y$ in the second bond.
$$\frac{10x}{100} + \frac{12y}{100} = 2800 \Rightarrow 5x + 6y = 140000$$
$$\frac{12x}{100} + \frac{10y}{100} = 2700 \Rightarrow 6x + 5y = 135000$$
In matrix form $AX = B$: $\begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 6 & 5 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 140000 \\ 135000 \end{bmatrix}$.
$|A| = 25 - 36 = -11 \ne 0$, so $A^{-1}$ exists.
$$\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -6 \\ -6 & 5 \end{bmatrix},\qquad A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A = -\frac{1}{11}\begin{bmatrix} 5 & -6 \\ -6 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$$

@topic Optimisation problems
Prove that the least perimeter of an isosceles triangle in which a circle of radius $r$ can be inscribed is $6\sqrt{3}\,r$.
--- ans
Let $\triangle ABC$ be isosceles with $AB = AC$, and let its incircle (centre O, radius $r$) touch $AB$, $AC$, $BC$ at $E$, $F$, $D$. Then $D$ is the mid-point of $BC$ and $A, O, D$ are collinear.
Tangents from a point are equal: let $AE = AF = x$ and $BE = BD = DC = CF = y$.
So $AB = AC = x + y$, $BC = 2y$, and $AD = \sqrt{(x + y)^2 - y^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + 2xy}$.

Area $= r \times$ (semi-perimeter) $= \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot BC\cdot AD$:
$$r(x + 2y) = y\sqrt{x(x + 2y)} \Rightarrow r^2(x + 2y)^2 = y^2x(x + 2y) \Rightarrow r^2(x + 2y) = xy^2$$
$$\Rightarrow x(y^2 - r^2) = 2r^2y \Rightarrow x = \frac{2r^2y}{y^2 - r^2}\quad (y > r)$$
Perimeter:
$$P = 2(x + y) + 2y = 2x + 4y = \frac{4r^2y}{y^2 - r^2} + 4y$$
$$\frac{dP}{dy} = 4r^2\cdot\frac{(y^2 - r^2) - 2y^2}{(y^2 - r^2)^2} + 4 = 4 - \frac{4r^2(y^2 + r^2)}{(y^2 - r^2)^2}$$
$\dfrac{dP}{dy} = 0 \Rightarrow (y^2 - r^2)^2 = r^2(y^2 + r^2) \Rightarrow y^4 - 3r^2y^2 = 0 \Rightarrow y^2 = 3r^2 \Rightarrow y = \sqrt{3}\,r$.
$$\frac{d^2P}{dy^2} = \frac{8r^2y(y^2 + 3r^2)}{(y^2 - r^2)^3};\qquad \text{at } y = \sqrt{3}\,r:\ \frac{8r^2\cdot\sqrt{3}r\cdot 6r^2}{8r^6} = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{r} > 0$$
So $P$ is minimum at $y = \sqrt{3}\,r$ (it is the only critical point for $y > r$, so this minimum is the least value). Then $x = \dfrac{2r^2\cdot\sqrt{3}r}{2r^2} = \sqrt{3}\,r$ and
$$P_{\min} = 2\sqrt{3}\,r + 4\sqrt{3}\,r = 6\sqrt{3}\,r$$
Hence proved. (All sides equal $2\sqrt{3}\,r$, i.e. the triangle is equilateral.)

@topic Optimisation problems
If the sum of lengths of hypotenuse and a side of a right angled triangle is given, show that the area of triangle is maximum, when the angle between them is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
--- ans
Let $ABC$ be right-angled at $B$, with side $BC = x$, hypotenuse $AC = h$ and $\angle ACB = \theta$ (the angle between them). Given $x + h = k$ (constant), so $h = k - x$.
$AB = \sqrt{h^2 - x^2}$, so the area is
$$A = \frac{1}{2}x\sqrt{h^2 - x^2} = \frac{1}{2}x\sqrt{(k - x)^2 - x^2} = \frac{1}{2}x\sqrt{k^2 - 2kx}$$
$A$ is maximum when $Z = A^2$ is maximum:
$$Z = \frac{1}{4}x^2(k^2 - 2kx) = \frac{1}{4}\left(k^2x^2 - 2kx^3\right)$$
$$\frac{dZ}{dx} = \frac{1}{4}\left(2k^2x - 6kx^2\right) = \frac{kx}{2}(k - 3x) = 0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{k}{3}\quad(x \ne 0)$$
$$\frac{d^2Z}{dx^2} = \frac{1}{4}\left(2k^2 - 12kx\right);\qquad \text{at } x = \frac{k}{3}:\ \frac{1}{4}\left(2k^2 - 4k^2\right) = -\frac{k^2}{2} < 0$$
So the area is maximum when $x = \dfrac{k}{3}$, i.e. $h = k - \dfrac{k}{3} = \dfrac{2k}{3}$.
Then $\cos\theta = \dfrac{x}{h} = \dfrac{k/3}{2k/3} = \dfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow \theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
Hence the area is maximum when the angle between the hypotenuse and the side is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

A shopkeeper has 3 varieties of pens 'A', 'B' and 'C'. Meenu purchased 1 pen of each variety for a total of ₹ 21. --- ans
Let the cost of one pen of variety A, B and C be ₹ $x$, ₹ $y$ and ₹ $z$ respectively.
$$x + y + z = 21,\qquad 4x + 3y + 2z = 60,\qquad 6x + 2y + 3z = 70$$
In matrix form $AX = B$, where
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 & 2 \\ 6 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix},\quad X = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix},\quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 21 \\ 60 \\ 70 \end{bmatrix}$$
$|A| = 1(9 - 4) - 1(12 - 12) + 1(8 - 18) = 5 - 0 - 10 = -5 \ne 0$, so $A^{-1}$ exists.
Cofactors:
$A_{11} = 5,\ A_{12} = 0,\ A_{13} = -10$
$A_{21} = -1,\ A_{22} = -3,\ A_{23} = 4$
$A_{31} = -1,\ A_{32} = 2,\ A_{33} = -1$
$$\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & -3 & 2 \\ -10 & 4 & -1 \end{bmatrix},\qquad A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A = -\frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} 5 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & -3 & 2 \\ -10 & 4 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$X = A^{-1}B = -\frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} 105 - 60 - 70 \\ 0 - 180 + 140 \\ -210 + 240 - 70 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} -25 \\ -40 \\ -40 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 8 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix}$$
