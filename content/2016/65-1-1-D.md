@topic Evaluation of determinants
Find the maximum value of $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 + \sin\theta & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 + \cos\theta \end{vmatrix}$.
--- ans
Expanding along $R_1$:
$$\Delta = 1\big[(1 + \sin\theta)(1 + \cos\theta) - 1\big] - 1\big[(1 + \cos\theta) - 1\big] + 1\big[1 - (1 + \sin\theta)\big]$$
$$= \sin\theta + \cos\theta + \sin\theta\cos\theta - \cos\theta - \sin\theta = \sin\theta\cos\theta = \frac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta$$
Since the maximum value of $\sin 2\theta$ is $1$, the maximum value of $\Delta$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.

=== 2 | mat | 1 | sub
@topic Operations on matrices
If $A$ is a square matrix such that $A^2 = I$, then find the simplified value of $(A - I)^3 + (A + I)^3 - 7A$.
--- ans
Since $A$ and $I$ commute, the binomial expansions hold:
$(A - I)^3 + (A + I)^3 = (A^3 - 3A^2 + 3A - I) + (A^3 + 3A^2 + 3A + I) = 2A^3 + 6A$.
$A^3 = A \cdot A^2 = A \cdot I = A$.
$\therefore\ (A - I)^3 + (A + I)^3 - 7A = 2A + 6A - 7A = \boxed{A}$.

@topic Systems of linear equations
The monthly incomes of Aryan and Babban are in the ratio $3 : 4$ and their monthly expenditures are in the ratio $5 : 7$. If each saves ₹ 15,000 per month, find their monthly incomes using matrix method. This problem reflects which value?
--- ans
Let the monthly incomes be ₹ $3x$ and ₹ $4x$, and the monthly expenditures be ₹ $5y$ and ₹ $7y$.
Savings = income − expenditure:
$$3x - 5y = 15000,\qquad 4x - 7y = 15000$$
In matrix form $AX = B$: $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -5 \\ 4 & -7 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 15000 \\ 15000 \end{bmatrix}$.
$|A| = -21 + 20 = -1 \ne 0$, so $A^{-1}$ exists.
$$\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} -7 & 5 \\ -4 & 3 \end{bmatrix},\qquad A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -5 \\ 4 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$X = A^{-1}B = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & -5 \\ 4 & -3 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 15000 \\ 15000 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 30000 \\ 15000 \end{bmatrix}$$
$\therefore\ x = 30000,\ y = 15000$.
Monthly income of Aryan $= 3x =$ **₹ 90,000** and of Babban $= 4x =$ **₹ 1,20,000**.
(Check: expenditures ₹ 75,000 and ₹ 1,05,000; each saves ₹ 15,000.)

**Value:** Saving regularly — spending less than one's income (thrift and financial prudence).

@topic Parametric differentiation
If $x = a\sin 2t\,(1 + \cos 2t)$ and $y = b\cos 2t\,(1 - \cos 2t)$, find the value of $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
--- ans
$x = a\sin 2t + a\sin 2t\cos 2t = a\sin 2t + \dfrac{a}{2}\sin 4t$
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = 2a\cos 2t + 2a\cos 4t = 2a(\cos 2t + \cos 4t)$$
$y = b\cos 2t - b\cos^2 2t$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = -2b\sin 2t + 2b\cos 2t\cdot 2\sin 2t = 2b(\sin 4t - \sin 2t)$$
$$\therefore\ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{b}{a}\cdot\frac{\sin 4t - \sin 2t}{\cos 4t + \cos 2t} = \frac{b}{a}\cdot\frac{2\cos 3t\sin t}{2\cos 3t\cos t} = \frac{b}{a}\tan t$$
At $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{b}{a}\tan\dfrac{\pi}{4} = \boxed{\dfrac{b}{a}}$
At $t = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{b}{a}\tan\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}\,b}{a}}$

=== 9b | cd | 4 | sub
@topic Second order derivatives
If $y = x^x$, prove that $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - \dfrac{1}{y}\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 - \dfrac{y}{x} = 0$.
--- ans
Taking logarithm: $\log y = x\log x$.
Differentiating w.r.t. $x$:
$$\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = \log x + 1$$
Differentiating again w.r.t. $x$:
$$\frac{1}{y}\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - \frac{1}{y^2}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{x}$$
Multiplying by $y$:
$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - \frac{1}{y}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 - \frac{y}{x} = 0$$
Hence proved.

@topic Continuity
Find the values of $p$ and $q$, for which
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1 - \sin^3 x}{3\cos^2 x}, & \text{if } x < \dfrac{\pi}{2} \\[2mm] p, & \text{if } x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} \\[2mm] \dfrac{q(1 - \sin x)}{(\pi - 2x)^2}, & \text{if } x > \dfrac{\pi}{2} \end{cases}$$
is continuous at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
--- ans
**LHL:**
$$\lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{2}^-} \frac{1 - \sin^3 x}{3\cos^2 x} = \lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{2}^-} \frac{(1 - \sin x)(1 + \sin x + \sin^2 x)}{3(1 - \sin x)(1 + \sin x)} = \lim_{x \to \frac{\pi}{2}^-} \frac{1 + \sin x + \sin^2 x}{3(1 + \sin x)} = \frac{3}{3 \cdot 2} = \frac{1}{2}$$
**RHL:** Put $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + h$, $h \to 0^+$. Then $1 - \sin x = 1 - \cos h = 2\sin^2\dfrac{h}{2}$ and $(\pi - 2x)^2 = 4h^2$.
$$\lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{q \cdot 2\sin^2\frac{h}{2}}{4h^2} = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{q}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\sin\frac{h}{2}}{\frac{h}{2}}\right)^2 = \frac{q}{8}$$
For continuity at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$: LHL $=$ RHL $= f\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$
$$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{q}{8} = p$$
$\therefore\ \boxed{p = \dfrac{1}{2},\ q = 4}$

@topic Partial fractions
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{(3\sin\theta - 2)\cos\theta}{5 - \cos^2\theta - 4\sin\theta}\,d\theta$
--- ans
$5 - \cos^2\theta - 4\sin\theta = 5 - (1 - \sin^2\theta) - 4\sin\theta = \sin^2\theta - 4\sin\theta + 4$.
Put $\sin\theta = t \Rightarrow \cos\theta\,d\theta = dt$:
$$I = \int \frac{3t - 2}{t^2 - 4t + 4}\,dt = \int \frac{3t - 2}{(t - 2)^2}\,dt$$
Writing $3t - 2 = 3(t - 2) + 4$:
$$I = 3\int \frac{dt}{t - 2} + 4\int \frac{dt}{(t - 2)^2} = 3\log|t - 2| - \frac{4}{t - 2} + C$$
$$\therefore\ I = 3\log|\sin\theta - 2| + \frac{4}{2 - \sin\theta} + C$$

=== 12b | int | 4 | sub
@topic Integration by parts
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} e^{2x}\cdot\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right)dx$
--- ans
Let $I = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right)e^{2x}\,dx$. Integrating by parts (taking the sine as the first function):
$$I = \left[\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right)\frac{e^{2x}}{2}\right]_0^{\pi} - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi} \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right)e^{2x}\,dx$$
Integrating by parts again:
$$I = \left[\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right)\frac{e^{2x}}{2}\right]_0^{\pi} - \frac{1}{2}\left\{\left[\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right)\frac{e^{2x}}{2}\right]_0^{\pi} + \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi} \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right)e^{2x}\,dx\right\}$$
$$\Rightarrow I + \frac{I}{4} = \left[\frac{e^{2x}}{4}\left\{2\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right) - \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4} + x\right)\right\}\right]_0^{\pi}$$
At $x = \pi$: $\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{4} = \cos\dfrac{5\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, so the bracket $= -\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.
At $x = 0$: the bracket $= \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2}} - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.
$$\frac{5I}{4} = \frac{1}{4}\left[-\frac{e^{2\pi}}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right] \Rightarrow I = -\frac{e^{2\pi} + 1}{5\sqrt{2}}$$

@topic Integration by substitution
Find: $\displaystyle\int \sqrt{\frac{x}{a^3 - x^3}}\,dx$
--- ans
$$I = \int \frac{\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{a^3 - x^3}}\,dx$$
Put $x^{3/2} = t \Rightarrow \dfrac{3}{2}x^{1/2}\,dx = dt \Rightarrow \sqrt{x}\,dx = \dfrac{2}{3}\,dt$. Also $x^3 = t^2$.
$$I = \frac{2}{3}\int \frac{dt}{\sqrt{\left(a^{3/2}\right)^2 - t^2}} = \frac{2}{3}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{t}{a^{3/2}}\right) + C$$
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{2}{3}\sin^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{x^3}{a^3}}\right) + C$$

@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} |x^3 - x|\,dx$
--- ans
$x^3 - x = x(x - 1)(x + 1)$: it is $\ge 0$ on $[-1, 0]$, $\le 0$ on $[0, 1]$ and $\ge 0$ on $[1, 2]$.
$$I = \int_{-1}^{0} (x^3 - x)\,dx + \int_{0}^{1} (x - x^3)\,dx + \int_{1}^{2} (x^3 - x)\,dx$$
$$= \left[\frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{0} + \left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_{0}^{1} + \left[\frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{1}^{2}$$
$$= \left(0 + \frac{1}{4}\right) + \frac{1}{4} + \left[(4 - 2) - \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$$

@topic Variable separable
Find the particular solution of the differential equation $(1 - y^2)(1 + \log x)\,dx + 2xy\,dy = 0$, given that $y = 0$ when $x = 1$.
--- ans
Dividing by $x(1 - y^2)$ to separate the variables:
$$\frac{1 + \log x}{x}\,dx + \frac{2y}{1 - y^2}\,dy = 0$$
Integrating (put $1 + \log x = u$ in the first and $1 - y^2 = v$ in the second):
$$\frac{(1 + \log x)^2}{2} - \log|1 - y^2| = C$$
When $x = 1$, $y = 0$: $\dfrac{1}{2} - \log 1 = C \Rightarrow C = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
$\therefore$ The particular solution is
$$(1 + \log x)^2 - 2\log|1 - y^2| = 1$$

@topic Linear differential equations
Find the general solution of the following differential equation:
$$(1 + y^2) + \left(x - e^{\tan^{-1}y}\right)\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$$
--- ans
The equation can be written as
$$\frac{dx}{dy} + \frac{1}{1 + y^2}\,x = \frac{e^{\tan^{-1}y}}{1 + y^2}$$
which is linear in $x$ with $P = \dfrac{1}{1 + y^2}$, $Q = \dfrac{e^{\tan^{-1}y}}{1 + y^2}$.
I.F. $= e^{\int \frac{dy}{1 + y^2}} = e^{\tan^{-1}y}$
Solution: $x \cdot e^{\tan^{-1}y} = \displaystyle\int \frac{e^{\tan^{-1}y}}{1 + y^2}\cdot e^{\tan^{-1}y}\,dy = \int \frac{e^{2\tan^{-1}y}}{1 + y^2}\,dy$
Put $\tan^{-1}y = t$, $\dfrac{dy}{1 + y^2} = dt$: $\displaystyle\int e^{2t}\,dt = \frac{1}{2}e^{2t}$.
$$\therefore\ x\,e^{\tan^{-1}y} = \frac{1}{2}e^{2\tan^{-1}y} + C$$
is the general solution.

@topic Total probability and Bayes' theorem
Three persons $A$, $B$ and $C$ apply for a job of Manager in a Private company. Chance of their selection ($A$, $B$ and $C$) are in the ratio $1 : 2 : 4$. The probabilities that $A$, $B$ and $C$ can introduce changes to improve profits of the company are $0.8$, $0.5$ and $0.3$ respectively. If the changes does not take place, find the probability that it is due to the appointment of $C$.
--- ans
Let $E_1, E_2, E_3$: $A$, $B$, $C$ respectively is selected; $E$: changes do not take place.
$$P(E_1) = \frac{1}{7},\quad P(E_2) = \frac{2}{7},\quad P(E_3) = \frac{4}{7}$$
$$P(E|E_1) = 1 - 0.8 = 0.2,\quad P(E|E_2) = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5,\quad P(E|E_3) = 1 - 0.3 = 0.7$$
By Bayes' theorem:
$$P(E_3|E) = \frac{P(E_3)P(E|E_3)}{\sum_{i=1}^{3} P(E_i)P(E|E_i)} = \frac{\frac{4}{7}(0.7)}{\frac{1}{7}(0.2) + \frac{2}{7}(0.5) + \frac{4}{7}(0.7)} = \frac{2.8}{0.2 + 1.0 + 2.8} = \frac{2.8}{4} = \frac{7}{10}$$

=== 19b | prob | 4 | sub
@topic Multiplication theorem and independent events
$A$ and $B$ throw a pair of dice alternately. $A$ wins the game if he gets a total of 7 and $B$ wins the game if he gets a total of 10. If $A$ starts the game, then find the probability that $B$ wins.
--- ans
Total of 7: $(1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1)$ $\Rightarrow$ P($A$ wins in a throw) $= \dfrac{6}{36} = \dfrac{1}{6}$, P($A$ fails) $= \dfrac{5}{6}$.
Total of 10: $(4,6), (5,5), (6,4)$ $\Rightarrow$ P($B$ wins in a throw) $= \dfrac{3}{36} = \dfrac{1}{12}$, P($B$ fails) $= \dfrac{11}{12}$.
$B$ can win in the 2nd, 4th, 6th, … throw of the game:
$$P(B \text{ wins}) = \frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{12} + \left(\frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{11}{12}\right)\frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{12} + \left(\frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{11}{12}\right)^2\frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{12} + \cdots$$
This is an infinite G.P. with first term $\dfrac{5}{72}$ and common ratio $\dfrac{55}{72}$:
$$P(B \text{ wins}) = \frac{\frac{5}{72}}{1 - \frac{55}{72}} = \frac{5}{72}\cdot\frac{72}{17} = \frac{5}{17}$$

@topic Optimisation problems
Show that the altitude of the right circular cone of maximum volume that can be inscribed in a sphere of radius $r$ is $\dfrac{4r}{3}$. Also find maximum volume in terms of volume of the sphere.
--- ans
Let O be the centre of the sphere, the vertex of the cone lie on the sphere, and the centre of the base of the cone be at distance $x$ from O (on the side away from the vertex), $0 \le x < r$.
Then the height of the cone is $h = r + x$ and the base radius $R$ satisfies $R^2 + x^2 = r^2$, i.e. $R^2 = r^2 - x^2$.
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi R^2 h = \frac{\pi}{3}(r^2 - x^2)(r + x)$$
$$\frac{dV}{dx} = \frac{\pi}{3}\left[-2x(r + x) + (r^2 - x^2)\right] = \frac{\pi}{3}(r + x)(r - x - 2x) = \frac{\pi}{3}(r + x)(r - 3x)$$
$\dfrac{dV}{dx} = 0 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{r}{3}$ (as $r + x > 0$).
$$\frac{d^2V}{dx^2} = \frac{\pi}{3}\left[(r - 3x) - 3(r + x)\right] = \frac{\pi}{3}(-2r - 6x) < 0$$
So $V$ is maximum when $x = \dfrac{r}{3}$, i.e. when the altitude $h = r + \dfrac{r}{3} = \dfrac{4r}{3}$. Hence shown.
Maximum volume:
$$V = \frac{\pi}{3}\left(r^2 - \frac{r^2}{9}\right)\frac{4r}{3} = \frac{\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{8r^2}{9}\cdot\frac{4r}{3} = \frac{32\pi r^3}{81} = \frac{8}{27}\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\right)$$
$\therefore$ Maximum volume of the cone $= \boxed{\dfrac{8}{27} \times \text{volume of the sphere}}$.

=== 22b | aod | 6 | sub
@topic Increasing and decreasing functions
Find the intervals in which $f(x) = \sin 3x - \cos 3x$, $0 < x < \pi$, is strictly increasing or strictly decreasing.
--- ans
$$f'(x) = 3\cos 3x + 3\sin 3x = 3\sqrt{2}\sin\left(3x + \frac{\pi}{4}\right)$$
$f'(x) = 0 \Rightarrow \tan 3x = -1 \Rightarrow 3x + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = n\pi$. For $0 < x < \pi$ (so $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < 3x + \dfrac{\pi}{4} < \dfrac{13\pi}{4}$):
$$3x + \frac{\pi}{4} = \pi, 2\pi, 3\pi \Rightarrow x = \frac{\pi}{4},\ \frac{7\pi}{12},\ \frac{11\pi}{12}$$
These points divide $(0, \pi)$ into four intervals:

| Interval | $3x + \frac{\pi}{4}$ lies in | Sign of $f'(x)$ | Nature of $f$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{4}\right)$ | $\left(\frac{\pi}{4}, \pi\right)$ | $+$ | strictly increasing |
| $\left(\frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{7\pi}{12}\right)$ | $(\pi, 2\pi)$ | $-$ | strictly decreasing |
| $\left(\frac{7\pi}{12}, \frac{11\pi}{12}\right)$ | $(2\pi, 3\pi)$ | $+$ | strictly increasing |
| $\left(\frac{11\pi}{12}, \pi\right)$ | $\left(3\pi, \frac{13\pi}{4}\right)$ | $-$ | strictly decreasing |

$\therefore$ $f$ is strictly increasing in $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) \cup \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{12}, \dfrac{11\pi}{12}\right)$ and strictly decreasing in $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}, \dfrac{7\pi}{12}\right) \cup \left(\dfrac{11\pi}{12}, \pi\right)$.
