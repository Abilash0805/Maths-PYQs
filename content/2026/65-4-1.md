**Assertion (A):** If A and B are two square matrices such that AB and BA are defined, then it is not necessary that $AB = BA$.
**Reason (R):** Product of two diagonal matrices of same order is commutative.
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of the Assertion (A).

@topic Differentiability
Find whether the function $f(x) = \begin{cases} x - 1, & x < 2 \\ 2x - 3, & x \ge 2 \end{cases}$ at $x = 2$ is differentiable or not.
--- ans
$f(2) = 2(2) - 3 = 1$.
$$\text{LHD} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(2 - h) - f(2)}{-h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(2 - h - 1) - 1}{-h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{-h}{-h} = 1$$
$$\text{RHD} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(2 + h) - f(2)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(4 + 2h - 3) - 1}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{2h}{h} = 2$$
Since LHD $\ne$ RHD, $f$ is **not differentiable at $x = 2$**.

Find the values of x for which $f(x) = x^x,\ x > 0$ is increasing.
--- ans

@topic One-one and onto functions
Let $A = \mathbb{R} - \{3\}$ and $B = \mathbb{R} - \{1\}$. A function $f : A \to B$ is defined by $f(x) = \left(\dfrac{x - 2}{x - 3}\right)$. Find whether $f$ is one-one and onto.
--- ans
**One-one:** Let $x_1, x_2 \in A$ with $f(x_1) = f(x_2)$.
$$\frac{x_1 - 2}{x_1 - 3} = \frac{x_2 - 2}{x_2 - 3} \Rightarrow x_1x_2 - 3x_1 - 2x_2 + 6 = x_1x_2 - 2x_1 - 3x_2 + 6 \Rightarrow -x_1 = -x_2 \Rightarrow x_1 = x_2$$
$\therefore f$ is one-one.
**Onto:** Let $y \in B$ ($y \ne 1$). Put $\dfrac{x - 2}{x - 3} = y \Rightarrow x - 2 = xy - 3y \Rightarrow x(1 - y) = 2 - 3y \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{3y - 2}{y - 1}$.
This $x$ is real since $y \ne 1$, and $x \ne 3$ (otherwise $3y - 2 = 3y - 3$, i.e. $-2 = -3$, impossible), so $x \in A$, and $f(x) = \dfrac{\frac{3y-2}{y-1} - 2}{\frac{3y-2}{y-1} - 3} = \dfrac{y}{1} = y$.
$\therefore$ every $y \in B$ has a pre-image; $f$ is onto.
Hence **$f$ is both one-one and onto** (bijective).

Since $R$ is reflexive, symmetric and transitive, **$R$ is an equivalence relation**.
