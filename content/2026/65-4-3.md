@topic Rate of change
A cylindrical tank is being filled with sand at a rate of $314\ \text{m}^3/\text{h}$. If the radius of the tank is 10 m, then the height of sand in the tank increases at the rate of :
(A) $1{\cdot}1$ m/h
(B) $1$ m/h
(C) $\pi$ m/h
(D) $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ m/h
--- ans B
Volume of sand $V = \pi r^2h = 100\pi h$ (radius fixed at 10 m), so $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = 100\pi\dfrac{dh}{dt}$.
$$314 = 100\pi\frac{dh}{dt} \Rightarrow \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{314}{100\pi} = \frac{3.14}{3.14} = 1\ \text{m/h}\quad(\text{taking } \pi = 3.14)$$

@topic Order and degree
The sum of the order and the degree of the differential equation $y = x\dfrac{dy}{dx} + 3\left[2 - \left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2\right]$ is :
(A) not defined
(B) $3$
(C) $1$
(D) $2$
--- ans B
The only derivative present is $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, so order $= 1$. The equation is a polynomial in $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ whose highest power is 2, so degree $= 2$.
Sum $= 1 + 2 = 3$.

@topic Multiplication theorem and independent events
The probability that a particular item is available in three shops A, B and C is $\dfrac{4}{5}$, $\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$ respectively. If a person visits all the three shops to buy the item, then what is the probability that it will be available in at least one of the shops ?
(A) $\dfrac{59}{60}$
(B) $1$
(C) $\dfrac{1}{60}$
(D) $\dfrac{9}{60}$
--- ans A
The availability in the three shops is independent. P(not available in any shop) $= \dfrac{1}{5} \times \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{60}$.
$$P(\text{available in at least one shop}) = 1 - \frac{1}{60} = \frac{59}{60}$$

@topic Increasing and decreasing functions
Find the interval(s) for which the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{3} + \dfrac{3}{x}$, $x \ne 0$ is increasing.
--- ans
$$f'(x) = \frac{1}{3} - \frac{3}{x^2} = \frac{x^2 - 9}{3x^2}$$
$f'(x) = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 = 9 \Rightarrow x = -3, 3$.
Since $3x^2 > 0$ for $x \ne 0$, the sign of $f'(x)$ is the sign of $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$:
$f'(x) > 0$ for $x < -3$ or $x > 3$; $\ f'(x) < 0$ for $-3 < x < 0$ or $0 < x < 3$.
$\therefore f$ is **increasing on $(-\infty, -3) \cup (3, \infty)$** (or $(-\infty, -3] \cup [3, \infty)$).

@topic Basic concepts of vectors
If in a parallelogram PQRS, $\overrightarrow{PQ} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $\overrightarrow{QR} = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + \hat{k}$, then find the unit vectors parallel to the diagonals $\overrightarrow{PR}$ and $\overrightarrow{SQ}$.
--- ans
By the triangle law, $\overrightarrow{PR} = \overrightarrow{PQ} + \overrightarrow{QR} = 3\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$, $\quad|\overrightarrow{PR}| = \sqrt{9 + 1 + 4} = \sqrt{14}$.
In a parallelogram $\overrightarrow{PS} = \overrightarrow{QR}$, so $\overrightarrow{SQ} = \overrightarrow{SP} + \overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{PQ} - \overrightarrow{QR} = \hat{i} + 5\hat{j}$, $\quad|\overrightarrow{SQ}| = \sqrt{1 + 25} = \sqrt{26}$.
Unit vectors:
$$\hat{PR} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{14}}\left(3\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}\right),\qquad \hat{SQ} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{26}}\left(\hat{i} + 5\hat{j}\right)$$

@topic Area enclosed by lines
Using integration, find the area of the region bounded by $y = 5x + 4$, $y = 0$, $x = -1$ and $x = 1$.
--- ans
The line $y = 5x + 4$ meets the x-axis at $x = -\dfrac{4}{5}$; it lies below the x-axis for $-1 \le x < -\dfrac{4}{5}$ and above it for $-\dfrac{4}{5} < x \le 1$.
```plot
{"x":[-1.6,1.6],"y":[-2,10],"lines":[{"a":5,"b":-1,"c":-4,"label":"y = 5x + 4"},{"a":1,"b":0,"c":-1,"label":"x = −1"},{"a":1,"b":0,"c":1,"label":"x = 1"}],"areas":[{"upper":"0","lower":"5*x+4","from":-1,"to":-0.8},{"upper":"5*x+4","lower":"0","from":-0.8,"to":1}],"points":[[-0.8,0,"(−4/5, 0)"],[1,9,"(1, 9)"],[-1,-1,"(−1, −1)"]]}
```
$$\text{Area} = \left|\int_{-1}^{-4/5}(5x + 4)\,dx\right| + \int_{-4/5}^{1}(5x + 4)\,dx = \left|\left[\frac{(5x + 4)^2}{10}\right]_{-1}^{-4/5}\right| + \left[\frac{(5x + 4)^2}{10}\right]_{-4/5}^{1}$$
$$= \left|0 - \frac{1}{10}\right| + \left(\frac{81}{10} - 0\right) = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{81}{10} = \frac{82}{10}$$
**Required area $= \dfrac{41}{5}$ sq. units.**

@topic Foot of perpendicular and image
Find the length of the perpendicular drawn from the point $P(1, 2, 3)$ to the line $\dfrac{x - 6}{3} = \dfrac{y - 7}{2} = \dfrac{z - 7}{-2}$. Also, find the equation of the perpendicular line joining P and the foot of the perpendicular.
--- ans
Let $\dfrac{x - 6}{3} = \dfrac{y - 7}{2} = \dfrac{z - 7}{-2} = \lambda$. Any point on the line is $Q(3\lambda + 6,\ 2\lambda + 7,\ -2\lambda + 7)$.
$$\overrightarrow{PQ} = (3\lambda + 5)\hat{i} + (2\lambda + 5)\hat{j} + (-2\lambda + 4)\hat{k}$$
Q is the foot of the perpendicular when $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ is perpendicular to the line, whose direction ratios are $3, 2, -2$:
$$3(3\lambda + 5) + 2(2\lambda + 5) - 2(-2\lambda + 4) = 0 \Rightarrow 17\lambda + 17 = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda = -1$$
Foot of the perpendicular: $Q(3, 5, 9)$, and $\overrightarrow{PQ} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}$.
$$\text{Length of perpendicular} = PQ = \sqrt{2^2 + 3^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{49} = 7 \text{ units}$$
Equation of the perpendicular PQ (through $P(1, 2, 3)$ with direction ratios $2, 3, 6$):
**Cartesian form:** $\dfrac{x - 1}{2} = \dfrac{y - 2}{3} = \dfrac{z - 3}{6}$
**Vector form:** $\vec{r} = (\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}) + \mu(2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k})$
