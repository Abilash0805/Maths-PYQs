@topic Properties of |A| and |adj A|
Find $|AB|$, if $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
--- ans
$|A| = 0 \cdot 2 - (-1) \cdot 0 = 0$ and $|B| = 3 \cdot 0 - 5 \cdot 0 = 0$.
$$|AB| = |A|\,|B| = 0$$

Differentiate $e^{\sqrt{3x}}$, with respect to $x$.
--- ans
By the chain rule,
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(e^{\sqrt{3x}}\right) = e^{\sqrt{3x}} \cdot \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3x}} \cdot 3 = \frac{\sqrt{3}\,e^{\sqrt{3x}}}{2\sqrt{x}}$$

--- ans

=== 12 | de | 2 | sub
@topic Variable separable
Find the general solution of the differential equation $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = e^{x + y}$.
--- ans
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = e^x \cdot e^y \Rightarrow e^{-y}\,dy = e^x\,dx$$
Integrating both sides:
$$\int e^{-y}\,dy = \int e^x\,dx \Rightarrow -e^{-y} = e^x + C$$
$\therefore$ The general solution is $e^x + e^{-y} = K$ (where $K = -C$ is an arbitrary constant).

=== 21 | cd | 4 | sub
@topic Second order derivatives
If $(a + bx)e^{y/x} = x$, then prove that
$$x^3\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \left(x\frac{dy}{dx} - y\right)^2$$
--- ans
$$e^{y/x} = \frac{x}{a + bx}$$
Taking log on both sides:
$$\frac{y}{x} = \log x - \log(a + bx)$$
Differentiating w.r.t. $x$:
$$\frac{x\frac{dy}{dx} - y}{x^2} = \frac{1}{x} - \frac{b}{a + bx} = \frac{a}{x(a + bx)}$$
$$\Rightarrow x\frac{dy}{dx} - y = \frac{ax}{a + bx} \qquad \dots(1)$$
Differentiating (1) again w.r.t. $x$:
$$x\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + \frac{dy}{dx} - \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{a(a + bx) - ax \cdot b}{(a + bx)^2} = \frac{a^2}{(a + bx)^2}$$
$$\Rightarrow x\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{a^2}{(a + bx)^2}$$
Multiplying by $x^2$:
$$x^3\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \left(\frac{ax}{a + bx}\right)^2 = \left(x\frac{dy}{dx} - y\right)^2 \quad \text{[using (1)]}$$
Hence proved.

=== 22 | aod | 4 | sub
@topic Rate of change
The volume of a cube is increasing at the rate of $8\ \text{cm}^3/\text{s}$. How fast is the surface area increasing when the length of its edge is 12 cm?
--- ans
Let the edge of the cube be $x$ cm, volume $V = x^3$ and surface area $S = 6x^2$.
$$\frac{dV}{dt} = 3x^2\frac{dx}{dt} = 8 \Rightarrow \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{8}{3x^2}$$
$$\frac{dS}{dt} = 12x\frac{dx}{dt} = 12x \cdot \frac{8}{3x^2} = \frac{32}{x}$$
When $x = 12$:
$$\frac{dS}{dt} = \frac{32}{12} = \frac{8}{3}$$
$\therefore$ The surface area is increasing at the rate of $\dfrac{8}{3}\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s}$.

=== 24 | aod | 6 | sub
@topic Optimisation problems
Find the point on the curve $y^2 = 4x$, which is nearest to the point $(2, -8)$.
--- ans
Let $P(x, y)$ be any point on the curve, so $x = \dfrac{y^2}{4}$. Its distance from $Q(2, -8)$ is $z = PQ = \sqrt{(x - 2)^2 + (y + 8)^2}$.
$z$ is least when $s = z^2$ is least:
$$s = \left(\frac{y^2}{4} - 2\right)^2 + (y + 8)^2$$
$$\frac{ds}{dy} = 2\left(\frac{y^2}{4} - 2\right)\cdot\frac{y}{2} + 2(y + 8) = \frac{y^3}{4} - 2y + 2y + 16 = \frac{y^3}{4} + 16$$
$$\frac{ds}{dy} = 0 \Rightarrow y^3 = -64 \Rightarrow y = -4$$
$$\frac{d^2s}{dy^2} = \frac{3y^2}{4};\qquad \left.\frac{d^2s}{dy^2}\right|_{y = -4} = \frac{3(16)}{4} = 12 > 0$$
So $s$ (and hence $z$) is minimum at $y = -4$, where $x = \dfrac{16}{4} = 4$.
$\therefore$ The nearest point is $\mathbf{(4, -4)}$ (at distance $\sqrt{4 + 16} = 2\sqrt{5}$ units).

=== 25b | aoi | 6 | sub
@topic Area enclosed by lines
Using integration, find the area of the triangular region whose sides have the equations $y = 2x + 1$, $y = 3x + 1$ and $x = 4$.
--- ans
Points of intersection:
$y = 2x + 1$ and $y = 3x + 1$: $2x + 1 = 3x + 1 \Rightarrow x = 0$, giving $A(0, 1)$.
$y = 2x + 1$ and $x = 4$: $B(4, 9)$. $\quad y = 3x + 1$ and $x = 4$: $C(4, 13)$.

```plot

```

For $0 \le x \le 4$, the line $y = 3x + 1$ lies above $y = 2x + 1$.
$$\text{Area} = \int_0^4 (3x + 1)\,dx - \int_0^4 (2x + 1)\,dx = \int_0^4 x\,dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = 8$$
$\therefore$ Area of the triangular region $= 8$ sq. units.
