@topic Operations on matrices
Find the value of $x - y$, if
$$2\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 0 & x \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} y & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 1 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$$
--- ans
$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 + y & 6 \\ 1 & 2x + 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 1 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$$
Equating corresponding elements: $2 + y = 5 \Rightarrow y = 3$ and $2x + 2 = 8 \Rightarrow x = 3$.
$\therefore\ x - y = 0$.

@topic Integration by substitution
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{\tan^2 x\sec^2 x}{1 - \tan^6 x}\,dx$.
--- ans
$$I = \int \frac{\tan^2 x\sec^2 x}{1 - (\tan^3 x)^2}\,dx$$
Put $\tan^3 x = t \Rightarrow 3\tan^2 x\sec^2 x\,dx = dt$:
$$I = \frac{1}{3}\int \frac{dt}{1 - t^2} = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\log\left|\frac{1 + t}{1 - t}\right| + C = \frac{1}{6}\log\left|\frac{1 + \tan^3 x}{1 - \tan^3 x}\right| + C$$

=== 19 | cd | 4 | sub
@topic Parametric differentiation
If $x = \cos t + \log\tan\left(\dfrac{t}{2}\right)$, $y = \sin t$, then find the values of $\dfrac{d^2y}{dt^2}$ and $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
--- ans
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = -\sin t + \frac{1}{\tan\frac{t}{2}}\cdot\sec^2\frac{t}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = -\sin t + \frac{1}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}\cos\frac{t}{2}} = -\sin t + \frac{1}{\sin t} = \frac{\cos^2 t}{\sin t}$$
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = \cos t,\qquad \frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = -\sin t$$
$$\left.\frac{d^2y}{dt^2}\right|_{t = \pi/4} = -\sin\frac{\pi}{4} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$$
Now
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \cos t\cdot\frac{\sin t}{\cos^2 t} = \tan t$$
$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dt}(\tan t)\cdot\frac{dt}{dx} = \sec^2 t\cdot\frac{\sin t}{\cos^2 t} = \sec^4 t\sin t$$
$$\left.\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right|_{t = \pi/4} = \left(\sqrt{2}\right)^4\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{2}} = 2\sqrt{2}$$

=== 24 | aod | 6 | sub
@topic Optimisation problems
Show that the altitude of the right circular cone of maximum volume that can be inscribed in a sphere of radius $r$ is $\dfrac{4r}{3}$. Also find the maximum volume of cone.
--- ans
Let O be the centre of the sphere of radius $r$, and let the cone have vertex A on the sphere, base radius $R$ and height $H$. Let the base centre M be at distance $x$ from O (on the side opposite to A), so $H = r + x$ and, from the right triangle formed by O, M and a point of the base circle, $R^2 = r^2 - x^2$.
Volume of the cone:
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi R^2H = \frac{1}{3}\pi(r^2 - x^2)(r + x) = \frac{1}{3}\pi(r + x)^2(r - x)$$
$$\frac{dV}{dx} = \frac{1}{3}\pi\left[2(r + x)(r - x) - (r + x)^2\right] = \frac{1}{3}\pi(r + x)(r - 3x)$$
$$\frac{dV}{dx} = 0 \Rightarrow x = \frac{r}{3} \quad (x = -r \text{ is rejected})$$
$$\frac{d^2V}{dx^2} = \frac{1}{3}\pi\left[(r - 3x) - 3(r + x)\right] = -\frac{\pi}{3}(2r + 6x);\qquad \left.\frac{d^2V}{dx^2}\right|_{x = r/3} = -\frac{4\pi r}{3} < 0$$
So $V$ is maximum when $x = \dfrac{r}{3}$, i.e. when the altitude is
$$H = r + \frac{r}{3} = \frac{4r}{3}$$
Hence shown.
Maximum volume:
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi\left(r + \frac{r}{3}\right)^2\left(r - \frac{r}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{3}\pi\cdot\frac{16r^2}{9}\cdot\frac{2r}{3} = \frac{32}{81}\pi r^3\ \text{cubic units}$$

=== 25a | det | 6 | sub
@topic Systems of linear equations
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 & 5 \\ 3 & 2 & -4 \\ 1 & 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$, then find $A^{-1}$. Hence solve the following system of equations:
$2x - 3y + 5z = 11$, $3x + 2y - 4z = -5$, $x + y - 2z = -3$.
--- ans
$|A| = 2(-4 + 4) + 3(-6 + 4) + 5(3 - 2) = 0 - 6 + 5 = -1 \ne 0$, so $A^{-1}$ exists.

Cofactors:
$A_{11} = 0,\ A_{12} = 2,\ A_{13} = 1$
$A_{21} = -1,\ A_{22} = -9,\ A_{23} = -5$
$A_{31} = 2,\ A_{32} = 23,\ A_{33} = 13$
$$\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -9 & 23 \\ 1 & -5 & 13 \end{bmatrix},\qquad A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 9 & -23 \\ -1 & 5 & -13 \end{bmatrix}$$
The system is $AX = B$ with $X = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}$, $B = \begin{bmatrix} 11 \\ -5 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$.
$$X = A^{-1}B = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 9 & -23 \\ -1 & 5 & -13 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 11 \\ -5 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 - 5 + 6 \\ -22 - 45 + 69 \\ -11 - 25 + 39 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$$
$\therefore\ x = 1,\ y = 2,\ z = 3$.
