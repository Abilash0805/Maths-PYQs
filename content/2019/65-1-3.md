--- ans

@topic Independent events
A die marked 1, 2, 3 in red and 4, 5, 6 in green is tossed. Let A be the event "number is even" and B be the event "number is marked red". Find whether the events A and B are independent or not.
--- ans

=== 12a | vec | 2 | sub
@topic Magnitude of sum and difference of vectors
If the sum of two unit vectors is a unit vector, prove that the magnitude of their difference is $\sqrt{3}$.
--- ans
Let $\hat{a}, \hat{b}$ be unit vectors with $|\hat{a} + \hat{b}| = 1$.
$$|\hat{a} + \hat{b}|^2 = |\hat{a}|^2 + |\hat{b}|^2 + 2\,\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b} \Rightarrow 1 = 1 + 1 + 2\,\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b} \Rightarrow 2\,\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b} = -1$$
$$|\hat{a} - \hat{b}|^2 = |\hat{a}|^2 + |\hat{b}|^2 - 2\,\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$$
$\therefore |\hat{a} - \hat{b}| = \sqrt{3}$. Hence proved.

=== 15a | rf | 4 | sub
@topic Reflexive, symmetric and transitive relations
Show that the relation $R$ on $\mathbb{R}$ defined as $R = \{(a, b) : a \le b\}$, is reflexive, and transitive but not symmetric.
--- ans
**Reflexive:** For every $a \in \mathbb{R}$, $a \le a$, so $(a, a) \in R$. Hence $R$ is reflexive.

**Transitive:** Let $(a, b) \in R$ and $(b, c) \in R$, where $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$.
Then $a \le b$ and $b \le c \Rightarrow a \le c \Rightarrow (a, c) \in R$. Hence $R$ is transitive.

**Not symmetric:** $1 \le 2$, so $(1, 2) \in R$; but $2 \not\le 1$, so $(2, 1) \notin R$. Hence $R$ is not symmetric.

$\therefore$ R is reflexive and transitive but not symmetric.

@topic Derivative of implicit functions
If $\log\left(x^2 + y^2\right) = 2\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)$, show that $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{x + y}{x - y}$.
--- ans
Differentiating both sides w.r.t. $x$:
$$\frac{1}{x^2 + y^2}\left(2x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = 2\cdot\frac{1}{1 + \frac{y^2}{x^2}}\cdot\frac{x\frac{dy}{dx} - y}{x^2}$$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{2}{x^2 + y^2}\left(x + y\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = \frac{2}{x^2 + y^2}\left(x\frac{dy}{dx} - y\right)$$
$$\Rightarrow x + y\frac{dy}{dx} = x\frac{dy}{dx} - y \Rightarrow (x - y)\frac{dy}{dx} = x + y$$
$$\therefore\ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x + y}{x - y}$$
Hence proved.

=== 17b | cd | 4 | sub
@topic Logarithmic differentiation
If $x^y - y^x = a^b$, find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
--- ans
Let $u = x^y$ and $v = y^x$. Then $u - v = a^b$, so
$$\frac{du}{dx} - \frac{dv}{dx} = 0 \quad\cdots(1)$$
$\log u = y\log x \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{u}\dfrac{du}{dx} = \dfrac{y}{x} + \log x\,\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{du}{dx} = x^y\left(\frac{y}{x} + \log x\,\frac{dy}{dx}\right) \quad\cdots(2)$$
$\log v = x\log y \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{v}\dfrac{dv}{dx} = \log y + \dfrac{x}{y}\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{dv}{dx} = y^x\left(\log y + \frac{x}{y}\frac{dy}{dx}\right) \quad\cdots(3)$$
From (1), (2) and (3):
$$x^y\left(\frac{y}{x} + \log x\,\frac{dy}{dx}\right) - y^x\left(\log y + \frac{x}{y}\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = 0$$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx}\left(x^y\log x - x\,y^{x-1}\right) = y^x\log y - y\,x^{y-1}$$
$$\therefore\ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y^x\log y - y\,x^{y-1}}{x^y\log x - x\,y^{x-1}}$$

@topic Second order derivatives
If $y = \left(\sin^{-1}x\right)^2$, prove that $\left(1 - x^2\right)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} - x\dfrac{dy}{dx} - 2 = 0$
--- ans
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2\sin^{-1}x\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} \Rightarrow \sqrt{1 - x^2}\,\frac{dy}{dx} = 2\sin^{-1}x$$

$$\left(1 - x^2\right)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - x\frac{dy}{dx} = 2$$
$$\therefore\ \left(1 - x^2\right)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - x\frac{dy}{dx} - 2 = 0$$
Hence proved.

=== 19 | int | 4 | sub
@topic Property ∫₀ᵃ f(x) dx = ∫₀ᵃ f(a − x) dx
Prove that $\displaystyle\int_0^a f(x)\,dx = \int_0^a f(a - x)\,dx$, hence evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \frac{x\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx$.
--- ans
**Proof:** In $\displaystyle\int_0^a f(a - x)\,dx$ put $a - x = t \Rightarrow dx = -dt$; when $x = 0,\ t = a$ and when $x = a,\ t = 0$.
$$\int_0^a f(a - x)\,dx = -\int_a^0 f(t)\,dt = \int_0^a f(t)\,dt = \int_0^a f(x)\,dx$$

**Evaluation:** Let $I = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \frac{x\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx \quad\cdots(1)$

$$I = \int_0^{\pi} \frac{(\pi - x)\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx \quad\cdots(2)$$

$$\therefore\ I = \frac{\pi^2}{4}$$

@topic Substitution and partial fractions
Find: $\displaystyle\int \frac{\cos x}{(1 + \sin x)(2 + \sin x)}\,dx$
--- ans
Put $\sin x = t \Rightarrow \cos x\,dx = dt$.
$$I = \int \frac{dt}{(1 + t)(2 + t)}$$
Partial fractions: $\dfrac{1}{(1 + t)(2 + t)} = \dfrac{A}{1 + t} + \dfrac{B}{2 + t} \Rightarrow 1 = A(2 + t) + B(1 + t)$.
$t = -1 \Rightarrow A = 1$; $\quad t = -2 \Rightarrow B = -1$.
$$I = \int \left(\frac{1}{1 + t} - \frac{1}{2 + t}\right)dt = \log|1 + t| - \log|2 + t| + C$$
$$\therefore\ I = \log\left|\frac{1 + \sin x}{2 + \sin x}\right| + C$$

@topic Linear differential equation
Solve the differential equation: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} - \dfrac{2x}{1 + x^2}\,y = x^2 + 2$
--- ans
This is linear of the form $\dfrac{dy}{dx} + Py = Q$ with $P = -\dfrac{2x}{1 + x^2}$, $Q = x^2 + 2$.
$$\text{I.F.} = e^{\int P\,dx} = e^{-\int \frac{2x}{1 + x^2}dx} = e^{-\log(1 + x^2)} = \frac{1}{1 + x^2}$$

$$\therefore\ y = \left(1 + x^2\right)\left(x + \tan^{-1}x + C\right)$$

If $\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$, $2\hat{i} + 5\hat{j}$, $3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$ and $\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} - \hat{k}$ respectively are the position vectors of points A, B, C and D, then find the angle between the straight lines AB and CD. @topic Perpendicular lines and intersection of lines
Find the value of $\lambda$, so that the lines $\dfrac{1 - x}{3} = \dfrac{7y - 14}{\lambda} = \dfrac{z - 3}{2}$ and $\dfrac{7 - 7x}{3\lambda} = \dfrac{y - 5}{1} = \dfrac{6 - z}{5}$ are at right angles. Also, find whether the lines are intersecting or not.
--- ans
Writing the lines in standard form:
$$L_1: \frac{x - 1}{-3} = \frac{y - 2}{\lambda/7} = \frac{z - 3}{2}, \qquad L_2: \frac{x - 1}{-3\lambda/7} = \frac{y - 5}{1} = \frac{z - 6}{-5}$$
Direction ratios: $L_1: \left(-3, \dfrac{\lambda}{7}, 2\right)$, $L_2: \left(-\dfrac{3\lambda}{7}, 1, -5\right)$.
Lines at right angles $\Rightarrow a_1a_2 + b_1b_2 + c_1c_2 = 0$:
$$\frac{9\lambda}{7} + \frac{\lambda}{7} - 10 = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{10\lambda}{7} = 10 \Rightarrow \lambda = 7$$
Then $L_1$ passes through $(1, 2, 3)$ with d.r.'s $(-3, 1, 2)$ and $L_2$ passes through $(1, 5, 6)$ with d.r.'s $(-3, 1, -5)$.
The lines intersect only if
$$\begin{vmatrix} x_2 - x_1 & y_2 - y_1 & z_2 - z_1 \\ a_1 & b_1 & c_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 & c_2 \end{vmatrix} = 0$$
$$\begin{vmatrix} 0 & 3 & 3 \\ -3 & 1 & 2 \\ -3 & 1 & -5 \end{vmatrix} = 0 - 3(15 + 6) + 3(-3 + 3) = -63 \ne 0$$
(the lines are not parallel, and the shortest distance between them is not zero).
$\therefore \lambda = 7$ and the lines are **not intersecting** (they are skew lines).

=== 24 | aod | 6 | sub
@topic Maxima and minima – word problem
A tank with rectangular base and rectangular sides, open at the top is to be constructed so that its depth is 2 m and volume is $8\ \text{m}^3$. If building of tank ₹ 70 per square metre for the base and ₹ 45 per square metre for the sides, what is the cost of least expensive tank?
--- ans
Let the length and breadth of the base be $x$ m and $y$ m. Depth $= 2$ m.
$$V = 2xy = 8 \Rightarrow y = \frac{4}{x}$$
Area of base $= xy = 4\ \text{m}^2$; area of four sides $= 2\cdot 2(x + y) = 4(x + y)\ \text{m}^2$.
Cost:
$$C = 70xy + 45\cdot 4(x + y) = 280 + 180x + \frac{720}{x}$$
$$\frac{dC}{dx} = 180 - \frac{720}{x^2} = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 = 4 \Rightarrow x = 2 \quad (x > 0)$$
$$\frac{d^2C}{dx^2} = \frac{1440}{x^3} = 180 > 0 \text{ at } x = 2$$
So $C$ is minimum at $x = 2$ m (and then $y = 2$ m).
$$C_{\min} = 280 + 180(2) + \frac{720}{2} = 280 + 360 + 360 = 1000$$
$\therefore$ The cost of the least expensive tank is **₹ 1000**.

@topic Area of a triangle using integration
Using integration, find the area of the triangle whose vertices are $(2, 3)$, $(3, 5)$ and $(4, 4)$.

@topic LPP – manufacturing problem
A manufacturer has employed 5 skilled men and 10 semi-skilled men and makes two models A and B of an article. The making of one item of model A requires 2 hours work by a skilled man and 2 hours work by a semi-skilled man. One item of model B requires 1 hour by a skilled man 3 hours by a semi-skilled man. No man is expected to work more than 8 hours per day. The manufacturer's profit on an item of model A is ₹ 15 and on an item of model B is ₹ 10. How many of items of each model should be made per day in order to maximize daily profit? Formulate the above LPP and solve it graphically and find the maximum profit.
--- ans
Let $x$ items of model A and $y$ items of model B be made per day.
Skilled man-hours available $= 5 \times 8 = 40$; semi-skilled man-hours available $= 10 \times 8 = 80$.

Maximize $Z = 15x + 10y$
subject to
$2x + y \le 40$ (skilled men),
$2x + 3y \le 80$ (semi-skilled men),
$x \ge 0,\ y \ge 0$.

Draw $2x + y = 40$ (through $(20, 0)$, $(0, 40)$) and $2x + 3y = 80$ (through $(40, 0)$, $\left(0, \frac{80}{3}\right)$). Both inequalities are satisfied by $(0, 0)$.
Intersection: subtracting, $2y = 40 \Rightarrow y = 20,\ x = 10$, i.e. $(10, 20)$.

```plot
{"x":[-2,45],"y":[-2,45],"lines":[{"a":2,"b":1,"c":40,"label":"2x + y = 40"},{"a":2,"b":3,"c":80,"label":"2x + 3y = 80"}],"polygons":[{"pts":[[0,0],[20,0],[10,20],[0,26.667]],"label":"Feasible region"}],"points":[{"x":0,"y":0,"label":"O(0, 0)"},{"x":20,"y":0,"label":"A(20, 0)"},{"x":10,"y":20,"label":"C(10, 20)"},{"x":0,"y":26.667,"label":"B(0, 80/3)"}]}
```

The feasible region OACB is bounded.

| Corner point | $Z = 15x + 10y$ |
|---|---|
| $O(0, 0)$ | $0$ |
| $A(20, 0)$ | $300$ |
| $C(10, 20)$ | $350$ ← maximum |
| $B\left(0, \frac{80}{3}\right)$ | $\frac{800}{3} \approx 266.67$ |

$\therefore$ The manufacturer should make **10 items of model A and 20 items of model B** per day; maximum profit $=$ **₹ 350**.
