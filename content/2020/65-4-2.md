=== 9 | vec | 1 | mcq
@topic Scalar product and projection
Let $\vec{a} = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$. If $\vec{b}$ is a vector such that $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{b}|^2$ and $|\vec{a} - \vec{b}| = \sqrt{7}$ then $|\vec{b}|$ equals
(A) $7$
(B) $14$
(C) $\sqrt{7}$
(D) $21$
--- ans C
$|\vec{a}|^2 = 1 + 4 + 9 = 14$. Squaring $|\vec{a} - \vec{b}| = \sqrt{7}$:
$$|\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 - 2\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 7 \Rightarrow 14 + |\vec{b}|^2 - 2|\vec{b}|^2 = 7 \Rightarrow |\vec{b}|^2 = 7$$
$\therefore\ |\vec{b}| = \sqrt{7}$.

=== 10 | prob | 1 | mcq
@topic Conditional probability
Three dice are thrown simultaneously. The probability of obtaining a total score of 5 is
(A) $\dfrac{5}{216}$
(B) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
(C) $\dfrac{1}{36}$
(D) $\dfrac{1}{49}$
--- ans C
Total outcomes $= 6^3 = 216$.
Sum 5: $(1, 1, 3), (1, 3, 1), (3, 1, 1), (1, 2, 2), (2, 1, 2), (2, 2, 1)$: 6 outcomes.
$$P = \frac{6}{216} = \frac{1}{36}$$

=== 15 | cd | 1 | sub
@topic Differentiability
Fill in the blank:
If $f(x) = 2|x| + 3|\sin x| + 6$, then the right hand derivative of $f(x)$ at $x = 0$ is ..................
--- ans
$f(0) = 6$. For $h > 0$ small, $|h| = h$ and $|\sin h| = \sin h$:
$$Rf'(0) = \lim_{h \to 0^+}\frac{f(h) - f(0)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0^+}\frac{2h + 3\sin h}{h} = 2 + 3\lim_{h \to 0^+}\frac{\sin h}{h} = 2 + 3 = 5$$
Right hand derivative $= \mathbf{5}$.

=== 19 | int | 1 | sub
@topic Integration by substitution
Find $\displaystyle\int \sin^5\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\cdot\cos\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)dx$
--- ans
Put $\sin\dfrac{x}{2} = t \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{2}\cos\dfrac{x}{2}\,dx = dt$:
$$\int \sin^5\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)dx = 2\int t^5\,dt = \frac{t^6}{3} + C = \frac{1}{3}\sin^6\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) + C$$

=== 25 | cd | 2 | sub
@topic Logarithmic differentiation
Find the derivative of $x^{\log x}$ w.r.t. $\log x$.
--- ans
Let $u = x^{\log x}$ and $v = \log x$. We need $\dfrac{du}{dv}$.
$\log u = \log x \cdot \log x = (\log x)^2$. Differentiating w.r.t. $x$:
$$\frac{1}{u}\frac{du}{dx} = \frac{2\log x}{x} \Rightarrow \frac{du}{dx} = x^{\log x}\cdot\frac{2\log x}{x}$$
$$\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{1}{x}$$
$$\frac{du}{dv} = \frac{du/dx}{dv/dx} = \frac{x^{\log x}\cdot\frac{2\log x}{x}}{\frac{1}{x}} = 2x^{\log x}\log x$$

=== 31 | rf | 4 | sub
@topic One-one and onto functions
Show that the function $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ defined by $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1}$, $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ is neither one-one nor onto.
--- ans
**Not one-one:** Let $f(x_1) = f(x_2)$:
$$\frac{x_1}{x_1^2 + 1} = \frac{x_2}{x_2^2 + 1} \Rightarrow x_1x_2^2 + x_1 = x_2x_1^2 + x_2 \Rightarrow (x_1 - x_2)(1 - x_1x_2) = 0$$
So $f(x_1) = f(x_2)$ whenever $x_1x_2 = 1$, even if $x_1 \ne x_2$. For example,
$$f(2) = \frac{2}{5} = \frac{1/2}{1/4 + 1} = f\left(\frac{1}{2}\right), \quad \text{but } 2 \ne \frac{1}{2}$$
Hence $f$ is not one-one.

**Not onto:** Let $y \in \mathbb{R}$ and suppose $f(x) = y$:
$$\frac{x}{x^2 + 1} = y \Rightarrow yx^2 - x + y = 0$$
For $y \ne 0$, real $x$ exists only if the discriminant $1 - 4y^2 \ge 0$, i.e. $-\dfrac{1}{2} \le y \le \dfrac{1}{2}$.
So, for example, $y = 1 \in \mathbb{R}$ (co-domain) has no pre-image: $x^2 - x + 1 = 0$ has no real root.
Range of $f$ $= \left[-\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right] \ne \mathbb{R}$, so $f$ is not onto.

Hence $f$ is neither one-one nor onto.

=== 32 | int | 4 | sub
@topic Definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} |x^3 - x|\,dx$
--- ans
$x^3 - x = x(x - 1)(x + 1)$: it is $\ge 0$ on $[-1, 0]$, $\le 0$ on $[0, 1]$ and $\ge 0$ on $[1, 2]$.
$$\int_{-1}^{2} |x^3 - x|\,dx = \int_{-1}^{0}(x^3 - x)\,dx - \int_{0}^{1}(x^3 - x)\,dx + \int_{1}^{2}(x^3 - x)\,dx$$
With $\displaystyle\int (x^3 - x)\,dx = \frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{x^2}{2}$:
$$\int_{-1}^{0}(x^3 - x)\,dx = 0 - \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}$$
$$-\int_{0}^{1}(x^3 - x)\,dx = -\left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}$$
$$\int_{1}^{2}(x^3 - x)\,dx = (4 - 2) - \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{9}{4}$$
$$\therefore\ \int_{-1}^{2} |x^3 - x|\,dx = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$$

=== 36 | aoi | 6 | sub
@topic Area bounded by a curve and a line
Using integration find the area of the region : $\{(x, y) : 0 \le y \le x^2,\ 0 \le y \le x,\ 0 \le x \le 2\}$
--- ans
The curves $y = x^2$ and $y = x$ meet where $x^2 = x$, i.e. at $x = 0$ and $x = 1$.
For $0 \le x \le 1$, $x^2 \le x$, so the upper boundary is $y = x^2$; for $1 \le x \le 2$, $x \le x^2$, so the upper boundary is $y = x$.
