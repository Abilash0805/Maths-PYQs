@topic Operations on matrices
If $\begin{bmatrix} x & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = 0$, then $x$ equals
(A) $0$
(B) $-2$
(C) $-1$
(D) $2$
--- ans D
$$\begin{bmatrix} x & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x - 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
By equality of matrices, $x - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2$.

@topic Standard integrals
$\displaystyle\int 4^x3^x\,dx$ equals
(A) $\dfrac{12^x}{\log 12} + C$
(B) $\dfrac{4^x}{\log 4} + C$
(C) $\left(\dfrac{4^x \cdot 3^x}{\log 4 \cdot \log 3}\right) + C$
(D) $\dfrac{3^x}{\log 3} + C$
--- ans A
$4^x3^x = (4 \times 3)^x = 12^x$, and $\displaystyle\int a^x\,dx = \frac{a^x}{\log a} + C$.
$$\int 4^x3^x\,dx = \int 12^x\,dx = \frac{12^x}{\log 12} + C$$

=== 3 | prob | 1 | mcq
@topic Conditional probability
A number is chosen randomly from numbers 1 to 60. The probability that the chosen number is a multiple of 2 or 5 is
(A) $\dfrac{2}{5}$
(B) $\dfrac{3}{5}$
(C) $\dfrac{7}{10}$
(D) $\dfrac{9}{10}$
--- ans B
Let $A$: multiple of 2, $B$: multiple of 5. Then $n(A) = 30$, $n(B) = 12$, $n(A \cap B) = 6$ (multiples of 10).
$$P(A \cup B) = \frac{30}{60} + \frac{12}{60} - \frac{6}{60} = \frac{36}{60} = \frac{3}{5}$$

@topic Types of relations
Fill in the blank:
A relation $R$ on a set $A$ is called .................. If $(a_1, a_2) \in R$ and $(a_2, a_3) \in R$ implies that $(a_1, a_3) \in R$, for $a_1, a_2, a_3 \in A$.
--- ans
A relation in which $(a_1, a_2) \in R$ and $(a_2, a_3) \in R \Rightarrow (a_1, a_3) \in R$ for all $a_1, a_2, a_3 \in A$ is called a **transitive** relation.

@topic Principal values
Evaluate: $\sin\left[\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \sin^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\right]$.
--- ans
The principal value of $\sin^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ is $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ (it lies in $\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$).
$$\sin\left[\frac{\pi}{3} - \left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\right] = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{6}\right) = \sin\frac{\pi}{2} = 1$$

@topic Partial fractions
Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{x + 1}{x(1 - 2x)}\,dx$.
--- ans
Let
$$\frac{x + 1}{x(1 - 2x)} = \frac{A}{x} + \frac{B}{1 - 2x} \Rightarrow x + 1 = A(1 - 2x) + Bx$$
Putting $x = 0$: $A = 1$. Putting $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$: $\dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{B}{2} \Rightarrow B = 3$.
$$\int \frac{x + 1}{x(1 - 2x)}\,dx = \int \frac{dx}{x} + 3\int \frac{dx}{1 - 2x} = \log|x| - \frac{3}{2}\log|1 - 2x| + C$$

=== 22 | int | 2 | sub
@topic Integration by substitution
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{x\sin^{-1}(x^2)}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}}\,dx$.
--- ans
Put $\sin^{-1}(x^2) = t$. Then
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - (x^2)^2}}\cdot 2x\,dx = dt \Rightarrow \frac{x\,dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}} = \frac{dt}{2}$$
$$\int \frac{x\sin^{-1}(x^2)}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int t\,dt = \frac{t^2}{4} + C = \frac{1}{4}\left[\sin^{-1}(x^2)\right]^2 + C$$

=== 28 | cd | 4 | sub
@topic Logarithmic differentiation
If $y = (\cos x)^x + \tan^{-1}\sqrt{x}$, find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
--- ans
Let $u = (\cos x)^x$ and $v = \tan^{-1}\sqrt{x}$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{du}{dx} + \dfrac{dv}{dx}$.

**For $u$:** $\log u = x\log(\cos x)$. Differentiating w.r.t. $x$:
$$\frac{1}{u}\frac{du}{dx} = \log(\cos x) + x\cdot\frac{-\sin x}{\cos x} = \log(\cos x) - x\tan x$$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{du}{dx} = (\cos x)^x\left[\log(\cos x) - x\tan x\right]$$

**For $v$:** by the chain rule,
$$\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{1}{1 + (\sqrt{x})^2}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}\,(1 + x)}$$

$$\therefore\ \frac{dy}{dx} = (\cos x)^x\left[\log(\cos x) - x\tan x\right] + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}\,(1 + x)}$$

=== 36 | aod | 6 | sub
@topic Optimisation problems
Find the point on the curve $y^2 = 4x$ which is nearest to the point $(2, 1)$.
--- ans
Let $P(x, y)$ be a point on the curve, so $x = \dfrac{y^2}{4}$. Let $D$ be the distance of $P$ from $A(2, 1)$; it is convenient to minimise $S = D^2$:
$$S = (x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = \left(\frac{y^2}{4} - 2\right)^2 + (y - 1)^2$$
$$\frac{dS}{dy} = 2\left(\frac{y^2}{4} - 2\right)\cdot\frac{y}{2} + 2(y - 1) = \frac{y^3}{4} - 2y + 2y - 2 = \frac{y^3}{4} - 2$$
For critical points, $\dfrac{dS}{dy} = 0 \Rightarrow y^3 = 8 \Rightarrow y = 2$.
$$\frac{d^2S}{dy^2} = \frac{3y^2}{4} = 3 > 0 \text{ at } y = 2$$
So $S$ (and hence $D$) is minimum at $y = 2$, where $x = \dfrac{4}{4} = 1$.
(Since $y = 2$ is the only critical point, this local minimum is the absolute minimum.)

$\therefore$ The nearest point is $\mathbf{(1, 2)}$, at distance $\sqrt{(1 - 2)^2 + (2 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{2}$ units.
