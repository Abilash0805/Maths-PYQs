=== 27 | rf | 4 | sub
@topic Equivalence relations and classes
Prove that the relation $R$ on $\mathbb{Z}$, defined by $R = \{(x, y) : (x - y) \text{ is divisible by } 5\}$ is an equivalence relation.
--- ans
**Reflexive:** For any $x \in \mathbb{Z}$, $x - x = 0 = 5 \times 0$ is divisible by 5, so $(x, x) \in R$. Hence $R$ is reflexive.

**Symmetric:** Let $(x, y) \in R$. Then $x - y = 5k$ for some $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.
$\Rightarrow y - x = 5(-k)$, with $-k \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $(y, x) \in R$. Hence $R$ is symmetric.

**Transitive:** Let $(x, y) \in R$ and $(y, z) \in R$. Then $x - y = 5k$ and $y - z = 5m$ for some $k, m \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Adding: $x - z = 5(k + m)$, with $k + m \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $(x, z) \in R$. Hence $R$ is transitive.

Since $R$ is reflexive, symmetric and transitive, **$R$ is an equivalence relation**.

@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \frac{x\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx$.
--- ans
Let $I = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \frac{x\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx \quad\cdots(1)$
Using $\displaystyle\int_0^a f(x)\,dx = \int_0^a f(a - x)\,dx$, and $\sin(\pi - x) = \sin x$, $\cos(\pi - x) = -\cos x$:
$$I = \int_0^{\pi} \frac{(\pi - x)\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx \quad\cdots(2)$$
Adding (1) and (2):
$$2I = \pi\int_0^{\pi} \frac{\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx$$
Put $\cos x = t \Rightarrow -\sin x\,dx = dt$; when $x = 0$, $t = 1$ and when $x = \pi$, $t = -1$.
$$2I = \pi\int_1^{-1} \frac{-dt}{1 + t^2} = \pi\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{dt}{1 + t^2} = \pi\left[\tan^{-1}t\right]_{-1}^{1} = \pi\left[\frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi}{4}\right] = \frac{\pi^2}{2}$$
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{\pi^2}{4}$$

@topic Word problems
A manufacturer has three machines I, II and III installed in his factory. Machine I and II are capable of being operated for atmost 12 hours whereas machine III must be operated for atleast 5 hours a day. He produces only two items M and N each requiring the use of all the three machines.
The number of hours required for producing 1 unit of M and N on three machines are given in the following table :

| Items | Machine I | Machine II | Machine III |
|---|---|---|---|
| M | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| N | 2 | 1 | 1.25 |

He makes a profit of ₹ 600 and ₹ 400 on one unit of items M and N respectively. How many units of each item should he produce so as to maximize his profit assuming that he can sell all the items that he produced. What will be the maximum profit ?
--- ans
Let the manufacturer produce $x$ units of M and $y$ units of N.

**LPP:** Maximise $Z = 600x + 400y$
subject to
$x + 2y \le 12$ (machine I)
$2x + y \le 12$ (machine II)
$x + 1.25y \ge 5$, i.e. $4x + 5y \ge 20$ (machine III)
$x \ge 0,\ y \ge 0$

Lines: $x + 2y = 12$ through $(12, 0)$, $(0, 6)$; $2x + y = 12$ through $(6, 0)$, $(0, 12)$; $4x + 5y = 20$ through $(5, 0)$, $(0, 4)$.
$(0, 0)$ satisfies the first two inequalities but not the third, so the region lies below the first two lines and above the third.
Intersection of $x + 2y = 12$ and $2x + y = 12$: $x = y = 4$, i.e. $(4, 4)$.

$P(H) = 3P(T)$ and $P(H) + P(T) = 1 \Rightarrow P(T) = \dfrac{1}{4}$, $P(H) = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

$$P(X = 0) = P(HH) = \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{3}{4} = \frac{9}{16}$$
$$P(X = 1) = P(HT) + P(TH) = 2\cdot\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{4} = \frac{6}{16}$$
$$P(X = 2) = P(TT) = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{16}$$

| $X$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $P(X)$ | $\frac{9}{16}$ | $\frac{6}{16}$ | $\frac{1}{16}$ |

$$\text{Mean} = \sum X\,P(X) = 0 \cdot \frac{9}{16} + 1 \cdot \frac{6}{16} + 2 \cdot \frac{1}{16} = \frac{8}{16} = \frac{1}{2}$$
