@topic Differentiability

(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of the Assertion (A).

Evaluate :
$$\int_0^{\pi}\frac{x\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx$$
--- ans
Let $I = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi}\frac{x\sin x}{1 + \cos^2 x}\,dx$. Using $\displaystyle\int_0^a f(x)\,dx = \int_0^a f(a - x)\,dx$:

$$\boxed{I = \frac{\pi^2}{4}}$$

@topic Multiplication theorem and independent events
Four students of class XII are given a problem to solve independently. Their chances of solving the problem respectively are $\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{5}$. Find the probability that at most one of them will solve the problem.
--- ans
Let $p_i$ be the probability that student $i$ solves it: $p_1 = \frac{1}{2}, p_2 = \frac{1}{3}, p_3 = \frac{2}{3}, p_4 = \frac{1}{5}$; failures $q_1 = \frac{1}{2}, q_2 = \frac{2}{3}, q_3 = \frac{1}{3}, q_4 = \frac{4}{5}$. The events are independent.
$$P(\text{none}) = q_1q_2q_3q_4 = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{4}{5} = \frac{8}{90}$$
$$P(\text{exactly one}) = p_1q_2q_3q_4 + q_1p_2q_3q_4 + q_1q_2p_3q_4 + q_1q_2q_3p_4 = \frac{8}{90} + \frac{4}{90} + \frac{16}{90} + \frac{2}{90} = \frac{30}{90}$$
$$P(\text{at most one}) = \frac{8}{90} + \frac{30}{90} = \frac{38}{90} = \boxed{\frac{19}{45}}$$
