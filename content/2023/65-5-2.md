=== 4 | mat | 1 | mcq
@topic Types of matrices
If $A = [a_{ij}]$ is a square matrix of order 2 such that $a_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{when } i \ne j \\ 0, & \text{when } i = j \end{cases}$, then $A^2$ is:
(A) $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
(B) $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
(C) $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
(D) $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
--- ans D
$a_{11} = a_{22} = 0$ and $a_{12} = a_{21} = 1$, so $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
$$A^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 + 1 & 0 + 0 \\ 0 + 0 & 1 + 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

=== 9 | cd | 1 | mcq
@topic Differentiability
The function $f(x) = x|x|$, $x \in \mathbb{R}$ is differentiable
(A) only at $x = 0$
(B) only at $x = 1$
(C) in $\mathbb{R}$
(D) in $\mathbb{R} - \{0\}$
--- ans C
$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2, & x \ge 0 \\ -x^2, & x < 0 \end{cases}$ is a polynomial on each side, so it is differentiable for every $x \ne 0$.
At $x = 0$: $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(h) - f(0)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{h|h|}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} |h| = 0$, so $f'(0) = 0$ exists.
Hence $f$ is differentiable in $\mathbb{R}$ (with $f'(x) = 2|x|$). (The publisher's key (D) is incorrect.)

@topic Definite integrals
The value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4} (\sin 2x)\,dx$ is:
(A) $0$
(B) $1$
(C) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
(D) $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
--- ans C
$$\int_0^{\pi/4} \sin 2x\,dx = \left[-\frac{\cos 2x}{2}\right]_0^{\pi/4} = -\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{2} - \cos 0\right) = -\frac{1}{2}(0 - 1) = \frac{1}{2}$$

=== 14 | vec | 1 | mcq
@topic Scalar product and projection
A unit vector $\hat{a}$ makes equal but acute angles on the co-ordinate axes. The projection of the vector $\hat{a}$ on the vector $\vec{b} = 5\hat{i} + 7\hat{j} - \hat{k}$ is:
(A) $\dfrac{11}{15}$
(B) $\dfrac{11}{5\sqrt{3}}$
(C) $\dfrac{4}{5}$
(D) $\dfrac{3}{5\sqrt{3}}$
--- ans A
Equal acute angles: $l = m = n > 0$ and $3l^2 = 1 \Rightarrow l = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, so $\hat{a} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}\right)$.
$|\vec{b}| = \sqrt{25 + 49 + 1} = \sqrt{75} = 5\sqrt{3}$.
$$\text{Projection} = \frac{\hat{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|} = \frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(5 + 7 - 1)}{5\sqrt{3}} = \frac{11}{15}$$

=== 18 | prob | 1 | mcq
@topic Multiplication theorem and independent events
If A and B are two independent events such that $P(A) = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $P(B) = \dfrac{1}{4}$, then $P\left(\dfrac{B'}{A}\right)$ is:
(A) $\dfrac{1}{4}$
(B) $\dfrac{1}{8}$
(C) $\dfrac{3}{4}$
(D) $1$
--- ans C
A and B independent $\Rightarrow$ A and $B'$ are also independent, so $P(A \cap B') = P(A)P(B')$.
$$P(B'|A) = \frac{P(A \cap B')}{P(A)} = P(B') = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$$

=== 25 | 3d | 2 | sub
@topic Angle between two lines
Find the angle between the following two lines:
$\vec{r} = 2\hat{i} - 5\hat{j} + \hat{k} + \lambda\left(3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}\right)$;
$\vec{r} = 7\hat{i} - 6\hat{k} + \mu\left(\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}\right)$
--- ans
The lines are parallel to $\vec{b}_1 = 3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b}_2 = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$.
$|\vec{b}_1| = \sqrt{9 + 4 + 36} = 7$, $|\vec{b}_2| = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = 3$, $\vec{b}_1\cdot\vec{b}_2 = 3 + 4 + 12 = 19$.
If $\theta$ is the angle between the lines,
$$\cos\theta = \left|\frac{\vec{b}_1\cdot\vec{b}_2}{|\vec{b}_1||\vec{b}_2|}\right| = \frac{19}{7 \times 3} = \frac{19}{21}$$
$$\therefore\ \theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{19}{21}\right)$$

=== 26 | det | 3 | sub
@topic Area of a triangle
Using determinants, find the area of $\Delta PQR$ with vertices $P(3, 1)$, $Q(9, 3)$ and $R(5, 7)$. Also, find the equation of line PQ using determinants.
--- ans
$$\text{Area of } \Delta PQR = \frac{1}{2}\left|\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 1 & 1 \\ 9 & 3 & 1 \\ 5 & 7 & 1 \end{vmatrix}\right|$$
Expanding along $R_1$:
$$\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 1 & 1 \\ 9 & 3 & 1 \\ 5 & 7 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 3(3 - 7) - 1(9 - 5) + 1(63 - 15) = -12 - 4 + 48 = 32$$
$$\therefore\ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\times 32 = 16\ \text{sq. units}$$

Let $A(x, y)$ be any point on PQ. Then P, Q, A are collinear, so the area of $\Delta PQA$ is zero:
$$\frac{1}{2}\begin{vmatrix} x & y & 1 \\ 3 & 1 & 1 \\ 9 & 3 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 0 \Rightarrow x(1 - 3) - y(3 - 9) + 1(9 - 9) = 0$$
$$\Rightarrow -2x + 6y = 0 \Rightarrow x - 3y = 0$$
$\therefore$ Equation of PQ is $\boxed{x - 3y = 0}$.

=== 28a | int | 3 | sub
@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} \frac{\cos 2x}{1 + \cos 2x}\,dx$
--- ans
Let $f(x) = \dfrac{\cos 2x}{1 + \cos 2x}$. Then $f(-x) = \dfrac{\cos(-2x)}{1 + \cos(-2x)} = f(x)$, so $f$ is an even function.
$$\therefore\ I = 2\int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{\cos 2x}{1 + \cos 2x}\,dx = 2\int_0^{\pi/4} \left(1 - \frac{1}{1 + \cos 2x}\right)dx$$
Using $1 + \cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x$:
$$I = 2\int_0^{\pi/4} \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\sec^2 x\right)dx = 2\left[x - \frac{1}{2}\tan x\right]_0^{\pi/4}$$
$$= 2\left[\frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{2}(1) - 0\right] = \frac{\pi}{2} - 1$$

=== 28b | int | 3 | sub
@topic Integration by substitution
Find: $\displaystyle\int e^{x^2}\left(x^5 + 2x^3\right)dx$
--- ans
$$I = \int x e^{x^2}\left(x^4 + 2x^2\right)dx$$
Put $x^2 = t \Rightarrow 2x\,dx = dt$:
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int e^t\left(t^2 + 2t\right)dt$$
This is of the form $\displaystyle\int e^t\left[f(t) + f'(t)\right]dt = e^t f(t) + C$ with $f(t) = t^2$, $f'(t) = 2t$.
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{1}{2}e^t t^2 + C = \frac{1}{2}x^4 e^{x^2} + C$$

=== 29 | aoi | 3 | sub
@topic Area of circles and ellipses
Find the area of the minor segment of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ cut off by the line $x = 1$, using integration.
--- ans
The circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ has centre $O(0, 0)$ and radius 2. The line $x = 1$ meets it at $\left(1, \pm\sqrt{3}\right)$. The minor segment is the part of the circle to the right of $x = 1$, i.e. $1 \le x \le 2$; it is symmetric about the $x$-axis.

```plot
{"x":[-3,3],"y":[-3,3],"circles":[{"cx":0,"cy":0,"r":2}],"lines":[{"a":1,"b":0,"c":1,"label":"x = 1"}],"areas":[{"upper":"sqrt(4-x*x)","lower":"-sqrt(4-x*x)","from":1,"to":2}],"points":[{"x":1,"y":1.732,"label":"(1, √3)"},{"x":1,"y":-1.732,"label":"(1, −√3)"},{"x":2,"y":0,"label":"(2, 0)"}],"labels":[{"x":-1.4,"y":1.9,"text":"x² + y² = 4"}]}
```

$$\text{Required area} = 2\int_1^2 y\,dx = 2\int_1^2 \sqrt{4 - x^2}\,dx = 2\left[\frac{x}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^2} + \frac{4}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{2}\right]_1^2$$
$$= 2\left[\left(0 + 2\sin^{-1}1\right) - \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + 2\sin^{-1}\frac{1}{2}\right)\right] = 2\left[\pi - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{\pi}{3}\right]$$
$$= 2\left[\frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right] = \left(\frac{4\pi}{3} - \sqrt{3}\right)\ \text{sq. units}$$
(The publisher's answer $\sqrt{3} + \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ is the area of the strip $0 \le x \le 1$, which is not the minor segment.)

=== 32 | int | 5 | sub
@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate: $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \frac{x}{1 + \sin x}\,dx$
--- ans
Let $I = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \frac{x}{1 + \sin x}\,dx \quad\cdots(1)$
Using $\displaystyle\int_0^a f(x)\,dx = \int_0^a f(a - x)\,dx$ and $\sin(\pi - x) = \sin x$:
$$I = \int_0^{\pi} \frac{\pi - x}{1 + \sin x}\,dx \quad\cdots(2)$$
Adding (1) and (2):
$$2I = \pi\int_0^{\pi} \frac{dx}{1 + \sin x}$$
Since $\dfrac{1}{1 + \sin(\pi - x)} = \dfrac{1}{1 + \sin x}$, by $\displaystyle\int_0^{2a} f(x)\,dx = 2\int_0^a f(x)\,dx$ when $f(2a - x) = f(x)$:
$$2I = 2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{dx}{1 + \sin x} = 2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{dx}{1 + \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right)} = 2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{dx}{1 + \cos x}$$
Using $1 + \cos x = 2\cos^2\dfrac{x}{2}$:
$$2I = 2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{1}{2}\sec^2\frac{x}{2}\,dx = 2\pi\left[\tan\frac{x}{2}\right]_0^{\pi/2} = 2\pi(1 - 0) = 2\pi$$
$$\therefore\ I = \pi$$
