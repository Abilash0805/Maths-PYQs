@topic Standard integrals
If $\dfrac{d}{dx}f(x) = 2x + \dfrac{3}{x}$ and $f(1) = 1$, then $f(x)$ is:
(A) $x^2 + 3\log|x| + 1$
(B) $x^2 + 3\log|x|$
(C) $2 - \dfrac{3}{x^2}$
(D) $x^2 + 3\log|x| - 4$
--- ans B
$f(x) = \displaystyle\int \left(2x + \frac{3}{x}\right)dx = x^2 + 3\log|x| + C$.
$f(1) = 1 \Rightarrow 1 + 3\log 1 + C = 1 \Rightarrow C = 0$.
$\therefore f(x) = x^2 + 3\log|x|$.

@topic Basic concepts of vectors
If in $\Delta ABC$, $\overrightarrow{BA} = 2\vec{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC} = 3\vec{b}$, then $\overrightarrow{AC}$ is:
(A) $2\vec{a} + 3\vec{b}$
(B) $2\vec{a} - 3\vec{b}$
(C) $3\vec{b} - 2\vec{a}$
(D) $-2\vec{a} - 3\vec{b}$
--- ans C
By the triangle law, $\overrightarrow{BA} + \overrightarrow{AC} = \overrightarrow{BC}$.
$$\therefore\ \overrightarrow{AC} = \overrightarrow{BC} - \overrightarrow{BA} = 3\vec{b} - 2\vec{a}$$

@topic Vector product and area
If $|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = \sqrt{3}$ and $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = -3$, then angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is:
(A) $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
(B) $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
(C) $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
(D) $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
--- ans D
$|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\theta = \sqrt{3}$ and $|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta = -3$.
Dividing: $\tan\theta = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. Since $\sin\theta > 0$ and $\cos\theta < 0$, $\theta$ lies in $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right)$:
$$\theta = \frac{5\pi}{6}$$

@topic Equation of a line
Equation of line passing through origin and making $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ wih $x$, $y$, $z$ axes respectively is
(A) $\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{y}{2} = \dfrac{z}{0}$
(B) $\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{2y}{1} = \dfrac{z}{0}$
(C) $2x = \dfrac{2y}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{z}{1}$
(D) $\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{2y}{1} = \dfrac{z}{1}$

$$\frac{x}{\sqrt{3}/2} = \frac{y}{1/2} = \frac{z}{0} \Rightarrow \frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{2y}{1} = \frac{z}{0}$$

=== 8 | prob | 1 | mcq
@topic Conditional probability
If A and B are two events such that $P\left(\dfrac{A}{B}\right) = 2 \times P\left(\dfrac{B}{A}\right)$ and $P(A) + P(B) = \dfrac{2}{3}$, then $P(B)$ is equal to
(A) $\dfrac{2}{9}$
(B) $\dfrac{7}{9}$
(C) $\dfrac{4}{9}$
(D) $\dfrac{5}{9}$
--- ans A
$$\frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} = 2 \times \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)} \Rightarrow P(A) = 2P(B) \quad (P(A \cap B) \ne 0)$$
$P(A) + P(B) = \dfrac{2}{3} \Rightarrow 3P(B) = \dfrac{2}{3} \Rightarrow P(B) = \dfrac{2}{9}$.
(The solved paper prints the condition as $P(A/B) = P(A/B)$, a misprint; its own solution uses $P(A/B) = 2 \times P(B/A)$, as written here.)

=== 15 | mat | 1 | mcq
@topic Operations on matrices
If A is a $2 \times 3$ matrix such that $AB$ and $AB'$ both are defined, then order of the matrix B is
(A) $2 \times 2$
(B) $2 \times 1$
(C) $3 \times 2$
(D) $3 \times 3$
--- ans D
Let B be of order $m \times n$.
$AB$ defined $\Rightarrow$ (columns of A) $= m \Rightarrow m = 3$.
$AB'$ defined, $B'$ is $n \times m$ $\Rightarrow n = 3$.
$\therefore$ B is of order $3 \times 3$.

@topic Equation of a line
If the equation of a line is $x = ay + b$, $z = cy + d$, then find the direction ratios of the line and a point on the line.
--- ans
From $x = ay + b$: $y = \dfrac{x - b}{a}$; from $z = cy + d$: $y = \dfrac{z - d}{c}$.
So the line in symmetric (Cartesian) form is
$$\frac{x - b}{a} = \frac{y - 0}{1} = \frac{z - d}{c}$$
Comparing with $\dfrac{x - x_1}{a_1} = \dfrac{y - y_1}{b_1} = \dfrac{z - z_1}{c_1}$:
Direction ratios: $\boxed{a,\ 1,\ c}$; a point on the line: $\boxed{(b, 0, d)}$.

=== 25 | aod | 2 | sub
@topic Rate of change
If the circumference of circle is increasing at the constant rate, prove that rate of change of area is directly proportional to its radius.
--- ans
Let $r$ be the radius, $C = 2\pi r$ the circumference and $A = \pi r^2$ the area at time $t$.
Given $\dfrac{dC}{dt} = k$ (constant) $\Rightarrow 2\pi\dfrac{dr}{dt} = k \Rightarrow \dfrac{dr}{dt} = \dfrac{k}{2\pi}$.
$$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\frac{dr}{dt} = 2\pi r \cdot \frac{k}{2\pi} = kr$$
Since $k$ is constant, $\dfrac{dA}{dt} \propto r$, i.e. the rate of change of area is directly proportional to the radius. Hence proved.

subject to constraints:
$x + 2y \ge 100$,

=== 30a | int | 3 | sub
@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} |x^4 - x|\,dx$.
--- ans
$x^4 - x = x(x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$, where $x^2 + x + 1 > 0$ for all $x$.
On $[-1, 0]$: $x \le 0,\ x - 1 < 0 \Rightarrow x^4 - x \ge 0$; on $[0, 1]$: $x \ge 0,\ x - 1 \le 0 \Rightarrow x^4 - x \le 0$.
$$\therefore\ I = \int_{-1}^{0} (x^4 - x)\,dx + \int_{0}^{1} (x - x^4)\,dx$$
$$= \left[\frac{x^5}{5} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{0} + \left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_{0}^{1} = \left(0 - \left(-\frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{5}\right)$$
$$= \frac{7}{10} + \frac{3}{10} = 1$$

=== 30b | int | 3 | sub
@topic Integration by parts
Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{\sin^{-1}x}{(1 - x^2)^{3/2}}\,dx$.
--- ans
Put $\sin^{-1}x = t \Rightarrow x = \sin t$, $dx = \cos t\,dt$, and $(1 - x^2)^{3/2} = \cos^3 t$.
$$I = \int \frac{t\cos t}{\cos^3 t}\,dt = \int t\sec^2 t\,dt$$
Integrating by parts (first function $t$):
$$I = t\tan t - \int 1 \cdot \tan t\,dt = t\tan t + \log|\cos t| + C$$
Now $\tan t = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$ and $\cos t = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$:
$$\therefore\ I = \frac{x\sin^{-1}x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} + \log\sqrt{1 - x^2} + C = \frac{x\sin^{-1}x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} + \frac{1}{2}\log|1 - x^2| + C$$

=== 31 | int | 3 | sub
@topic Special forms
Find $\displaystyle\int e^x\left(\frac{1 - \sin x}{1 - \cos x}\right)dx$.
--- ans
$$\frac{1 - \sin x}{1 - \cos x} = \frac{1 - 2\sin\frac{x}{2}\cos\frac{x}{2}}{2\sin^2\frac{x}{2}} = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{cosec}^2\frac{x}{2} - \cot\frac{x}{2}$$
$$\therefore\ I = \int e^x\left(-\cot\frac{x}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{cosec}^2\frac{x}{2}\right)dx$$
This is of the form $\displaystyle\int e^x\big(f(x) + f'(x)\big)dx = e^x f(x) + C$ with $f(x) = -\cot\dfrac{x}{2}$, $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{cosec}^2\dfrac{x}{2}$.
$$\therefore\ I = -e^x\cot\frac{x}{2} + C$$

=== 33 | aoi | 5 | sub
@topic Area enclosed by lines
Using Integration, find the area of triangle whose vertices are $(-1, 1)$, $(0, 5)$ and $(3, 2)$.
--- ans
Let $A(-1, 1)$, $B(0, 5)$, $C(3, 2)$.
Side AB: $y - 1 = \dfrac{5 - 1}{0 + 1}(x + 1) \Rightarrow y = 4x + 5$
Side BC: $y - 5 = \dfrac{2 - 5}{3 - 0}(x - 0) \Rightarrow y = 5 - x$
Side AC: $y - 1 = \dfrac{2 - 1}{3 + 1}(x + 1) \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{x + 5}{4}$

```plot
{"x":[-2,4],"y":[-1,6],"lines":[{"a":4,"b":-1,"c":-5,"label":"y = 4x + 5"},{"a":1,"b":1,"c":5,"label":"x + y = 5"},{"a":1,"b":-4,"c":-5,"label":"x − 4y + 5 = 0"}],"polygons":[{"pts":[[-1,1],[0,5],[3,2]]}],"points":[{"x":-1,"y":1,"label":"A(−1, 1)"},{"x":0,"y":5,"label":"B(0, 5)"},{"x":3,"y":2,"label":"C(3, 2)"}]}
```

$$\text{Area of } \Delta ABC = \int_{-1}^{0} (4x + 5)\,dx + \int_{0}^{3} (5 - x)\,dx - \int_{-1}^{3} \frac{x + 5}{4}\,dx$$
$$= \Big[2x^2 + 5x\Big]_{-1}^{0} + \left[5x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{3} - \frac{1}{4}\left[\frac{x^2}{2} + 5x\right]_{-1}^{3}$$
$$= \big(0 - (2 - 5)\big) + \left(15 - \frac{9}{2}\right) - \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{39}{2} - \left(-\frac{9}{2}\right)\right)$$
$$= 3 + \frac{21}{2} - 6 = \frac{15}{2}$$
Required area $= \dfrac{15}{2}$ sq. units.
(Check: $\dfrac{1}{2}\left|-1(5 - 2) + 0(2 - 1) + 3(1 - 5)\right| = \dfrac{15}{2}$. The solved paper's answer 9 comes from evaluating $\int_{-1}^{0}(4x + 5)\,dx$ as $\frac{9}{2}$ instead of $3$.)
