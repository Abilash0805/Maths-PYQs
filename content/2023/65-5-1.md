@topic Counting relations and functions
Let $A = \{3, 5\}$. Then number of reflexive relations of A is:
(A) $2$
(B) $4$
(C) $0$
(D) $8$
--- ans B
For a set with $n$ elements, $A \times A$ has $n^2$ pairs; a reflexive relation must contain the $n$ diagonal pairs, and each of the other $n^2 - n$ pairs may or may not be included.
Number of reflexive relations $= 2^{n^2 - n} = 2^{n(n-1)}$. For $n = 2$: $2^{2} = 4$.

**Assertion (A):** Two coins are tossed simultaneously. The probability of getting two heads, if it is known that at least one head comes up, is $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
**Reason (R):** Let E and F be two events with a random experiment, then $P(F/E) = \dfrac{P(E \cap F)}{P(E)}$.
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and the Reason (R) is the correct explanation of the Assertion (A).
(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of the Assertion (A).

@topic Differentiability
If $f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2, & \text{if } x \ge 1 \\ x, & \text{if } x < 1 \end{cases}$, then show that $f$ is not differentiable at $x = 1$.
--- ans

@topic Rate of change
The median of an equilateral triangle is increasing at the rate of $2\sqrt{3}$ cm/s. Find the rate at which its side is increasing.
--- ans
Let the side of the equilateral triangle be $a$ cm and its median be $m$ cm at time $t$.
In an equilateral triangle the median is also the altitude, so by Pythagoras theorem
$$m^2 = a^2 - \left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{3a^2}{4} \Rightarrow m = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a$$
Differentiating w.r.t. $t$:
$$\frac{dm}{dt} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\frac{da}{dt}$$
Given $\dfrac{dm}{dt} = 2\sqrt{3}$ cm/s:
$$2\sqrt{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\frac{da}{dt} \Rightarrow \frac{da}{dt} = 4$$
$\therefore$ The side is increasing at the rate of **4 cm/s**.

@topic Homogeneous equations
An equation involving derivatives of the dependent variable with respect to the independent variables is called a differential equation. A differential equation of the form $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = F(x, y)$ is said to be homogeneous if $F(x, y)$ is a homogeneous function of degree zero, whereas a function $F(x, y)$ is a homogenous function of degree $n$ if $F(\lambda x, \lambda y) = \lambda^n F(x, y)$. To solve a homogeneous differential equation of the type $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = F(x, y) = g\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)$, we make the substitution $y = vx$ and then separate the variables.

Based on the above, answer the following questions:
(i) Show that $(x^2 - y^2)\,dx + 2xy\,dy = 0$ is a differential equation of the type $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = g\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)$. **[2]**
(ii) Solve the above equation to find its general solution. **[2]**
--- ans
**(i)** $(x^2 - y^2)\,dx + 2xy\,dy = 0 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{y^2 - x^2}{2xy}$.
Dividing numerator and denominator by $x^2$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^2 - 1}{2\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)} = g\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$$
(Equivalently, $F(x, y) = \dfrac{y^2 - x^2}{2xy}$ satisfies $F(\lambda x, \lambda y) = \lambda^0 F(x, y)$, so it is homogeneous of degree zero.)

**(ii)** Put $y = vx \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = v + x\dfrac{dv}{dx}$:
$$v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{v^2 - 1}{2v} \Rightarrow x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{v^2 - 1 - 2v^2}{2v} = -\frac{1 + v^2}{2v}$$

$$\int \frac{2v}{1 + v^2}\,dv = -\int \frac{dx}{x} \Rightarrow \log\left(1 + v^2\right) = -\log|x| + \log|C|$$
$$\Rightarrow x\left(1 + v^2\right) = C \Rightarrow x\left(1 + \frac{y^2}{x^2}\right) = C$$
$$\therefore\ x^2 + y^2 = Cx$$
is the general solution.
