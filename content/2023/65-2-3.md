@topic Scalar product and projection
If the angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = 3\sqrt{3}$, then the value of $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$ is:
(A) $9$
(B) $3$
(C) $\dfrac{1}{9}$
(D) $\dfrac{1}{3}$
--- ans B
$|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = 3\sqrt{3} \Rightarrow |\vec{a}||\vec{b}| \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 3\sqrt{3} \Rightarrow |\vec{a}||\vec{b}| = 6$.
$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\frac{\pi}{3} = 6 \times \frac{1}{2} = 3$$

@topic Position vectors and section formula
The position vector of three consecutive vertices of a parallelogram ABCD are $A(4\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 6\hat{k})$, $B(5\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k})$ and $C(12\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k})$. The position vector of D is given by
(A) $-3\hat{i} - 5\hat{j} - 10\hat{k}$
(B) $21\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}$
(C) $11\hat{i} + 9\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$
(D) $-11\hat{i} - 9\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$
--- ans C
The diagonals AC and BD bisect each other, so $\vec{a} + \vec{c} = \vec{b} + \vec{d}$:
$$\vec{d} = \vec{a} + \vec{c} - \vec{b} = (4 + 12 - 5)\hat{i} + (2 + 4 + 3)\hat{j} + (-6 + 5 - 1)\hat{k} = 11\hat{i} + 9\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$$

=== 3 | prob | 1 | mcq
@topic Conditional probability
If for two events A and B, $P(A - B) = \dfrac{1}{5}$ and $P(A) = \dfrac{3}{5}$, then $P\left(\dfrac{B}{A}\right)$ is equal to
(A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
(B) $\dfrac{3}{5}$
(C) $\dfrac{2}{5}$
(D) $\dfrac{2}{3}$
--- ans D
$P(A - B) = P(A) - P(A \cap B) \Rightarrow P(A \cap B) = \dfrac{3}{5} - \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{2}{5}$.
$$P(B|A) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)} = \frac{2/5}{3/5} = \frac{2}{3}$$

=== 4 | int | 1 | mcq
@topic Properties of definite integrals
If $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} \cos^2 x\,dx = k\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2 x\,dx$, then the value of $k$ is
(A) $4$
(B) $2$
(C) $1$
(D) $0$
--- ans A
Since $\cos^2(2\pi - x) = \cos^2 x$, $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} \cos^2 x\,dx = 2\int_0^{\pi} \cos^2 x\,dx$; and since $\cos^2(\pi - x) = \cos^2 x$, $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \cos^2 x\,dx = 2\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2 x\,dx$.
$$\therefore\ \int_0^{2\pi} \cos^2 x\,dx = 4\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2 x\,dx \Rightarrow k = 4$$

@topic Symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices
Number of symmetric matrices of order $3 \times 3$ with each entry 1 or $-1$ is
(A) $512$
(B) $64$
(C) $8$
(D) $4$
--- ans B
A $3 \times 3$ symmetric matrix is fixed by its 3 diagonal entries and the 3 entries above the diagonal, i.e. 6 entries.
Each can be chosen in 2 ways, so the number of such matrices $= 2^6 = 64$.

@topic Equation of a line
Equation of a line passing through point $(1, 2, 3)$ and equally inclined to the coordinate axis, is
(A) $\dfrac{x}{1} = \dfrac{y}{2} = \dfrac{z}{3}$
(B) $\dfrac{x}{1} = \dfrac{y}{2} = \dfrac{z}{3}$
(C) $\dfrac{x - 1}{1} = \dfrac{y - 1}{2} = \dfrac{z - 1}{3}$
(D) $\dfrac{x - 1}{1} = \dfrac{y - 2}{1} = \dfrac{z - 3}{1}$
--- ans D
A line equally inclined to the coordinate axes has direction cosines $l = m = n$, so its direction ratios are $1, 1, 1$.
Through $(1, 2, 3)$:
$$\frac{x - 1}{1} = \frac{y - 2}{1} = \frac{z - 3}{1}$$
(Options (A) and (B) are printed identically in the source.)

=== 21 | vec | 2 | sub
@topic Basic concepts of vectors
If points A, B and C have position vectors $2\hat{i}$, $\hat{j}$ and $2\hat{k}$ respectively, then show that $\Delta ABC$ is an isosceles triangle.
--- ans
$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \hat{j} - 2\hat{i} = -2\hat{i} + \hat{j} \Rightarrow |\overrightarrow{AB}| = \sqrt{4 + 1} = \sqrt{5}$$
$$\overrightarrow{BC} = 2\hat{k} - \hat{j} \Rightarrow |\overrightarrow{BC}| = \sqrt{1 + 4} = \sqrt{5}$$
$$\overrightarrow{AC} = 2\hat{k} - 2\hat{i} \Rightarrow |\overrightarrow{AC}| = \sqrt{4 + 4} = 2\sqrt{2}$$
Since $AB = BC = \sqrt{5}$ (and A, B, C are not collinear, as $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ are not parallel), $\Delta ABC$ is an isosceles triangle.

=== 23 | aod | 2 | sub
@topic Rate of change
If equal sides of an isosceles triangle with fixed base 10 cm are increasing at the rate of 4 cm/s, how fast is the area of triangle increasing at an instant when all sides become equal ?
--- ans
Let each equal side be $x$ cm. The altitude to the base 10 cm bisects it, so the altitude $= \sqrt{x^2 - 25}$.
$$A = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times \sqrt{x^2 - 25} = 5\sqrt{x^2 - 25}$$
$$\frac{dA}{dt} = 5 \cdot \frac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2 - 25}}\cdot\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{5x}{\sqrt{x^2 - 25}}\cdot\frac{dx}{dt}$$
When all sides are equal, $x = 10$; and $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 4$ cm/s:
$$\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{5 \times 10}{\sqrt{100 - 25}} \times 4 = \frac{200}{5\sqrt{3}} = \frac{40}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{40\sqrt{3}}{3}\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s}$$
The area is increasing at $\dfrac{40}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 23.09$ cm²/s. (The solved paper's $40$ cm²/s uses $\sqrt{25}$ in place of $\sqrt{75}$.)

@topic Special forms
Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{x + 2}{\sqrt{x^2 - 4x - 5}}\,dx$
--- ans
Write $x + 2 = \dfrac{1}{2}(2x - 4) + 4$, where $2x - 4 = \dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2 - 4x - 5)$.
$$I = \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{2x - 4}{\sqrt{x^2 - 4x - 5}}\,dx + 4\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x - 2)^2 - 3^2}}$$
For the first integral put $x^2 - 4x - 5 = u$: $\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u}} = \sqrt{u}$.
For the second use $\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}} = \log\left|x + \sqrt{x^2 - a^2}\right|$.
$$\therefore\ I = \sqrt{x^2 - 4x - 5} + 4\log\left|x - 2 + \sqrt{x^2 - 4x - 5}\right| + C$$

=== 27b | int | 3 | sub
@topic Properties of definite integrals
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx$, where $f(x) = \dfrac{9^x}{1 + 9^x}$
--- ans
$$I = \int_{-a}^{a} \frac{9^x}{1 + 9^x}\,dx \quad\cdots(1)$$
Using $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = \int_{-a}^{a} f(-x)\,dx$:
$$I = \int_{-a}^{a} \frac{9^{-x}}{1 + 9^{-x}}\,dx = \int_{-a}^{a} \frac{1}{9^x + 1}\,dx \quad\cdots(2)$$
Adding (1) and (2):
$$2I = \int_{-a}^{a} \frac{9^x + 1}{1 + 9^x}\,dx = \int_{-a}^{a} 1\,dx = 2a$$
$$\therefore\ I = a$$

=== 31a | prob | 3 | sub
@topic Random variables and probability distributions
Two numbers are selected from first six even natural numbers at random without replacement. If X denotes the greater of two numbers selected, find the probability distribution of X.
--- ans
The first six even natural numbers are $2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12$. Two of them can be selected in ${}^6C_2 = 15$ equally likely ways.
X (the greater number) can take the values $4, 6, 8, 10, 12$.
$X = 4$: $\{2, 4\}$ — 1 way $\Rightarrow P(X = 4) = \dfrac{1}{15}$
$X = 6$: $\{2, 6\}, \{4, 6\}$ — 2 ways $\Rightarrow P(X = 6) = \dfrac{2}{15}$
$X = 8$: $\{2, 8\}, \{4, 8\}, \{6, 8\}$ — 3 ways $\Rightarrow P(X = 8) = \dfrac{3}{15} = \dfrac{1}{5}$
$X = 10$: 4 ways $\Rightarrow P(X = 10) = \dfrac{4}{15}$
$X = 12$: 5 ways $\Rightarrow P(X = 12) = \dfrac{5}{15} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

| $X$ | $4$ | $6$ | $8$ | $10$ | $12$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $P(X)$ | $\frac{1}{15}$ | $\frac{2}{15}$ | $\frac{1}{5}$ | $\frac{4}{15}$ | $\frac{1}{3}$ |

(Check: $\frac{1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5}{15} = 1$.)

=== 31b | prob | 3 | sub
@topic Multiplication theorem and independent events
A fair coin and an unbiased die are tossed. Let A be the event, "Head appears on the coin" and B be the event, "3 comes on the die". Find whether A and B are independent events or not.
--- ans
Sample space: $S = \{(H, 1), \ldots, (H, 6), (T, 1), \ldots, (T, 6)\}$, $n(S) = 12$, all outcomes equally likely.
$A = \{(H, 1), (H, 2), \ldots, (H, 6)\} \Rightarrow P(A) = \dfrac{6}{12} = \dfrac{1}{2}$
$B = \{(H, 3), (T, 3)\} \Rightarrow P(B) = \dfrac{2}{12} = \dfrac{1}{6}$
$A \cap B = \{(H, 3)\} \Rightarrow P(A \cap B) = \dfrac{1}{12}$
$$P(A)\,P(B) = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12} = P(A \cap B)$$
$\therefore$ A and B are **independent** events.

@topic Area bounded by a curve and a line
Find the area of the smaller region bounded by the curves $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$ and $\dfrac{x}{5} + \dfrac{y}{4} = 1$, using integration.
--- ans
The ellipse meets the axes at $(\pm 5, 0)$, $(0, \pm 4)$; the line $\dfrac{x}{5} + \dfrac{y}{4} = 1$ meets the ellipse at $A(5, 0)$ and $B(0, 4)$.
The smaller region lies in the first quadrant between the arc AB of the ellipse (above) and the chord AB (below), for $0 \le x \le 5$.

```plot
{"x":[-6,6],"y":[-5,5],"ellipses":[{"cx":0,"cy":0,"rx":5,"ry":4,"label":"x²/25 + y²/16 = 1"}],"lines":[{"a":4,"b":5,"c":20,"label":"x/5 + y/4 = 1"}],"areas":[{"upper":"4/5*sqrt(25-x*x)","lower":"4-4*x/5","from":0,"to":5}],"points":[{"x":5,"y":0,"label":"A(5, 0)"},{"x":0,"y":4,"label":"B(0, 4)"}]}
```

Ellipse (upper half): $y = \dfrac{4}{5}\sqrt{25 - x^2}$; line: $y = 4\left(1 - \dfrac{x}{5}\right) = 4 - \dfrac{4x}{5}$.
$$\text{Area} = \int_0^5 \frac{4}{5}\sqrt{25 - x^2}\,dx - \int_0^5 \left(4 - \frac{4x}{5}\right)dx$$
$$= \frac{4}{5}\left[\frac{x}{2}\sqrt{25 - x^2} + \frac{25}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{5}\right]_0^5 - \left[4x - \frac{2x^2}{5}\right]_0^5$$
$$= \frac{4}{5}\left(0 + \frac{25}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - (20 - 10) = 5\pi - 10$$
Required area $= (5\pi - 10)$ sq. units.
