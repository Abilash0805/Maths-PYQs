@topic Types of relations
Let R be a relation in the set N given by $R = \{(a, b) : a = b - 2,\ b > 6\}$. Then
(A) $(8, 7) \in R$
(B) $(6, 8) \in R$
(C) $(3, 8) \in R$
(D) $(2, 4) \in R$
--- ans B
$(a, b) \in R$ requires $a = b - 2$ and $b > 6$.
(A) $8 \ne 7 - 2$; (C) $3 \ne 8 - 2$; (D) $b = 4 \not> 6$.
(B) $6 = 8 - 2$ and $8 > 6$, so $(6, 8) \in R$.

(B) $x = y$

@topic Increasing and decreasing functions
The function $f(x) = x^3 + 3x$ is increasing in interval
(A) $(-\infty, 0)$
(B) $(0, \infty)$
(C) $\mathbb{R}$
(D) $(0, 1)$
--- ans C
$f'(x) = 3x^2 + 3 = 3(x^2 + 1) > 0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ (since $x^2 \ge 0$).
Hence $f$ is (strictly) increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.

@topic Scalar product and projection
If $\vec{a}\cdot\hat{i} = \vec{a}\cdot\left(\hat{i} + \hat{j}\right) = \vec{a}\cdot\left(\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}\right) = 1$, then $\vec{a}$ is:
(A) $\hat{k}$
(B) $\hat{i}$
(C) $\hat{j}$
(D) $\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$
--- ans B
Let $\vec{a} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$. Then $\vec{a}\cdot\hat{i} = x$, $\vec{a}\cdot(\hat{i} + \hat{j}) = x + y$, $\vec{a}\cdot(\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}) = x + y + z$.
$x = x + y = x + y + z = 1 \Rightarrow x = 1,\ y = 0,\ z = 0$.
$\therefore \vec{a} = \hat{i}$.

=== 21a | cd | 2 | sub
@topic Continuity
Find the value of $k$ for which the function $f$ given as
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1 - \cos x}{2x^2}, & \text{if } x \ne 0 \\[2mm] k, & \text{if } x = 0 \end{cases}$$
is continuous at $x = 0$.
--- ans
$f$ is continuous at $x = 0$ if $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = f(0) = k$.
Using $1 - \cos x = 2\sin^2\dfrac{x}{2}$:
$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{2x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2\sin^2\frac{x}{2}}{2x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\sin\frac{x}{2}}{\frac{x}{2}}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}\times 1 = \frac{1}{4}$$
$$\therefore\ k = \frac{1}{4}$$

=== 21b | cd | 2 | sub
@topic Parametric differentiation
If $x = a\cos t$ and $y = b\sin t$, then find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$.
--- ans
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = -a\sin t,\qquad \frac{dy}{dt} = b\cos t$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{b\cos t}{-a\sin t} = -\frac{b}{a}\cot t$$
Differentiating w.r.t. $x$:
$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -\frac{b}{a}\left(-\operatorname{cosec}^2 t\right)\frac{dt}{dx} = \frac{b}{a}\operatorname{cosec}^2 t \times \frac{1}{-a\sin t}$$
$$\therefore\ \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -\frac{b}{a^2}\operatorname{cosec}^3 t$$

=== 26 | det | 3 | sub
@topic Evaluation of determinants
Show that the determinant $\begin{vmatrix} x & \sin\theta & \cos\theta \\ -\sin\theta & -x & 1 \\ \cos\theta & 1 & x \end{vmatrix}$ is independent of $\theta$.
--- ans
Let $\Delta$ be the given determinant. Expanding along $R_1$:
$$\Delta = x\begin{vmatrix} -x & 1 \\ 1 & x \end{vmatrix} - \sin\theta\begin{vmatrix} -\sin\theta & 1 \\ \cos\theta & x \end{vmatrix} + \cos\theta\begin{vmatrix} -\sin\theta & -x \\ \cos\theta & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$
$$= x\left(-x^2 - 1\right) - \sin\theta\left(-x\sin\theta - \cos\theta\right) + \cos\theta\left(-\sin\theta + x\cos\theta\right)$$
$$= -x^3 - x + x\sin^2\theta + \sin\theta\cos\theta - \sin\theta\cos\theta + x\cos^2\theta$$
$$= -x^3 - x + x\left(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta\right) = -x^3 - x + x = -x^3$$
Since $\Delta = -x^3$ does not contain $\theta$, the determinant is independent of $\theta$. Hence proved.

=== 28a | 3d | 3 | sub
@topic Foot of perpendicular and image
Find the coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular drawn from point $(5, 7, 3)$ to the line $\dfrac{x - 15}{3} = \dfrac{y - 29}{8} = \dfrac{z - 5}{-5}$.
--- ans
Let $P(5, 7, 3)$. Any point on the line is $Q(3\lambda + 15,\ 8\lambda + 29,\ -5\lambda + 5)$.
Direction ratios of $PQ$: $3\lambda + 10,\ 8\lambda + 22,\ -5\lambda + 2$.
$Q$ is the foot of the perpendicular when $PQ$ is perpendicular to the line (d.r.'s $3, 8, -5$):
$$3(3\lambda + 10) + 8(8\lambda + 22) - 5(-5\lambda + 2) = 0$$
$$\Rightarrow 9\lambda + 30 + 64\lambda + 176 + 25\lambda - 10 = 0 \Rightarrow 98\lambda + 196 = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda = -2$$
$\therefore$ Foot of the perpendicular is $(-6 + 15,\ -16 + 29,\ 10 + 5) = \boxed{(9, 13, 15)}$.

@topic Vector product and area
If $\vec{a} = \hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$ and $\vec{b} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$, then find a unit vector perpendicular to both $\vec{a} + \vec{b}$ and $\vec{a} - \vec{b}$.
--- ans
$\vec{a} + \vec{b} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$, $\quad\vec{a} - \vec{b} = -\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$.
A vector perpendicular to both is their cross product:
$$\left(\vec{a} + \vec{b}\right) \times \left(\vec{a} - \vec{b}\right) = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & -1 & -2 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{i}(-6 + 4) - \hat{j}(-4 - 0) + \hat{k}(-2 - 0) = -2\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$$
$$\left|-2\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}\right| = \sqrt{4 + 16 + 4} = \sqrt{24} = 2\sqrt{6}$$
$$\therefore\ \text{Required unit vector} = \pm\frac{-2\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} - 2\hat{k}}{2\sqrt{6}} = \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(-\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - \hat{k}\right)$$

@topic Graphical method – unbounded region
Solve the following Linear Programming Problem graphically:
Minimise: $Z = 60x + 80y$
Subject to constraints:
$3x + 4y \ge 8$
$5x + 2y \ge 11$
$x, y \ge 0$
--- ans
Draw the lines $3x + 4y = 8$ (through $(0, 2)$, $\left(\frac{8}{3}, 0\right)$) and $5x + 2y = 11$ (through $\left(0, \frac{11}{2}\right)$, $\left(\frac{11}{5}, 0\right)$).
$(0, 0)$ satisfies neither inequality, so the feasible region lies on the side of each line away from the origin, in the first quadrant.
Intersection: $5x + 2y = 11 \Rightarrow 10x + 4y = 22$; subtracting $3x + 4y = 8$ gives $7x = 14 \Rightarrow x = 2,\ y = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

```plot
{"x":[-0.5,5],"y":[-0.5,7],"lines":[{"a":3,"b":4,"c":8,"label":"3x + 4y = 8"},{"a":5,"b":2,"c":11,"label":"5x + 2y = 11"}],"polygons":[{"pts":[[0,5.5],[2,0.5],[2.6667,0],[5,0],[5,7],[0,7]],"label":"Feasible region (unbounded)"}],"points":[{"x":0,"y":5.5,"label":"A(0, 11/2)"},{"x":2,"y":0.5,"label":"B(2, 1/2)"},{"x":2.6667,"y":0,"label":"C(8/3, 0)"}]}
```

The feasible region is **unbounded**, with corner points:

| Corner point | $Z = 60x + 80y$ |
|---|---|
| $A\left(0, \frac{11}{2}\right)$ | $440$ |
| $B\left(2, \frac{1}{2}\right)$ | $120 + 40 = 160$ ← minimum |
| $C\left(\frac{8}{3}, 0\right)$ | $160$ ← minimum |

Since the region is unbounded, check the open half-plane $60x + 80y < 160$, i.e. $3x + 4y < 8$. It has no point in common with the feasible region (which satisfies $3x + 4y \ge 8$).
$\therefore$ Minimum $Z = 160$, attained at $B\left(2, \frac{1}{2}\right)$, at $C\left(\frac{8}{3}, 0\right)$ and at every point of the segment BC.
